# Thompson -- The IBD Remission Diet: Elemental Diet Protocol

Jini Patel Thompson is a Crohn's patient who achieved remission through elemental diet therapy and formulated a commercial elemental shake product (Absorb Plus). The IBD Remission Diet (2nd edition, 2013) presents a comprehensive protocol combining exclusive elemental nutrition, antimicrobial herbal therapy (Wild Oregano Oil), high-dose probiotics, and a systematic four-phase food reintroduction protocol. Unlike Gottschall (SCD) which is a long-term dietary pattern, Thompson's protocol is an intensive 2-12 week medicalized intervention designed to induce remission, followed by structured food reintroduction.

## Ch1: The Story & The Vision

### Claim 1: Elemental diet therapy is as effective as corticosteroids for inducing remission in pediatric Crohn's, with superior mucosal healing

**Author's claim:** "Elemental diet therapy is as effective as steroids in inducing remission, whilst avoiding steroid side effects." Thompson cites two clinical trials: an English study (44 children, 90% remission in average 6 weeks) and an Italian study (37 children on elemental diet vs 10 on steroids: 86% elemental remission in 2.5 weeks vs 90% steroid remission in 3.7 weeks, but 7 elemental diet children showed complete mucosal healing vs 0 on steroids).

**Evidence presented:** Two pediatric trials from England and Italy. Thompson quotes Dr. Robert Canani: "In children with active Crohn's disease, exclusive nutritional therapy shows a more rapid effect than steroids in inducing clinical remission and is markedly more effective than steroids in producing healing of mucosal inflammation."

**Confidence:** HIGH. The efficacy of exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN) for pediatric Crohn's is one of the best-established findings in IBD dietary therapy. Multiple meta-analyses confirm EEN equivalence to corticosteroids for remission induction with superior mucosal healing. This is NOT controversial in pediatric gastroenterology.

**What's at stake:** If elemental diet is as effective as steroids with better mucosal healing and fewer side effects, it should be first-line therapy for pediatric Crohn's. The fact that it's not widely used in adults (due to palatability and compliance issues) is a delivery problem, not an efficacy problem.

**Who disagrees:** No one disputes the pediatric data. The controversy is: (1) whether EEN is equally effective in adults (compliance is much worse), (2) whether polymeric formulas (whole protein) work as well as elemental (amino acid) formulas (trials suggest they do, undermining the "elemental" rationale), and (3) what the mechanism actually is (bowel rest? microbiome modulation? elimination of dietary antigens?).

**My assessment:** This is the strongest evidence base for ANY dietary therapy in IBD. Thompson correctly identifies EEN as a validated intervention. Her framing of Absorb Plus within this evidence is reasonable but the product itself has not been directly tested in trials -- the evidence is for EEN as a class, not for her specific formulation.

### Claim 2: Semi-elemental formulas (whey isolate peptides) are clinically equivalent or superior to strictly elemental formulas (free-form amino acids)

**Author's claim:** Citing a controlled trial: amino acid feed = 69% remission in 3 weeks; whole protein feed = 72% remission. When groups were switched, 43% of those switched to amino acids relapsed, but NONE switched to whole protein relapsed. Thompson argues Absorb Plus (whey isolate-based) is at least as effective as free-form amino acid formulas and better for weight gain.

**Evidence presented:** One controlled trial. Additional reference to Silk et al. (1985) on protein digestion showing di-peptide bonds are needed for muscle synthesis.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH for polymeric formulas being as effective as elemental. Multiple trials and meta-analyses support this. LOW for Thompson's specific product claims -- Absorb Plus has not been tested in independent trials.

**What's at stake:** If polymeric/semi-elemental formulas work as well as elemental, the cost barrier drops dramatically (elemental formulas cost ~400% more). This makes EEN more accessible. But it also undermines the "predigested" mechanistic rationale -- if whole protein works, the mechanism isn't about predigestion.

**My assessment:** Thompson's argument is supported by the literature but overstates the case for her specific product. The broader point -- that expensive elemental formulas aren't necessary and that peptide-based formulas work -- is valid and important for accessibility.

## Ch2: The Elemental Diet Protocol

### Claim 3: The elemental diet works through three mechanisms: bowel rest, bacterial starvation, and mucosal healing supplementation

**Author's claim:** An elemental diet "provides the colon with the rest necessary to heal its wounds unhindered." Because nutrients are pre-digested, "there is very little undigested matter passing into the colon" -- providing bowel rest. The bacterial population "has less to eat" making it "easier to eliminate undesirable micro-organisms." Combined with antimicrobials and probiotics, the protocol "eliminates a lot of the bad bacteria in your gut."

**Evidence presented:** Clinical reasoning and Thompson's personal experience. The bowel rest concept is standard surgical rationale. The bacterial starvation concept echoes Gottschall's SCD mechanism.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. Bowel rest is a valid physiological concept (used in NPO/bowel rest protocols). Bacterial substrate reduction is plausible. But the relative contribution of each mechanism is unknown. EEN also induces rapid microbiome changes (within days) that may be as important as substrate reduction.

**What's at stake:** If bowel rest is the primary mechanism, any elemental/polymeric formula should work and food reintroduction timing matters. If bacterial starvation is primary, the protocol's antimicrobial component (Wild Oregano Oil) is essential, not adjunctive.

**Alternative reading:** EEN may work primarily by eliminating dietary emulsifiers, food additives, and antigens that drive inflammation, rather than by bowel rest or bacterial starvation. The exclusion of ALL normal food may be the active ingredient.

**My assessment:** Thompson's mechanistic model is coherent but unproven. The three-mechanism framing (rest + starve + supplement) is rhetorically effective but each component's contribution is unknown. The protocol's strength is that it works empirically; the mechanism is secondary.

### Claim 4: Elemental diet duration should be guided by disease severity: 6 weeks for severe disease (bleeding, fistulas, surgery risk), 3-4 weeks for moderate symptoms, 2 weeks for food allergy testing

**Author's claim:** Duration guidelines: moderate-to-severe bleeding or fistulas = 6+ weeks (up to 3 months for fistulas); sporadic bleeding = 4-6 weeks; minor symptoms (bloating, gas, pain, diarrhea) = 3-4 weeks; food allergy testing = 10-14 days. "Stay on it for at least one to two more weeks after ALL symptoms have cleared." Thompson's gastroenterologist advised: 3 or 6 weeks as standard; if 3 weeks fails, full 6 weeks required (9 weeks total).

**Evidence presented:** Clinical experience and Thompson's gastroenterologist's protocol. No controlled data on optimal EEN duration. The 6-week standard maps to corticosteroid tapering timelines in IBD.

**Confidence:** LOW. These durations are clinical heuristics, not trial-validated endpoints. The pediatric trials cited in Ch1 used durations of 2-12 weeks (individualized). The "stay 1-2 weeks after symptoms clear" rule is sensible but arbitrary.

**What's at stake:** Longer elemental diet = higher cost, greater compliance burden, more social disruption. If 3 weeks works as well as 6 for moderate disease, the shorter protocol would dramatically improve accessibility.

**My assessment:** The duration guidelines are reasonable clinical defaults but entirely unvalidated. Thompson's emphasis on intuition ("ask your body") alongside clinical guidelines is both wise (acknowledging individual variation) and problematic (substituting feelings for evidence).

### Claim 5: Rest is essential during the elemental diet -- patients should take time off work and minimize activity

**Author's claim:** "You really need to take time off work/school during this period and treat the process like a healing vacation... The IBD Remission Diet is an intensive healing program and it takes a lot of energy for your body to heal itself... you have to rest and conserve your energy for healing work -- not other work."

For moderate-to-severe bleeding: "you have to spend most of your day horizontal until the blood clots... you cannot be bent at your waist, your waist/torso needs to be horizontally in line with your hips."

**Evidence presented:** Anecdotal: a patient who worked full-time while on the diet showed minimal improvement. Thompson attributes this to energy diversion from healing to work.

**Confidence:** LOW. No evidence that bed rest or time off work improves EEN outcomes. This is a reasonable inference from the energy demands of healing but is untested. The horizontal positioning for bleeding has some physiological basis (reduced intra-abdominal pressure) but is not standard medical advice.

**What's at stake:** Recommending 3-6 weeks off work makes the protocol inaccessible to many patients. If rest is not actually essential, this recommendation creates an unnecessary barrier. If rest is essential, the protocol's real-world applicability is severely limited.

**My assessment:** Thompson's rest recommendation reflects maximalist healing philosophy, not evidence. It may help some patients and exclude others. A more evidence-based approach would be to recommend reduced activity without requiring complete work cessation. The horizontal positioning advice for bleeding is potentially dangerous if it delays seeking emergency care for hemorrhage.

## Ch5: Food Re-Introduction & Allergy Testing

### Claim 6: A systematic four-phase food reintroduction protocol, testing one food at a time, is the most reliable method for identifying food triggers

**Author's claim:** After the elemental diet, reintroduce foods in four progressive phases: Phase 1 = easiest-to-digest foods (cooked, pureed vegetables, bone broth, well-cooked fruits); Phase 4 = common allergens (wheat, dairy, corn, potatoes). Test one new food per day (or one every 3 days for rigorous testing). At the end of each week, stay on just those 7 foods for another week to confirm.

Thompson cites a clinical trial: 33 Crohn's patients on TPN, then food reintroduction. Wheat triggered symptoms in 69%, dairy in 48%, yeast in 31%, corn in 24%, potato in 17%.

**Evidence presented:** One clinical trial on food reintroduction triggers. Thompson's personal experience and reader feedback. The phase structure is based on general digestibility principles.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM for the general approach. Systematic food reintroduction after an exclusion period is standard allergy testing methodology. LOW for the specific phase structure -- no validation that Phase 1 foods are universally better tolerated than Phase 2 foods.

**What's at stake:** The reintroduction protocol is arguably as important as the elemental diet itself. A successful elemental diet followed by careless reintroduction produces immediate relapse. The protocol is labor-intensive but the logic is sound: after clearing symptoms and healing mucosa, systematically identify which foods can be tolerated.

**Who disagrees:** SCD advocates would argue that many Phase 1 foods (if they include grains or starches) should NOT be reintroduced at all, since they fuel the vicious cycle. Thompson's reintroduction is based on individual tolerance testing; SCD is based on a priori carbohydrate classification.

**Alternative reading:** The food trigger hierarchy (wheat > dairy > yeast > corn > potato) from the TPN study may reflect general food intolerance patterns in the population, not IBD-specific triggers. The fact that 69% reacted to wheat doesn't necessarily mean wheat causes IBD -- it may mean wheat challenges a still-healing gut.

**My assessment:** The reintroduction protocol is Thompson's most valuable contribution. It's more structured than Gottschall's "try one food at a time after a year" and more systematic than Foote's "eliminate one food at a time." The four-phase structure provides a roadmap. The weakness is the complete absence of validation -- we don't know if Phase 1 truly represents the most tolerable foods for the average IBD patient post-EEN.

### Claim 7: The Coca Pulse Test can detect food allergies by monitoring heart rate after food ingestion

**Author's claim:** Dr. Arthur Coca's pulse test (1950s): take pulse before eating, then 3 times at 30-minute intervals after eating a single food. If pulse increases beyond normal range (typically >16 BPM variation), food allergy is suspected. Thompson includes the full procedure and claims the test detected triggers for conditions including colitis, GI bleeding, abdominal pain, and many others.

**Evidence presented:** Coca's clinical observations from the 1950s. No modern validation. Thompson acknowledges limitations: not effective for hay fever, asthma, eczema, or contact dermatitis; invalidated by caffeine, smoking, or beta-blockers.

**Confidence:** VERY LOW. The Coca Pulse Test has no modern validation. Heart rate variability after eating is influenced by meal size, macronutrient composition, and autonomic responses unrelated to allergy. The test was developed before modern IgE/IgG testing and has been superseded.

**What's at stake:** If patients rely on pulse testing instead of systematic symptom tracking, they may misclassify foods. False positives (pulse increase from non-allergic causes) could lead to unnecessary restriction. False negatives could lead to continued consumption of trigger foods.

**Who disagrees:** The entire field of allergy/immunology. Food allergy diagnosis now relies on oral food challenge (gold standard), skin prick testing, and serum IgE testing. Pulse testing is not recognized by any allergy society.

**My assessment:** Including the Coca Pulse Test undermines Thompson's credibility. The rest of the book is grounded in real clinical trial evidence and sensible clinical reasoning. The pulse test is pseudoscientific and should not be presented alongside validated methods without strong caveats about its lack of evidence.

## Protocol Summary

### The IBD Remission Diet Protocol

1. **Elemental Phase (2-12 weeks):** Exclusive Absorb Plus shakes (or equivalent semi-elemental formula) providing 100% of nutrition. Calories: 2000-4400/day depending on weight goals. Shakes consumed every 1.5-2 hours.

2. **Supplementation (concurrent):** Jini's Wild Oregano Oil Protocol (antimicrobial), high-dose probiotics (450+ billion CFU/day recommended), L-glutamine, vitamins/minerals. All supplements emptied into shakes.

3. **Food Reintroduction (variable duration):** Four-phase structured reintroduction. Phase 1: easiest-to-digest foods (cooked vegetables, broths, well-cooked fruits). Continue shakes as calorie base, gradually reducing as solid food increases. Test 1 food per 1-3 days.

4. **Maintenance:** Continued probiotic supplementation, identified safe foods as dietary foundation, gradual expansion of food variety as health improves.

## Cross-Cutting Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| EEN = steroid efficacy + superior mucosal healing | HIGH | Pediatric data; adult compliance limits applicability |
| Semi-elemental = elemental efficacy | MEDIUM-HIGH | Polymeric formula trials support broader claim |
| Three-mechanism model (rest/starve/heal) | MEDIUM | Mechanism unknown; microbiome modulation may be key |
| Duration guidelines | LOW | Arbitrary; no duration trials |
| Rest requirement | LOW | Untested; creates access barrier |
| Four-phase reintroduction | MEDIUM (approach) / LOW (specific phases) | No phase validation |
| Coca Pulse Test | VERY LOW | Pseudoscientific; no modern validation |

**Strongest section:** The clinical trial evidence for EEN efficacy in Ch1 -- this is real, robust data that validates the entire approach.

**Weakest section:** The Coca Pulse Test -- inclusion of pseudoscientific allergy testing damages credibility of an otherwise evidence-aware book.

**Cross-reference note:** Thompson's elemental diet represents the most intensive dietary intervention in the IBD spectrum -- more restrictive than SCD, more medicalized than any other dietary approach, and with the strongest clinical trial evidence. The irony is that the protocol with the strongest evidence is also the least accessible (cost, compliance, social disruption). The gap between what works in trials (EEN) and what's sustainable (SCD, anti-inflammatory eating) is the central tension in IBD dietary therapy.
