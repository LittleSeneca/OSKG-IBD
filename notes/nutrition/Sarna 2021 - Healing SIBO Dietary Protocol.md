# Sarna -- Healing SIBO: Dietary Intervention and the SIBO Specific Food Guide

Shivan Sarna is a SIBO patient, patient advocate, and founder of the SIBO SOS Summit series. This 2021 book presents a patient-facing protocol for SIBO management, with dietary intervention centered on Dr. Allison Siebecker's SIBO Specific Food Guide (SSFG). Unlike Gottschall (SCD purist) or Pimentel (LFE/Cedars-Sinai diet), Sarna presents the SSFG as a pragmatic hybrid of SCD and low-FODMAP, with flexibility for vegetarians and an emphasis on sustainability over purity.

## SIBO Dietary Approaches Compared (from Ch4 text)

Sarna provides capsule summaries of five SIBO dietary approaches:

### Low FODMAP Diet (Monash University)
- Created for IBS/IBD, adopted for SIBO
- Restriction phase followed by structured reintroduction
- Risk: long-term strict low-FODMAP "can actually disrupt the number and diversity of good bacteria in your microbiome"
- Key insight: "it's important to not get stuck in the initial restrictive phase"

### Cedars-Sinai Low Fermentation Diet / LFE (Pimentel)
- "Low fiber and emphasizes the importance of fasting between meals so that the MMC can function properly"
- White rice, white bread, cookies generally allowed
- Vegetables less restricted than low-FODMAP
- Lactose-free dairy allowed
- "If you're feeling overwhelmed... this diet is a great way to start because it is easy to follow (even when eating out)"

### Specific Carbohydrate Diet / SCD (Gottschall)
- "Grain-free, low-sugar, low-lactose diet originally created for the management of celiac disease"
- "Because it focuses on foods that are easy to digest but doesn't consider FODMAPs"
- "Generally recommended for people with more moderate cases of SIBO who can tolerate a wider variety of vegetables"

### GAPS Diet (Campbell-McBride)
- Variation of SCD designed for autistic patients
- "Works very well for people with mood or brain disorders who also have GI disorders"
- "Not a SIBO diet" -- may need modification to eliminate high FODMAP foods
- "Emphasizes animal products, so it is hard to follow if you are vegetarian"

### SIBO Bi-Phasic Diet (Siebecker/Jacobi)
- SSFG broken into two phases by Dr. Nirala Jacobi
- Phase 1: symptom reduction
- Phase 2: concurrent with SIBO treatment (herbal antibiotics)

## SSFG Implementation Principles (from Ch4-5)

1. **Start with green foods, cooked/pureed.** For at least the first week, cook, peel, deseed, and puree all vegetables and fruits.

2. **Limit yellow foods to one per meal.** With 4-5 hour meal spacing.

3. **Portion size determines fermentability.** Large quantities of green foods can become problematic; small quantities of red foods may be tolerated.

4. **No food is permanently forbidden.** Tolerance changes over time. Retry previously problematic foods periodically.

5. **Delay introduction of challenging foods.** Wait until feeling better before trying celery root, rutabagas, cruciferous vegetables, beans, seeds, nuts, coffee, alcohol, raw vegetables, salads.

6. **Keep a food diary.** Track what you ate, quantity, timing, and symptoms.

7. **Meal spacing is non-negotiable.** 4-5 hours between meals to allow MMC function.

|-----------|----------------------|------------------|--------------------|--------------------|

## Cross-Cutting Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| All SIBO diets work through fermentable carb restriction | HIGH | Shared mechanism is clear; secondary mechanisms may differ |
| SSFG color-coded tier system | LOW (validation) / MEDIUM-HIGH (flexibility) | No trials comparing SSFG to other diets |
| 3-week structured meal plan | LOW | Arbitrary timeframe; personal tolerances may differ |
| Meal spacing supports MMC | HIGH (mechanism) / LOW (optimal interval) | Mechanism established; timing not validated |
| Balance restriction with sustainability | MEDIUM-HIGH | Harm reduction principle; threshold unknown |

**Strongest section:** The unifying claim that all SIBO diets work through fermentable carbohydrate restriction. This is the conceptual key that unlocks the dietary landscape.

**Weakest section:** The 21-day meal plan. It's too personalized (Sarna's vegetarian tolerances) to serve as a general protocol, and the inclusion of potatoes and white rice as staples contradicts the SSFG's own color-coding.

**Cross-reference note:** Sarna's SSFG represents the pragmatic middle ground in SIBO dietary therapy. It's more flexible than SCD, more targeted than general anti-inflammatory eating, and more accessible than elemental diet. Its primary weakness is the complete absence of clinical trial validation -- it's a clinical tool refined through practice, not tested through research. The SSFG's value in the OSKG is as the synthesis point where SCD and low-FODMAP meet: it acknowledges the SCD's clinical lineage while incorporating low-FODMAP's dose-dependent fermentability model.
