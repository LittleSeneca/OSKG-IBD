# LaPine 2021 — SIBO Made Simple: SCD + Low-FODMAP Integration for the Home Cook

Phoebe LaPine is a food writer, recipe developer, and SIBO patient who hosts the "SIBO Made Simple" podcast. Her book is primarily a cookbook: 90+ recipes tagged with dietary restriction compatibility (LF, SCD, SSFG, Bi-phasic Diet phases, Paleo). The introductory chapters provide patient education on SIBO and dietary management. The book's unique contribution to the graph is its practical integration of SCD and low-FODMAP for the home cook — something neither Pimentel (LFE), Gottschall (SCD), nor Sarna (SSFG) provides at this level of recipe detail.

## Key Contributions

### 1. Multi-Diet Recipe Tagging

LaPine's recipes are tagged with compatibility across five SIBO dietary protocols: Low-FODMAP, SCD, SSFG, Bi-phasic Diet (both phases), and Paleo. This is the only SIBO cookbook that systematically cross-references recipes across multiple dietary frameworks. The tagging system acknowledges that SIBO patients may cycle through different diets during different treatment phases (restriction phase, reintroduction phase, maintenance phase).

### 2. The SCD + Low-FODMAP Integration

LaPine's approach combines the two most influential SIBO dietary frameworks:
- **SCD** (Gottschall): restricts disaccharides and polysaccharides; allows monosaccharides
- **Low-FODMAP** (Monash): restricts fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols

By finding the intersection of these two diets — foods that are both SCD-legal and low-FODMAP — LaPine creates recipes that work for patients on either protocol. This is practically useful because many SIBO patients try both diets sequentially or combine elements.

### 3. Patient Education Chapters

The introductory chapters provide accessible SIBO education consistent with the 2021 consensus:
- SIBO definition and causes (consistent with Pimentel)
- The three-gas model (H2, CH4, H2S)
- Breath testing overview
- Dietary management principles
- Lifestyle factors (meal spacing, stress management, sleep)

These chapters do not introduce new claims but synthesize the existing literature for a patient audience.

### 4. Expert Acknowledgment

LaPine's acknowledgments list reads as a who's-who of the SIBO field: Pimentel, Siebecker, Jacobi, Bulsiewicz, Sarna, Ruscio, and many others. The book is explicitly curated from expert sources rather than presenting original clinical recommendations. This positions it as a synthesis, not a primary source.

## Assessment

**Value for the graph:** Low as a primary evidence source (no original claims); moderate as a practical dietary resource (multi-diet recipe tagging). The book's significance is in its demonstration that the SIBO dietary landscape has matured enough to support a multi-protocol cookbook — a marker of the field's growth from niche clinical specialty to patient-accessible practice.

**Key limitation:** The recipes assume the reader has already determined which diet is appropriate. LaPine does not provide a protocol for choosing between SCD, low-FODMAP, SSFG, Bi-phasic, and Paleo. This reflects the broader field's lack of comparative dietary trial data.

**Relationship to the graph's dietary tension:** LaPine's multi-diet approach implicitly acknowledges the unresolved question in SIBO dietary management: no single diet is universally effective, and patients often need to experiment across protocols. This pragmatism is more honest than any single-diet advocacy but less clinically directive.
