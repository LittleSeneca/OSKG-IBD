# Sarna 2021 — Healing SIBO: A Patient's Guide to the SIBO Consensus

Shivan Sarna is a SIBO patient who became a patient advocate, organizing the SIBO SOS Summits and working closely with Dr. Allison Siebecker, ND (who wrote the foreword). The book is a patient-facing synthesis of the SIBO treatment consensus as of 2021, integrating the Cedars-Sinai medical approach (Pimentel) with the naturopathic SIBO treatment tradition (Siebecker). It fills a gap the Pimentel books don't: practical, day-by-day patient guidance on navigating SIBO treatment, including herbal antimicrobial protocols, symptom management remedies, and psychological coping strategies.

## The Siebecker Connection

The foreword by Dr. Allison Siebecker, ND, is significant. Siebecker is arguably the most influential SIBO clinician-educator outside of Cedars-Sinai. She created the SIBO Specific Food Guide (SSFG), which combines SCD and low-FODMAP principles into a color-coded food tier system. She runs siboinfo.com and teaches the SIBO Pro Course to clinicians. Her endorsement of Sarna's book bridges the conventional SIBO medicine (Pimentel, rifaximin) and the naturopathic SIBO tradition (herbal antimicrobials, dietary protocols, lifestyle modification).

## Key Contributions Beyond Pimentel

### 1. The Three-Treatment-Modality Framework

Sarna presents three treatment modalities as co-equal options (Ch6):

| Modality | Examples | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Pharmaceutical antibiotics | Rifaximin (H2); Rifaximin + Neomycin/Metronidazole (CH4) | 10-14 day courses; gas-type-specific |
| Herbal antimicrobials | Oil of oregano, berberine, neem, allicin (garlic extract) | 30-day courses typically; ~50% efficacy per retrospective study |
| Elemental diet | Vivonex, Physicians' Elemental Diet, homemade formulas | 14 days; 80%+ efficacy for hydrogen SIBO |

Sarna notes that herbal protocols "won't destroy the beneficial bacteria like conventional antibiotics do" — a claim Pimentel does not make but that is common in naturopathic SIBO literature. She acknowledges that herbal protocols take longer (30 days vs. 10-14 days) and have less published evidence.

### 2. Lovastatin/SYN-010 Mention

Sarna cites Pimentel's lovastatin research as "an exciting new development" (2021), noting the modified-release formulation SYN-010 as potentially "inhibit[ing] the production of methane" for constipation-predominant SIBO. This predates the 2022 book's more cautious assessment that the non-absorbed formulation development "was not successful."

### 3. Symptom Management Remedies (Ch3)

Sarna provides a "SIBO Medicine Cabinet" of over-the-counter symptom relief options not covered in Pimentel's books:

- Digestive bitters (gentian, dandelion) to stimulate digestive juices
- Prokinetics: Iberogast (herbal), ginger, magnesium citrate
- Gas relief: activated charcoal, simethicone (acknowledged as limited)
- Pain/cramping: peppermint oil, CBD oil
- Sleep/anxiety: melatonin, magnesium glycinate, lemon balm

### 4. Relapse Management (Ch7)

Sarna's relapse chapter is the most practically detailed in the SIBO literature. Key points:

- Retesting after treatment: breath test 2-4 weeks post-antibiotics to confirm eradication
- Relapse patterns: "Most SIBO patients need more than one course of treatment or even one kind of treatment"
- Prokinetic maintenance: "once you've cleared the overgrowth, you need to keep the small intestine clean with a prokinetic" — same as Pimentel's Pillar 3
- The emotional component: "SIBO is a marathon, not a sprint" — managing expectations and psychological resilience

### 5. SIBO Prevalence

Sarna cites "up to 78 percent of people with IBS have SIBO" — splitting the difference between Pimentel's 2006 figure (84%) and his 2022 figure (60-75%). This reflects the 2021 consensus before the 2022 book's refinement.

## Alignment with Pimentel

Sarna's SIBO education (Ch1-2) is consistent with Pimentel's framework:

- SIBO definition: >10^3 bacteria/mL in small intestine; not an infection but bacterial overgrowth in the wrong location
- The three-gas model: hydrogen (diarrhea), methane (constipation), hydrogen sulfide (diarrhea)
- The MMC housekeeper wave mechanism
- Food poisoning as the most common initiating event
- IBS-SIBO connection: SIBO is often the cause of IBS

## Differences from Pimentel

| Aspect | Pimentel | Sarna |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Author role | Research gastroenterologist | Patient advocate |
| Herbal antimicrobials | Cautiously open; cites one retrospective study | Enthusiastically recommends; detailed protocols |
| Prokinetic options | Prucalopride, erythromycin, tegaserod, low-dose naltrexone | Adds Iberogast, ginger, magnesium citrate |
| Elemental diet | Vivonex (validated in published study) | Multiple brands + homemade formulas |
| Autoimmunity mechanism | Central to the argument (CdtB-vinculin) | Mentioned but not emphasized |
| IBS blood test | Prominently featured | Not mentioned |
| Tone | Scientific authority | Patient empowerment |

## Assessment

**The book's value for the OSKG graph:** Sarna provides the patient-experience dimension that Pimentel's clinician-scientist books lack. The herbal antimicrobial protocols, the symptom management remedies, and the psychological coping strategies represent the naturopathic SIBO tradition that operates in parallel to Cedars-Sinai's medical model. For the graph, these are Tier 2 claims — useful context but not primary evidence.

**Strongest contribution:** The relapse management chapter and the normalization of multiple treatment rounds. Both Pimentel books acknowledge relapse but Sarna's patient perspective makes the emotional reality of relapse more vivid and provides practical coping strategies.

**Weakest contribution:** The herbal antimicrobial efficacy claims, which rely on a single retrospective study and clinical experience rather than RCT data. Sarna appropriately caveats this, but the enthusiasm may create false equivalence between pharmaceutical and herbal approaches.

**Relationship to the evolution narrative:** Published in 2021, Sarna's book sits chronologically between Pimentel 2006 and Pimentel 2022. It incorporates the three-gas model and IMO concept (developed 2010-2020) but predates the 2022 book's more refined treatment protocols and lovastatin caution. It represents the state of SIBO knowledge as disseminated to patients at the end of the 2010s research cycle.
