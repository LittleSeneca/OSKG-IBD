# Pimentel & Rezaie -- Ch5: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth -- Definition, Diagnosis, and the Three-Gas Model

This chapter operationalizes SIBO: the quantitative definition (>10^3 CFU/mL), the diagnostic tools (lactulose breath test vs. jejunal aspirate culture), and the three-gas model that categorizes SIBO subtypes by dominant microbial gas production. It introduces the critical 2020 terminology change from "methane-dominant SIBO" to "Intestinal Methanogen Overgrowth (IMO)," reflecting the finding that methane producers are archaea (Methanobrevibacter smithii), not bacteria. The three-gas model (hydrogen, methane, hydrogen sulfide) provides a mechanistic framework linking specific microbial populations to specific symptom profiles.

## Chapter 5 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| SIBO defined as >10^3 CFU/mL; caused by impaired clearance | HIGH (>10^3 definition) / MEDIUM (colonic reflux model) | REIMAGINE suggests native small intestine overgrowth, not just colonic reflux |
| E. coli and Klebsiella are primary SIBO disrupters | MEDIUM-HIGH | Single-group data; needs independent replication |
| Three-gas model (H2, CH4, H2S) explains symptoms | MEDIUM-HIGH (H2/CH4) / MEDIUM (H2S) | H2S test not yet commercially available |
| Lactulose superior to glucose for breath testing | MEDIUM | Field lacks consensus; ACG says evidence insufficient |
| IMO terminology replaces methane-dominant SIBO | HIGH | Archaeal nature of methanogens is definitive |
| Jejunal aspirate is an unreliable gold standard | HIGH | 20% contamination rate; culture limitations well-documented |

**Strongest section:** The three-gas model and IMO terminology. These represent genuine conceptual advances that have practical implications for diagnosis and treatment. The "foxes and wolves" ecological competition model is a useful clinical heuristic.

**Weakest section:** The lactulose vs. glucose debate. Pimentel's argument for lactulose is physiologically reasonable but the field has not reached consensus, and the ACG guideline does not endorse one substrate over the other.

**Historical note:** The 2022 book's SIBO chapter represents a substantial evolution from the 2006 book. In 2006, SIBO was presented as a single entity diagnosed by lactulose breath test and treated with antibiotics. In 2022, SIBO is a family of conditions (hydrogen SIBO, IMO, hydrogen sulfide SIBO) with distinct microbial populations and gas profiles, diagnosed by multi-gas breath testing, and treated with gas-type-specific antibiotic combinations. The three-gas model did not exist in 2006. This is the most important conceptual evolution in Pimentel's thinking between the two books.
