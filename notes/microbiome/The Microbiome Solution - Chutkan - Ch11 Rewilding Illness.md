# Chutkan — Ch 11: A Rewilding Approach to Illness

Practical clinical guidance for minimizing microbiome damage during illness and medical treatment. The chapter provides specific scripts for patient-clinician communication, protocols for antibiotic damage mitigation, a catalog of medications to avoid, alternative therapies for common conditions, and a microbiome-preserving birth plan. It is the "how to" companion to Ch 5's "what is dysbiosis."

## Chapter Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Structured antibiotic questioning | MEDIUM-HIGH | No trial validation of the question set |
| Antibiotic damage mitigation protocol | HIGH (probiotics), MEDIUM (diet/supplements) | Package not tested as protocol |
| Avoid PPI/steroid/NSAID when possible | HIGH | Practicality in IBD management |
| Acne antibiotics → IBD risk | MEDIUM-HIGH | Clinical observation, not controlled evidence |
| Microbiome-preserving birth plan | HIGH | Supported by microbiome seeding literature |

**Strongest sections:** The 10 questions for antibiotic prescribing and the birth plan. Both are practical, evidence-grounded, and give patients language for clinical conversations they might otherwise not know how to have.

**Weakest section:** The dietary and supplement recommendations during antibiotics (beyond probiotics) rely more on traditional remedies and mechanistic reasoning than on antibiotic-specific clinical trials. They are unlikely to harm and may help, but the evidence base is thin.

**Clinical significance for IBD/SIBO:** The acne-antibiotic-IBD claim is the most important takeaway for the knowledge graph. Ch 11 also provides the practical bridge between recognizing dysbiosis (Ch 5) and doing something about it — the patient-empowerment scripts are useful for translating microbiome knowledge into clinical action.
