# Pimentel & Rezaie -- Ch2: Gut Anatomy, Motility, and the Migrating Motor Complex

This chapter provides the anatomical and physiological foundation for the book's central argument: that SIBO results from impaired gut motility, specifically the migrating motor complex (MMC) that sweeps bacteria from the small intestine during fasting. It establishes the two-mode model of gut function (feeding vs. fasting/cleaning), introduces the natural defenses against SIBO, and presents Pimentel's foundational 2002 finding that IBS/SIBO patients have 60% fewer housekeeping waves than healthy controls.

## Chapter 2 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Gut two-mode model (feeding/fasting MMC) | HIGH | Established GI physiology |
| IBS/SIBO patients have 60% fewer MMC waves | HIGH | Pimentel's 2002 finding; replicated |
| Five natural protective factors against SIBO | HIGH | Standard physiology; framework is Pimentel's synthesis |
| Gut as body's largest immune organ | HIGH | Standard immunology |
| SIBO as main cause of bile acid diarrhea | MEDIUM-HIGH | Understudied; SeHCAT data lacking in US |
| 30% fecal incontinence in SIBO/IBS | MEDIUM | Clinical observation; needs epidemiological validation |

**Strongest section:** The MMC mechanism and the 2002 finding of 60% reduced housekeeping waves. This is Pimentel's signature contribution and the physiological basis for the entire SIBO treatment model.

**Weakest section:** The bile acid diarrhea connection. While mechanistically plausible, the claim that SIBO is "one of the main causes" of BAD is stated without supporting data on prevalence or treatment response.

**Cross-reference note:** This chapter's MMC framework directly underpins the meal spacing rule in the LFE diet (Ch8), the prokinetic pillar of SIBO treatment (Ch6), and the anti-vinculin autoimmunity mechanism (Ch4). The five natural defenses framework explains why proton pump inhibitors (reducing stomach acid), opioids (slowing motility), and bowel surgery (removing the ileocecal valve) are all SIBO risk factors.
