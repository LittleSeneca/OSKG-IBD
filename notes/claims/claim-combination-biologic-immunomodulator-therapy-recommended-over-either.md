# aga-uc2020-6: Combination biologic + immunomodulator therapy is recommended over either monotherapy, but the evidence is almost entirely infliximab-specific and the extrapolation to other biologics is acknowledged as indirect

**Source:** [[AGA UC 2020 - Feuerstein]] — Feuerstein et al. — AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines: Ulcerative Colitis (2020)

## The Claim

Combination biologic + immunomodulator therapy is recommended over either monotherapy, but the evidence is almost entirely infliximab-specific and the extrapolation to other biologics is acknowledged as indirect

## Evidence

All based on UC-SUCCESS (infliximab + azathioprine vs. infliximab vs. azathioprine, biologic-naïve patients, terminated prematurely). Combination vs. infliximab monotherapy: RR 1.78 (1.08-1.94) for corticosteroid-free remission at week 16. Combination vs. azathioprine monotherapy: RR 1.70 (1.04-2.78). No maintenance data from RCTs. Observational French study (n=82): continuing combination superior to de-escalating to infliximab monotherapy (very low quality). The panel explicitly extrapolates from infliximab to vedolizumab and ustekinumab, citing the pharmacokinetic rationale (immunomodulators increase trough concentrations and decrease immunogenicity), while acknowledging that "immunogenicity of newer biologic agents may be lower than infliximab."

## Assessment

The AGA's extrapolation is the most aggressive inferential move in the guideline. The logic is: (1) immunomodulators increase biologic trough levels and reduce anti-drug antibodies, (2) this should benefit all biologics, (3) therefore combine. But the magnitude of benefit depends on immunogenicity — and vedolizumab and ustekinumab have substantially lower immunogenicity rates than infliximab. The added benefit of an immunomodulator may be proportionally smaller for these agents. The ACG's infliximab-only scope is more conservative but more defensible. The clinical reality is that many patients on vedolizumab or ustekinumab are on monotherapy, and the guideline's recommendation to add an immunomodulator will change practice based on pharmacokinetic theory rather than clinical trial data.
