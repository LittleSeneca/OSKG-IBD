# steinhart-surg-1: Surgery for UC is curative (colectomy removes the disease); surgery for Crohn's is damage control (disease can recur)

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch8 Surgical Treatment]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 8

## The Claim

Patients with ulcerative colitis have a major advantage over patients with Crohn's disease because their disease involves only the large intestine (colon): when the large intestine is surgically removed, the disease is effectively 'cured.'

## Evidence

Decades of surgical experience. Recurrence data: "Approximately 1 out of every 3 patients undergoing surgery for Crohn's disease will have a recurrence of symptoms within 3 to 5 years if they are on no treatment, and a portion of those will end up requiring another operation." The clinical consequence: "the real challenge in the field of surgery for Crohn's disease is not so much how to perform an operation, but how to prevent the disease from coming back."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. This is the fundamental surgical distinction between the two diseases and remains unchanged. The UC-surgery-as-cure paradigm is well-established (though pouchitis complicates the picture for IPA

## Stakes

This distinction determines surgical strategy entirely. In UC, the goal is complete removal of the colon and rectum. In Crohn's, the goal is bowel conservation -- removing only what is necessary, using strictureplasty to preserve bowel length, and accepting that re-operation is likely.

## Assessment

Steinhart's framing is correct and clinically useful. He does not shy away from the recurrence problem in Crohn's but also does not use it to dissuade patients from surgery when it is indicated. The honesty about recurrence rates empowers patients to make realistic decisions.
