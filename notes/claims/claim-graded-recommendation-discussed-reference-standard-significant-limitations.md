# acg-sibo2020-4: Small bowel aspirate and culture with >=10^3 CFU/mL is the reference standard but is invasive, expensive, and not widely practical

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

Not a graded recommendation. Discussed as the reference standard with significant limitations.

## Evidence

Standardized techniques for aseptic collection are lacking. Requires upper endoscopy with deep duodenal intubation, sterile collection into 3rd/4th portion of duodenum, immediate transfer to microbiology for aerobic/anaerobic culture with precise colony counts (not just "positive/negative"). Diagnostic agreement with breath testing is only ~65% (reference 20). 16S rRNA gene sequencing is emerging and has validated the 10^3 CFU/mL threshold by correlating with symptoms, breath testing, and sequencing results (reference 35, Leite et al.).

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The AGA Practice Update concurs: "Small bowel aspiration and culture is the most direct method... however, it is invasive, costly, and not standardized." The Rome Foundation Report emphasizes the same limitations and notes that culture captures only a fraction of small bowel microbiota (many organisms are unculturable).

## Assessment

The ~65% agreement between aspirate culture and breath testing is the key number. If the "gold standard" and the practical test agree only 2/3 of the time, we have a measurement problem, not just a clinical one. The 16S sequencing data from Leite et al. (2019) is promising -- it independently validates the 10^3 threshold by showing correlation with symptoms and breath testing results -- but large-scale validation is pending.
