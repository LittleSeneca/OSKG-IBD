# yamada-cd64-2: 5-ASA preparations are not effective for Crohn's disease despite widespread clinical use

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch64 Crohn's Disease Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

Neither sulfasalazine nor mesalamine was effective at maintaining remission in an intention-to-treat analysis.

## Evidence

Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis. Sulfasalazine: RR of 0.85 for induction in colonic disease. Mesalamine: per-protocol analysis modestly reduced relapse risk (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66-0.95) but intention-to-treat was negative. ACG and ECCO guidelines hold 5-ASA is "probably not much better than placebo."

## Stakes

If 5-ASA is ineffective for CD, billions of dollars in drug expenditure are wasted annually, and patients are exposed to ineffective therapy while disease progresses. The authors note the clinical inertia: "their relative safety and physician familiarity with these medications" perpetuates use despite evidence.
