# aga-uc2020-5: Thiopurine monotherapy is ineffective for induction but effective for maintenance; methotrexate monotherapy is ineffective for both

**Source:** [[AGA UC 2020 - Feuerstein]] — Feuerstein et al. — AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines: Ulcerative Colitis (2020)

## The Claim

Thiopurine monotherapy is ineffective for induction but effective for maintenance; methotrexate monotherapy is ineffective for both

## Evidence

Thiopurine induction: 5 trials (3 vs. placebo, 2 vs. 5-ASA). Most patients were corticosteroid-dependent. RR for corticosteroid-free remission 1.25 (1.01-1.56) but evidence rated very low due to serious risk of bias, imprecision, indirectness (variable outcome definitions, 4-52 weeks, unclear if remission was corticosteroid- or thiopurine-induced). Thiopurine maintenance: 7 trials (4 vs. placebo, 3 vs. 5-ASA). RR for preventing relapse 0.61 (0.49-0.77), rated low quality. Methotrexate: the pivotal METEOR trial showed no significant difference vs. placebo for corticosteroid-free remission (RR 1.31; 0.89-1.94). Maintenance similarly negative (RR 1.01; 0.79-1.29). Both rated very low quality.

**Recommendation 4a:** Biologic monotherapy over thiopurine monotherapy for induction. **Conditional, low quality.**

**Recommendation 4b:** No recommendation on biologic vs. thiopurine for maintenance. **Knowledge gap.**

**Evidence summary:** Based on UC-SUCCESS (infliximab vs. azathioprine vs. combination, terminated prematurely). No difference in corticosteroid-free remission at week 16 between infliximab and azathioprine (RR 0.96; 0.53-1.72), but infliximab superior for endoscopic remission. No head-to-head maintenance trials. The panel's logic: since AGA recommends biologics over placebo (moderate quality) and recommends against thiopurines for induction (very low quality), biologics > thiopurines for induction follows. But for maintenance, both strategies work over no treatment (PICO 1 and 3b), and no comparative data exist — hence the knowledge gap.

## Assessment

The methotrexate recommendation (3c) is the most clinically impactful negative recommendation in this guideline. Many clinicians extrapolate methotrexate's proven efficacy in Crohn's disease to UC — this guideline says don't. The METEOR trial was well-designed and negative. The thiopurine maintenance recommendation (3b) survives on old, methodologically weak trials — the same evidence that would not support a modern drug approval. But the RR of 0.61 for relapse prevention is clinically meaningful, and in resource-limited settings (or in patients who refuse biologics), thiopurine monotherapy remains a reasonable maintenance option. The knowledge gap on biologic vs. thiopurine maintenance (4b) is honest — we simply don't know if stepping down from a biologic to a thiopurine is safe in UC.
