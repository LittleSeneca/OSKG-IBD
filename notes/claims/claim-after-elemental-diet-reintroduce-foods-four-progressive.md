# thompson-een-6: A systematic four-phase food reintroduction protocol, testing one food at a time, is the most reliable method for identifying food triggers

**Source:** [[Thompson 2013 - Elemental Diet Protocol]] — Thompson — The IBD Remission Diet (2013)

## The Claim

After the elemental diet, reintroduce foods in four progressive phases: Phase 1 = easiest-to-digest foods (cooked, pureed vegetables, bone broth, well-cooked fruits); Phase 4 = common allergens (wheat, dairy, corn, potatoes). Test one new food per day (or one every 3 days for rigorous testing). At the end of each week, stay on just those 7 foods for another week to confirm.

## Evidence

One clinical trial on food reintroduction triggers. Thompson's personal experience and reader feedback. The phase structure is based on general digestibility principles.

## Stakes

The reintroduction protocol is arguably as important as the elemental diet itself. A successful elemental diet followed by careless reintroduction produces immediate relapse. The protocol is labor-intensive but the logic is sound: after clearing symptoms and healing mucosa, systematically identify which foods can be tolerated.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** SCD advocates would argue that many Phase 1 foods (if they include grains or starches) should NOT be reintroduced at all, since they fuel the vicious cycle. Thompson's reintroduction is based on individual tolerance testing; SCD is based on a priori carbohydrate classification.

**Alternative reading:** The food trigger hierarchy (wheat > dairy > yeast > corn > potato) from the TPN study may reflect general food intolerance patterns in the population, not IBD-specific triggers. The fact that 69% reacted to wheat doesn't necessarily mean wheat causes IBD -- it may mean wheat challenges a still-healing gut.

## Assessment

The reintroduction protocol is Thompson's most valuable contribution. It's more structured than Gottschall's "try one food at a time after a year" and more systematic than Foote's "eliminate one food at a time." The four-phase structure provides a roadmap. The weakness is the complete absence of validation -- we don't know if Phase 1 truly represents the most tolerable foods for the average IBD patient post-EEN.
