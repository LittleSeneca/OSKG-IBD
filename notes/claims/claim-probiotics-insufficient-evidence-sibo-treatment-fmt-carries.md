# acg-sibo2020-9: Probiotics have insufficient evidence for SIBO treatment; FMT carries risks and should not be used outside trials

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

Probiotics have insufficient evidence for SIBO treatment; FMT carries risks and should not be used outside trials

## Evidence

A meta-analysis (reference 112) found probiotics reduced hydrogen production (OR 1.61, CI 1.19-2.17) but studies were small and of poor quality. A controlled study (reference 23) suggested probiotics may CAUSE SIBO and D-lactic acidosis in some patients. For FMT: one study found recipients of stool from SIBO-positive donors had more GI symptoms post-FMT. An FDA warning about multidrug-resistant organisms transmitted via FMT was cited. A case report documented transmission of constipation and methane-positive breath test from donor to recipient.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The AGA Practice Update is similarly cautious on probiotics and FMT. The Rome Foundation Report discusses probiotics in the context of IBS rather than SIBO specifically.

## Assessment

The probiotic caution is well-justified -- the concept of treating bacterial overgrowth by adding more bacteria is physiologically counterintuitive, and the one controlled study showing probiotics may cause SIBO is concerning. The FMT warning is prescient given subsequent FDA safety alerts. These two key concepts are among the most clinically actionable in the entire guideline because they steer clinicians away from commonly requested but unproven interventions.

**Overall GRADE assessment:** Every single recommendation is conditional, and all but one have very low quality of evidence. R6 (antibiotics) is the only "low" rating. This is not a weakness of the guideline -- it is an honest reflection of the SIBO evidence base. The 15 key concepts effectively function as de facto recommendations where GRADE methodology cannot reach.

**Strongest section:** Treatment (R6, Table 5, recurrence data). Provides the specific, actionable guidance clinicians need.

**Weakest section:** Diet. Acknowledges the mechanistic rationale but provides no formal recommendation, leaving the most commonly asked patient question ("what should I eat?") unanswered.

**Most controversial claim:** The IMO nomenclature proposal (methanogens are not bacteria, so "SIBO" is the wrong term). Logically sound but not adopted by other societies.

**Cross-reference priority:** Compare directly with G4 (AGA SIBO 2020) -- the two major US gastroenterology societies' SIBO guidance issued in the same year. Points of agreement (rifaximin first-line, breath test limitations) and divergence (AGA is more skeptical of breath testing, less prescriptive on antibiotic choice) should be mapped.
