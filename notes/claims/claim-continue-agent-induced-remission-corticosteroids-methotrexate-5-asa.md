# acg-uc2019-12: Continue the agent that induced remission; corticosteroids, methotrexate, and 5-ASA have no maintenance role after advanced therapy induction

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Continue the agent that induced remission; corticosteroids, methotrexate, and 5-ASA have no maintenance role after advanced therapy induction

## Evidence

The MERIT-UC trial (2018): parenteral methotrexate 25 mg/week not superior to placebo for maintenance after steroid induction (66% relapse vs 63%). Thiopurines: meta-analysis shows RR 0.6 (0.37-0.95) for preventing relapse after steroid induction; NNT=5. GEMINI 1 maintenance: vedolizumab every 8 weeks maintained remission in 40% vs 16% placebo at week 52. OCTAVE Sustain: tofacitinib 10 mg BID maintained remission in 40.6% vs 11% placebo at week 52.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 (AGA 2020) aligns on all points. The thiopurine maintenance recommendation is notably conditional and based on steroid-induced remission specifically -- the guideline does not recommend thiopurines as maintenance after biologic induction (unlike in CD). The methotrexate recommendation against use is consistent across all guidelines. G9 (ECCO 2022) offers slightly more support for thiopurine monotherapy in selected patients.

## Assessment

The MERIT-UC trial (published just months before this guideline) definitively closed the door on methotrexate for UC -- a finding that separates UC from CD where methotrexate has a clear maintenance role. The recommendation against concomitant 5-ASA with biologics (R30) is cost-conscious and evidence-based: a pooled analysis of clinical trial data showed no added benefit. The conditional recommendation for thiopurine monotherapy after steroid induction acknowledges the limited evidence while preserving an option for patients who cannot access or decline biologics.
