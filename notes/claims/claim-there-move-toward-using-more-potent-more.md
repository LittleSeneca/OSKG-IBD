# steinhart-drug-2: Top-down therapy with biologics early in Crohn's disease can prevent irreversible bowel damage

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch7 Drug Therapy]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 7

## The Claim

There has been a move toward using the more potent and more consistently effective therapies that are more likely to result in ulcer healing earlier on in the course of the disease, before complications have occurred.

## Evidence

The observation that Crohn's complications (strictures, fistulas, abscesses) are often irreversible once they occur, and that surgery becomes necessary. The argument is preventive: treat aggressively before damage accumulates. Steinhart notes that predicting which patients will have aggressive disease is difficult -- "certain features of the disease and certain blood tests that might help with the determination, they are not sufficiently accurate to be reliable."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH for the principle in moderate-to-severe Crohn's. The SONIC trial demonstrated superiority of combination therapy (infliximab + azathioprine) over either alone in recent-onset Crohn's. Howe

## Stakes

If top-down is correct, delaying biologics in moderate-to-severe Crohn's allows preventable bowel damage. If step-up is correct, starting with biologics exposes patients unnecessarily to infection risk and cost. This is the central tension in Crohn's management.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Cost-conscious health systems and insurers favor step-up. Some gastroenterologists argue that the long-term safety data for biologics is still accumulating and that rushing to them in all patients is premature.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Steinhart's framing is appropriately nuanced. He does not advocate universal top-down but explains why the pendulum is swinging that direction for patients with aggressive or extensive disease. The "accelerated step care" compromise -- essentially treat-to-target with time-bound escalation -- has become the dominant approach in guidelines since 2018.
