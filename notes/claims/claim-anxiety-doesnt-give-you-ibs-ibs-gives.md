# pimentel-ch1-6: IBS is not psychosomatic -- the gut problem causes the anxiety, not the reverse

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Intro Ch1 IBS and SIBO Overlap]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Intro + Ch1

## The Claim

Anxiety Doesn't Give You IBS -- IBS Gives You Anxiety.

## Evidence

The authors cite Nicholas Talley and Marjorie Walker's research at the University of Newcastle, Australia. They document the historical injustice: IBS listed in DSM-IV until 2000, physicians telling patients "It's all in your head," the medical school teaching that "Those IBS patients you're going to see? They're all nuts!" The fact that antipsychotics and antidepressants were used with "some success" (which they note is now less common) is presented as a perverse historical validation: drugs that affect the brain affected the gut, but not because the gut problem was in the brain.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH for the directional claim. The gut-brain axis is bidirectional, and Talley's work supports the gut→brain direction in a substantial subset. However, the relationship is almost certainly bi

## Stakes

This is the most important rhetorical move in the book. By reversing the causal arrow, Pimentel destigmatizes IBS and reframes it as an organic disease. This allows patients to stop blaming themselves for their symptoms and empowers them to seek treatment. The claim also challenges the pharmaceutical approach of treating IBS with neuromodulators (antidepressants, which are still prescribed for IBS).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Psychogastroenterology researchers would argue that the brain-gut axis is bidirectional and that cognitive behavioral therapy and gut-directed hypnotherapy have demonstrated efficacy in IBS -- which would be unlikely if the gut→brain direction were exclusively causal. Mayer (The Mind-Gut Connection, 2016) presents a more nuanced bidirectional model.

**Alternative reading:** Pimentel's framing is politically necessary: after decades of patients being told their disease was imaginary, the corrective requires an emphatic reversal. A more nuanced truth (bidirectional causality, with gut→brain dominant in SIBO-positive IBS and brain→gut more relevant in other subtypes) would be less effective at combating stigma and changing clinical practice.

## Assessment

Pimentel is right about the historical injustice, and his directional claim is an important corrective. But his either/or framing (anxiety causes IBS vs. IBS causes anxiety) oversimplifies. The gut-brain axis is a feedback loop, and successful treatment likely requires addressing both ends. Pimentel's SIBO treatment (antibiotics + prokinetics) may resolve the gut→brain pathway for SIBO-positive patients, but brain→gut interventions (stress management, CBT) may still benefit patients with residual symptoms or non-SIBO IBS.
