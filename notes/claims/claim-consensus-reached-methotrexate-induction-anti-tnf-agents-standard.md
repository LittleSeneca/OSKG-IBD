# ecco-cd2020-5: No consensus was reached on methotrexate for induction; anti-TNF agents are the standard for moderate-to-severe disease (R1.5-R1.7)

**Source:** [[ECCO Crohn's Medical 2020 - Torres]] — Torres et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Crohn's Disease: Medical Management (2020)

## The Claim

No consensus was reached on methotrexate for induction; anti-TNF agents are the standard for moderate-to-severe disease (R1.5-R1.7)

## Evidence

This is the most clinically important set of recommendations in the guideline, centered on the divergent evidence for infliximab vs. adalimumab combination therapy.

**Anti-TNF vs. placebo:** Meta-analysis of RCTs supports efficacy for clinical remission (RR 1.6; 95% CI 1.17-2.36) and clinical response (RR 1.43; 95% CI 1.17-1.73). No significant differences in AEs (RR 0.99).

**Infliximab combination (SONIC trial):** Combination therapy with infliximab + AZA was superior to infliximab monotherapy for clinical remission at Week 26 (RR 1.64; 95% CI 1.07-2.53) and mucosal healing (RR 1.82; 95% CI 1.01-3.26). Lower rates of serious AEs were observed in the combination arm (RR 0.56; 95% CI 0.32-0.97).

**Adalimumab combination (DIAMOND trial):** Combination therapy with adalimumab + AZA was NOT superior to adalimumab monotherapy for clinical remission at Week 26 (RR 0.95; 95% CI 0.78-1.15). Endoscopic improvement favored combination at Week 26 (RR 1.32; 95% CI 1.06-1.65) but was lost at 1 year. The DIAMOND trial used lower AZA doses (25-100 mg/day vs. standard 2-2.5 mg/kg/day), which may explain the negative result.

**Certolizumab pegol:** Not approved in the EU for CD (commercially available in Switzerland and Russia). A 2015 network meta-analysis suggested infliximab + AZA (OR 3.1) and adalimumab monotherapy (OR 2.1) were superior to certolizumab pegol for induction.

**Methotrexate:** Only one RCT (Feagan 1995, n=141): MTX 25 mg/week IM was superior to placebo for remission in steroid-dependent patients at 16 weeks (RR 2.06). However, no studies compared MTX monotherapy vs. placebo for induction, and no consensus could be reached — the guideline makes **no recommendation on MTX for induction**. MTX may be considered when alternatives (including surgery) cannot be used.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
This is the most important area of divergence between G8 and G1. G1 (ACG 2018, R26) gives a single strong recommendation for combination therapy regardless of anti-TNF agent — it does NOT differentiate between infliximab and adalimumab. G8's differentiation (R1.7 strong for infliximab combination vs. R1.6 weak against adalimumab combination) is a more nuanced, evidence-based position that directly reflects the divergent SONIC and DIAMOND trial results. G6 (AGA 2021) is likely to align more with individualized approaches based on TDM.

## Assessment

This is the single most important section of the guideline and the clearest contribution of ECCO 2020 to CD therapeutics. The differential recommendation — combination strongly recommended for infliximab but not for adalimumab — is evidence-based and clinically actionable. It reflects the reality that different anti-TNF agents have different immunogenicity profiles. The DIAMOND trial's low-dose AZA is a significant confounder that the guideline acknowledges but doesn't fully resolve. The lack of consensus on MTX for induction leaves a genuine treatment gap for patients who cannot use thiopurines or biologics.
