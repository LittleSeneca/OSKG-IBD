# thompson-een-2: Semi-elemental formulas (whey isolate peptides) are clinically equivalent or superior to strictly elemental formulas (free-form amino acids)

**Source:** [[Thompson 2013 - Elemental Diet Protocol]] — Thompson — The IBD Remission Diet (2013)

## The Claim

Citing a controlled trial: amino acid feed = 69% remission in 3 weeks; whole protein feed = 72% remission. When groups were switched, 43% of those switched to amino acids relapsed, but NONE switched to whole protein relapsed. Thompson argues Absorb Plus (whey isolate-based) is at least as effective as free-form amino acid formulas and better for weight gain.

## Evidence

One controlled trial. Additional reference to Silk et al. (1985) on protein digestion showing di-peptide bonds are needed for muscle synthesis.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH for polymeric formulas being as effective as elemental. Multiple trials and meta-analyses support this. LOW for Thompson's specific product claims -- Absorb Plus has not been tested in ind

## Stakes

If polymeric/semi-elemental formulas work as well as elemental, the cost barrier drops dramatically (elemental formulas cost ~400% more). This makes EEN more accessible. But it also undermines the "predigested" mechanistic rationale -- if whole protein works, the mechanism isn't about predigestion.

## Assessment

Thompson's argument is supported by the literature but overstates the case for her specific product. The broader point -- that expensive elemental formulas aren't necessary and that peptide-based formulas work -- is valid and important for accessibility.
