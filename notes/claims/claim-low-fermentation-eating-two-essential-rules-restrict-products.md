# pimentel-lfe-3: The LFE diet has two essential rules: restrict non-digestible carbohydrates, and space meals 4-5 hours apart

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Low-Fermentation Eating]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), LFE

## The Claim

Low-fermentation eating has two essential rules: (1) Restrict products that contain high levels of carbohydrates or ingredients in food that humans can't digest, and therefore are digested by bacteria. (2) Space meals four to five hours apart.

## Evidence

Clinical experience over "almost 20 years" of prescribing LFE at Cedars-Sinai. "Eighty to ninety percent of our patients see some improvement with low-fermentation eating. Patients who have minimal or no improvement may have other accompanying problems to consider." Estimated improvement range: 10% to 70%.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM (no published LFE trials). The meal spacing rule (Rule 2) has strong mechanistic support from Pimentel's MMC research. The food restriction rule (Rule 1) is less precisely specified than low-FO

## Stakes

LFE is positioned as the most livable SIBO diet. Its two-rule simplicity contrasts with SCD's complex food lists and low-FODMAP's 207-item avoidance list. If LFE is equally effective, it would be the default first-line SIBO diet for its compliance advantage alone.

## Assessment

The two-rule structure is elegant and accessible. Rule 2 (meal spacing) is evidence-based and should be universal for SIBO. Rule 1 (restrict non-digestible carbs) is sound in principle but underspecified -- "avoid high levels of carbohydrates that humans can't digest" leaves substantial room for interpretation. The Appendix food lists provide more specificity but the protocol's strength (simplicity) is also its weakness (vagueness).
