# ecco-uc2022-4: Topical 5-ASA is superior to topical steroids for clinical remission; topical steroids remain an option with very low-quality evidence

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Topical 5-ASA is superior to topical steroids for clinical remission; topical steroids remain an option with very low-quality evidence

## Evidence

Topical steroids vs placebo meta-analysis of 5 RCTs: clinical remission RR 2.12 (1.48-3.06), clinical response RR 2.18 (1.58-3.01), endoscopic response RR 1.44 (1.21-1.70). Topical 5-ASA vs topical steroids (13 studies, n=1,395): clinical remission RR 1.36 (1.19-1.56) favoring 5-ASA, but clinical response RR 1.09 (0.97-1.22) and endoscopic response RR 1.08 (0.82-1.44) were not significantly different. SAEs did not differ.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019) also prefers rectal 5-ASA over rectal steroids (R7, strong, moderate quality). The ECCO quality rating of "very low" for these recommendations is notably lower than ACG's "moderate" — reflecting the ECCO methodologists' stricter assessment. Both guidelines agree on the clinical hierarchy: 5-ASA first, steroids as alternative.

## Assessment

The simultaneous strong recommendation for topical steroids (R4) and weak recommendation for 5-ASA over steroids (R5) creates a tension: if 5-ASA is superior, why is the recommendation to prefer it only "weak"? The answer lies in GRADE mechanics — the very low quality of evidence for the comparative question and the panel's acknowledgment of patient acceptability issues (some patients cannot retain enemas, prefer foam, or have 5-ASA intolerance). The guideline appropriately preserves topical steroids as a legitimate option.
