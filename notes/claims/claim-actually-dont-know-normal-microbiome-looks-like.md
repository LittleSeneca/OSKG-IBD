# pimentel-ch3-4: There is no "normal" microbiome -- each person's microbiome is unique and dynamic throughout life

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch3 Gut Microbiome]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 3

## The Claim

Actually, we don't know what a 'normal' microbiome looks like. Your microbiome is unique to you... There's no known magic mix of bacteria, which makes treatment of the microbiome complicated.

## Evidence

Diet-driven variation: "If you live in Africa, your microbiome would be different because the African diet is different from the American diet." Age-related changes: "methanogenic archaea increase by almost five-fold from childhood into the eighth decade of life." Even pets affect the microbiome. The Human Microbiome Project (NIH, 2007) demonstrated that the microbiome encompasses all body zones.

**Rating:** high

## Stakes

If there is no normal microbiome, then treatments aiming to "normalize" the microbiome (probiotics, fecal transplantation) are targeting a moving, undefined target. This supports Pimentel's approach of reducing bacterial load (antibiotics) rather than attempting to achieve a specific microbial composition.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Not documented.

**Alternative reading:** While there may be no single normal configuration, there are healthy functional patterns (metabolic capacity, diversity, resilience) that distinguish healthy from diseased microbiomes. The absence of a compositional norm does not mean the concept of a healthy microbiome is meaningless.

## Assessment

Pimentel's skepticism about "normal" microbiome definitions is justified and aligns with the broader field. It supports his antibiotic-based approach (reduce the overgrowth rather than achieve a specific composition) over probiotic-based approaches (add specific strains to achieve a target composition).
