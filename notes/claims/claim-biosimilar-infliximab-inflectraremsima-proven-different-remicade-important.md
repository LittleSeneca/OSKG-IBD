# steinhart-drug-9: Biosimilars are clinically equivalent to originator biologics and essential for cost control

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch7 Drug Therapy]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 7

## The Claim

Biosimilar infliximab (Inflectra/Remsima) has proven "not different from Remicade in any important way when used in Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis." Biosimilars "have the potential to help limit the rapidly increasing cost of drug treatment for IBD, as they are generally substantially less costly than the originator biologic drugs."

## Evidence

The regulatory pathway: biosimilar must prove chemical similarity and clinical equivalence in at least one indication, after which it receives approval for all indications of the originator. Steinhart acknowledges patient anxiety about switching: "They may ask, 'Why should I switch my medication when I am doing so well? Just so my insurance plan can save some money?'"

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The clinical equivalence of biosimilar infliximab to originator Remicade has been demonstrated in multiple studies and confirmed by extensive real-world experience since 2018.

## Stakes

Biosimilars have reduced the cost of biologic therapy by 30-50% or more in many markets. This has expanded access dramatically and made early biologic use economically feasible in public health systems. The non-medical switching debate (switching stable patients from originator to biosimilar) was active in 2018 and has largely been resolved in favor of biosimilar use.
