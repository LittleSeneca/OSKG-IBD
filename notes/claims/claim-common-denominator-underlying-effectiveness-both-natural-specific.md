# gottschall-ch12-2: The SCD and the Elemental Diet share a common mechanism via monosaccharide provision

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch1-2 Origins and Scientific Evidence]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 1-2

## The Claim

The common denominator underlying the effectiveness of both the natural Specific Carbohydrate Diet and the synthetic Elemental Diet is the type of carbohydrate which predominates. In the synthetic Elemental Diet, the principal carbohydrate is the single sugar, glucose, which, in biochemical circles, is called a monosaccharide... In the natural Specific Carbohydrate Diet, the carbohydrates are also predominantly single sugars -- those found in fruit, honey, properly-made yoghurt, and certain vegetables.

## Evidence

References to six hundred scientific publications on elemental diet efficacy (1970s-early 1980s) for malabsorption and intestinal disorders. Specific citations: cystic fibrosis of the pancreas, cancer chemotherapy diarrhea, Crohn's disease in children (dramatic weight and height gains). Notes that the elemental diet is administered via stomach tube, not sustainable long-term.

## Stakes

This is the SCD's claim to a scientific mechanism rather than folk wisdom. If the monosaccharide/disaccharide distinction is the operative variable, then any diet that eliminates disaccharides and polysaccharides should work -- making SCD, low-FODMAP, and LFE variants of the same principle rather than competing diets.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Researchers who attribute EEN's efficacy to its effect on the microbiome (exclusion of all normal dietary antigens) rather than to monosaccharide provision specifically. Exclusion diets that use polymeric formulas (whole protein, complex carbohydrates) can also induce remission -- suggesting the mechanism may be bowel rest or microbiome reset, not carbohydrate structure.

**Alternative reading:** EEN works because it bypasses normal digestion entirely (predigested nutrients), not because of monosaccharide content per se. SCD may share some elements of this (easily absorbed nutrients) but the analogy is imperfect.

## Assessment

This is the most intellectually interesting claim in the book. Gottschall correctly identified that EEN and carbohydrate-restricted diets both reduce fermentable substrate reaching the colon. She was wrong about the precise mechanism (it's not about mono vs. di, it's about fermentability), but right about the principle. Modern FODMAP research vindicates the core insight while refining the carbohydrate classification.
