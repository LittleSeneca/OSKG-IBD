# yamada-uc63-3: Infliximab has direct evidence for reducing colectomy and hospitalization in UC (ACT trials)

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch63 UC Clinical Manifestations and Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

Infliximab was shown to be effective in inducing and maintaining clinical remission of ulcerative colitis in the ACT-1 and ACT-2 trials.

## Evidence

ACT-1 and ACT-2 (each n=364, placebo vs infliximab 5 or 10 mg/kg at weeks 0, 2, 6, then every 8 weeks). Clinical response at week 8: 69.4% (ACT-1) and 64.5% (ACT-2) with 5 mg/kg vs 37.2% and 29.3% with placebo. The UC-SUCCESS trial demonstrated combination therapy (infliximab + azathioprine) superiority over monotherapy for clinical remission and endoscopic improvement.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The ACT trials are landmark studies in UC. The colectomy reduction data is a hard endpoint that few UC therapies have demonstrated directly. The UC-SUCCESS trial established combination therapy 

## Stakes

Infliximab is the best-studied biologic in UC with the strongest evidence for preventing colectomy. This makes it the preferred anti-TNF for severe hospitalized UC and for patients at high risk of colectomy.
