# acg-cd2018-5: Chromoendoscopy increases dysplasia detection over standard-definition white light, but may not be superior to high-definition white light (R2-R5)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Chromoendoscopy increases dysplasia detection over standard-definition white light, but may not be superior to high-definition white light (R2-R5)

## Evidence

A 2017 systematic review (10 RCTs, 1,500 patients) found chromoendoscopy significantly associated with higher dysplasia detection vs. other techniques (RR 1.37), but this was only significant vs. standard-definition white light (RR 2.12) — not vs. high-definition white light (RR 1.42, 95% CI 0.80-2.52). Chromoendoscopy adds ~9 minutes to procedural time. Random biopsies have extremely low yield (0.1-0.2% per biopsy) vs. targeted biopsies (80-90% of dysplastic lesions detected by targeted biopsies).

**Intestinal malignancy risk (Summary Statements 17-18):** Patients with colonic involvement have increased colorectal cancer risk (risk factors: disease duration, extent, PSC, family history, severity of ongoing inflammation). Small bowel CD carries markedly elevated relative risk of small bowel adenocarcinoma (>18-fold), though absolute risk remains low (~0.3/1,000 patient-years). Surveillance colonoscopy is recommended after 8 years of disease with ≥30% colonic involvement; PSC patients should start surveillance at diagnosis.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The SCENIC international consensus (Laine 2015, referenced) provides more detailed surveillance recommendations. G8 (ECCO 2020) is more assertive on chromoendoscopy. G6 (AGA 2021) surveillance recommendations are more aligned with SCENIC.

## Assessment

The nuanced recommendation on chromoendoscopy is honest — it helps over SD white light but the incremental benefit over HD white light is not clearly proven. The narrow-band imaging recommendation against use is definitive: two RCTs showed no benefit. The move toward targeted-only biopsies (without random biopsies) is the biggest practical change for endoscopists and is well-supported by the data.
