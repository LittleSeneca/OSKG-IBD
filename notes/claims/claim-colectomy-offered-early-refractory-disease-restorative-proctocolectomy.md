# acg-uc2019-18: Colectomy should be offered early for refractory disease; restorative proctocolectomy with IPAA is standard; staged procedures reduce complications

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Colectomy should be offered early for refractory disease; restorative proctocolectomy with IPAA is standard; staged procedures reduce complications

## Evidence

Indications for colectomy: ASUC refractory to medical therapy, chronic refractory UC, or dysplasia/carcinoma. Early postoperative complications: 9-65%. Late complications: 17-55%. Overall mortality: 1%. Preoperative corticosteroids increase infectious risk. Anti-TNF does not increase postoperative complications in UC (unlike CD where the data are mixed). Preliminary data: vedolizumab does not increase postoperative infectious complications. Staged IPAA: initial subtotal colectomy with end ileostomy, followed by pouch creation after nutritional optimization and steroid weaning.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 and G9 both endorse early surgical consultation. The staged approach recommendation is universal. G9 provides more detailed discussion of pouch surveillance after IPAA.

## Assessment

The guideline appropriately frames surgery as a therapeutic option, not a failure. The key surgical messages: (1) don't delay surgery in ASUC -- it increases complications; (2) staged procedures in the sick patient reduce morbidity; (3) biologic exposure does not meaningfully increase surgical risk in UC. Pouchitis management (a common post-IPAA complication) is not addressed in this guideline, reflecting the medical focus of the document. This is a notable gap -- the ECCO guidelines include pouchitis management separately.

**Overall GRADE assessment:** Of 49 recommendations, 14 are strong (29%) and 35 are conditional (71%). Only 2 recommendations have "high" quality evidence (R6: rectal 5-ASA for proctitis; R22: anti-TNF for induction). The overwhelming majority cluster at "moderate" or "low" quality. This is not a weakness of the guideline -- it reflects the UC evidence base, which has many effective therapies but few large, rigorous comparative-effectiveness trials. The 54 key concept statements fill the GRADE gap, functioning as expert-consensus recommendations where formal grading is impossible.

**Strongest sections:**
1. **ASUC management** (R37-R46, KC 33-41): Protocolized, evidence-based, and directly actionable. The 3-5 day steroid window, Oxford index, CYSIF/CONSTRUCT data, and surgical triggers are the standard of care.
2. **5-ASA dosing and maintenance** (R6-R18): Decades of data consolidated into clear, practical recommendations. The once-daily dosing and anti-formulation-switching messages directly improve care.

**Weakest sections:**
1. **Diet and complementary therapies** (KC 20-21): Acknowledges FMT, probiotics, and curcumin as needing more study but provides no guidance. This is honest but leaves clinicians without answers for the most common patient questions.
2. **Pouchitis management:** Completely absent. Post-IPAA pouchitis affects up to 50% of patients and is a major gap in a comprehensive UC guideline.
3. **Sequencing of advanced therapies:** The guideline makes strong recommendations for individual agents but offers no algorithm for choosing among anti-TNF, vedolizumab, and tofacitinib. The key concept about "organ-selective before systemic" is suggestive but not prescriptive.

**Cross-reference priorities:**
- **G5 (AGA UC 2020 - Feuerstein):** Compare directly. Both US guidelines from consecutive years. Major difference: AGA uses conditional recommendations and explicitly sequences infliximab/vedolizumab before tofacitinib; ACG makes strong recommendations for all three. AGA additionally covers 5-ASA dosing, rectal therapy, and scope of monitoring in more granular detail.
- **G9 (ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine):** The most current international guideline. Will differ on: (a) tofacitinib positioning (more conservative post-FDA warning), (b) inclusion of ustekinumab (not in ACG 2019), (c) histologic healing as an adjunctive target, (d) more detailed therapeutic drug monitoring algorithms, and (e) pouchitis management.
- **STRIDE-II (2021):** Directly updates the treatment targets from STRIDE-I cited in this guideline. Key change: histologic remission elevated from non-target to adjunctive measure.
- **SCENIC (2015):** The dysplasia detection consensus that this guideline partially walks back (allowing HD-WLE as equivalent to chromoendoscopy).

**Most controversial claims:**
1. **Strong recommendations for tofacitinib in anti-TNF-naive patients (R27):** Contested by AGA 2020 and ECCO 2022. Post-2021 safety data would likely downgrade this.
2. **Low-dose 5-ASA sufficient (R13):** The subgroup signal for moderate disease benefiting from 4.8 g/d, combined with very low evidence quality, makes this actively debated.
3. **Conditional surveillance recommendation (R47):** Given that surveillance colonoscopy is standard of care in every IBD center, a "very low quality" conditional recommendation feels incongruous -- but accurately reflects the absence of prospective mortality data.

**Historical significance:** This guideline marks the transition from the biologic era (anti-TNF only) to the advanced therapy era (vedolizumab, tofacitinib, and imminently ustekinumab). It was the last major UC guideline published before the JAK inhibitor safety controversy and before ustekinumab's UC approval. As such, it captures a specific moment in the therapeutic timeline -- one where optimism about small molecules was at its peak and before the safety concerns that would reshape treatment algorithms.
