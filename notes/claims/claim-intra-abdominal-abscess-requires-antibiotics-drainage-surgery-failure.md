# acg-cd2018-28: Intra-abdominal abscess requires antibiotics and drainage; surgery is not a failure of medical therapy (R60)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Intra-abdominal abscess requires antibiotics and drainage; surgery is not a failure of medical therapy (R60)

## Evidence

Intra-abdominal abscess typically results from a sinus tract or fistula, often with an associated stricture. Most CD abscesses are accessible to US- or CT-guided percutaneous drainage. After drainage, most patients benefit from delayed surgical resection (avoiding emergent surgery with diverting ostomy). Some patients, especially new diagnosis without stricturing disease, may respond to drainage + medical therapy alone. No studies directly compare percutaneous drainage + delayed surgery vs. medical therapy alone.

**Surgery indications (Summary Statements 51-53):** Intractable hemorrhage, perforation, persisting/recurrent obstruction, abscess, dysplasia/cancer, or medically refractory disease. Small bowel obstruction from fibrostenotic stricture is the most common indication. Stricturoplasty is a bowel-preserving option for patients at risk of short gut syndrome. Early surgical consultation is appropriate — "surgery is not considered to be a failure of medication."

## Assessment

The "surgery is not a failure" statement is an important philosophical correction to the common narrative that the goal is always to avoid surgery. For some patients with limited ileocecal disease, early resection may be preferable to years of escalating medical therapy. The guideline doesn't provide formal guidance on when to choose surgery over medical therapy escalation — this remains an area of clinical judgment that later guidelines attempt to systematize.

## Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (Not a Formal Recommendation)

**Context:** TDM was "beyond the scope" of this guideline. The guideline references the 2017 AGA TDM guideline and endorses the approach of checking drug levels and anti-drug antibodies in patients with loss of response. Three failure scenarios:
1. **Mechanistic failure:** Therapeutic drug levels, no antibodies, active mucosal ulceration → switch to another class
2. **Non-immune pharmacokinetic failure:** Subtherapeutic trough, absent antibodies (rapid clearance due to high inflammatory burden) → dose escalation
3. **Immune-mediated failure:** Low/undetectable trough, high anti-drug antibodies → switch within class or to another class

Suggested minimal therapeutic trough targets: infliximab >7.5 μg/mL, adalimumab >5 μg/mL, certolizumab pegol >20 μg/mL (from the 2017 AGA guideline).

**Comparison against other guidelines:** G6 (AGA 2021) will have a dedicated TDM section building on the 2017 AGA guideline. G8 (ECCO 2020) provides detailed TDM algorithms. The ACG 2018 guideline's decision to defer TDM to a separate guideline was reasonable at the time but leaves a significant practical gap.

### Overall Guideline Strengths
- **Comprehensiveness:** 60 GRADE recommendations covering the full clinical spectrum
- **Honesty about evidence quality:** "Very low" and "low" used where appropriate — the panel doesn't inflate ratings
- **Negative recommendations:** R11 (no mesalamine), R46 (no 5-ASA maintenance), R47 (no steroid maintenance), R13-R15 (no antibiotics/antimycobacterials for luminal disease), R31 (no cyclosporine/MMF/tacrolimus for CD) — these change practice
- **Fistulizing disease section:** The most detailed clinical guidance in any CD guideline at the time, with combined medical-surgical approach
- **Risk-stratified postoperative approach:** Practical algorithm balancing treatment intensity against recurrence risk
- **SONIC data (R26):** One of only five "high" quality recommendations — correctly identifies the strongest evidence in CD therapeutics

### Overall Guideline Weaknesses
- **Therapeutic drug monitoring:** Explicitly deferred to a separate guideline — creates a practical gap for readers
- **Health maintenance:** Vaccinations, bone health, cancer screening largely deferred to the separate ACG Preventive Care guideline (Farraye 2017)
- **No treatment algorithm diagram:** Unlike ECCO guidelines, no visual algorithm for treatment sequencing by risk stratum
- **Special populations:** No sections on pregnancy, elderly, or perianal Crohn's specifically — notable omissions for a comprehensive guideline
- **Biologic positioning:** Cannot answer the most common clinical question: "which biologic first?" No head-to-head data exist, but the guideline could provide more structured decision-making frameworks
- **Limited diet/lifestyle guidance:** Diet discussed only briefly; no formal recommendations

### Strongest Section
**Fistulizing disease (R35-R43) and postoperative prevention (R54-R59).** These are the most clinically detailed and practically useful sections, with specific guidance on abscess drainage, seton timing, antibiotic combination, and risk-stratified prophylaxis.

### Weakest Section
**Diet and lifestyle.** The guideline acknowledges dietary therapies may have a role but provides no formal recommendation. This is the most common patient question and the guideline is virtually silent. The lack of special population sections (pregnancy, elderly) is also a gap.

### Most Controversial Claim
**R11 (mesalamine should not be used for active CD).** Despite being "strong, moderate evidence," this recommendation directly contradicts widespread community practice. Many gastroenterologists continue to prescribe mesalamine for CD. The persistence of this practice pattern despite multiple guidelines recommending against it is a case study in guideline implementation failure.

### Key Take-Home Messages
1. **CD is progressive** — 70-80% will develop complications. Treat to prevent, not just to relieve symptoms.
2. **Symptoms ≠ inflammation** — always confirm with objective testing before escalating therapy.
3. **Mesalamine doesn't work for CD** — stop prescribing it (strong, moderate evidence).
4. **Steroids are a bridge, never a destination** — start steroid-sparing agent early; never use for maintenance.
5. **Combination therapy (anti-TNF + immunomodulator) is superior to monotherapy** — SONIC data is the best evidence in CD therapeutics (high quality).
6. **Fistulizing CD requires combined medical-surgical care** — drain abscesses first, place setons, then start anti-TNF.
7. **Postoperative prophylaxis should be risk-stratified** — anti-TNF within 4 weeks for high-risk patients; thiopurines for moderate risk; observation for low risk.
8. **Ustekinumab and vedolizumab expand the biologic armamentarium** — effective in both anti-TNF-naive and anti-TNF-failure populations.
