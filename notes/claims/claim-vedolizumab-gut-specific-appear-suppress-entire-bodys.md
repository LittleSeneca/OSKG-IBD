# steinhart-drug-7: Vedolizumab is gut-selective with an excellent safety profile; ustekinumab targets IL-12/23

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch7 Drug Therapy]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 7

## The Claim

Vedolizumab "is 'gut specific'... it does not appear to suppress the entire body's immune system, only the immune system of the gut... serious infections occurring only very rarely." For ustekinumab: "It is an antibody that attaches to and blocks the action of two proteins, called interleukin-12 and interleukin-23... inflammation caused by Crohn's disease is reduced."

## Evidence

Vedolizumab's mechanism (alpha4beta7 integrin blockade preventing lymphocyte gut homing) is described accessibly. Steinhart notes the unknowns: whether vedolizumab treats extra-intestinal manifestations (arthritis, skin, liver) is not established. Ustekinumab had only been tested in Crohn's at the time of writing (2018); its UC approval came later (2019).

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for vedolizumab's safety advantage and moderate efficacy. HIGH for ustekinumab's efficacy in Crohn's. Both drugs now have extensive post-marketing data confirming their profiles.

## Stakes

These drugs expanded the biologic options beyond anti-TNF. For patients who fail or cannot tolerate anti-TNF, vedolizumab and ustekinumab provide mechanistically distinct alternatives. The safety profile of vedolizumab makes it attractive for elderly patients or those with infection risk.

## Assessment

Steinhart correctly identifies the gut-selectivity of vedolizumab as its defining advantage. His caution about the unknowns (extra-intestinal manifestations, fistula efficacy) is appropriate for a 2018 book. The ustekinumab section is briefer, reflecting the more recent approval at the time of writing. Since 2018, both drugs have established themselves firmly in treatment algorithms.
