# yamada-cddx64-7: IBD-U represents 5-15% of colonic IBD cases where CD vs UC cannot be distinguished; misdiagnosis risk is highest when inflammatory changes are partial or when disease-modifying therapy has been initiated

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch64 Crohns Disease Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

In 5-15% of patients with isolated colonic involvement, it may not be possible initially to distinguish between ulcerative colitis or CD; these patients are given a diagnosis of IBD-unclassified (IBDU). Before making an IBDU determination, evaluation of these patients should include careful clinical, radiographic, colonoscopic, and histological assessment for features more typical for CD.

## Evidence

Features favoring CD in indeterminate colitis: family history of CD, nonbloody diarrhea and weight loss at presentation, patchy inflammation or rectal sparing on endoscopy/histology, serological markers (ASCA+/pANCA-). Features favoring UC: continuous inflammation from rectum, bloody diarrhea, pANCA+/ASCA-. The chapter emphasizes that "it is vital to wash off these exudates and examine the underlying mucosa when characterizing endoscopic disease activity" (Ch 63) because exudates can be mistaken for ulceration leading to overestimation of severity or misclassification.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The 5-15% figure is derived from cohort studies but varies by how rigorously CD features are sought.

## Stakes

IBD-U has enormous therapeutic implications. If the patient actually has CD and undergoes IPAA (ileal pouch-anal anastomosis, the standard surgery for UC), the pouch is at high risk for CD recurrence, fistula formation, and pouch failure. Conversely, if the patient has UC and is denied curative surgery because of IBD-U classification, they may endure years of unnecessary immunosuppression.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some argue IBD-U is overdiagnosed and that rigorous evaluation (including small bowel imaging, upper endoscopy, serology, and repeat colonoscopy after treatment) can correctly classify most cases. Others argue it's underdiagnosed because clinicians prematurely commit to UC or CD based on insufficient evidence.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

IBD-U is a diagnosis of humility -- it acknowledges diagnostic uncertainty rather than forcing a premature classification. The chapter's approach of using all available data (clinical, endoscopic, histological, radiological, serological) before accepting IBD-U is appropriate. In practice, many IBD-U cases eventually declare themselves as CD (if small bowel disease develops or fistulas appear) or UC (if colectomy specimens show only mucosal disease).

**Strongest section:** The imaging section (Claim 2). The description of CTE vs MRE trade-offs, the explanation of comb sign and target sign, and the superiority of MRE for perianal disease are clear, practical, and evidence-based. The Figure 64.14 comparison of CT vs MR for perianal fistula is particularly instructive.

**Weakest section:** The differential diagnosis (surrounding text for Claim 7). While the list of entities is comprehensive, the chapter lacks the systematic differential diagnosis framework that Ch 63 provided for UC. The infectious mimics (TB, MAI, amebiasis) are mentioned but the specific distinguishing features are not presented in a structured format.
