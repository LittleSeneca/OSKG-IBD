# pimentel-ch6-5: Prokinetics prevent SIBO recurrence -- serotonin agonists (prucalopride, tegaserod) are superior to erythromycin

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch6 Three Pillars of SIBO Management]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 6

## The Claim

We performed a study in which we compared erythromycin to another serotonin agonist similar to prucalopride (tegaserod). The serotonin agonist kept bacteria away for over 200 days, while erythromycin kept bacteria away for only a few months. So, in this one study, serotonin receptor agonists were more effective at preventing the relapse of SIBO than erythromycin (but erythromycin was better than not taking anything at all).

## Evidence

Comparative study of tegaserod vs. erythromycin for SIBO prevention. Pharmacological rationale: erythromycin's short half-life vs. prucalopride's longer half-life. Tachyphylaxis observation with long-term prokinetic use: "After a few months, prucalopride may lose some of its effectiveness... To treat this, we put our patients on a 'drug holiday' for two weeks."

## Stakes

Prokinetics are the closest thing to a disease-modifying therapy for SIBO. If SIBO is caused by impaired MMC, then restoring MMC function with prokinetics addresses the root cause (Pillar 3), not just the overgrowth (Pillar 2). The nighttime dosing insight is clinically important and not obvious from standard prokinetic prescribing (which is typically for constipation, taken in the morning).

## Assessment

Prokinetics are underprescribed for SIBO prevention. Gastroenterologists are familiar with prokinetics for constipation but less aware of their role in SIBO prevention. The nighttime dosing rationale and the serotonin-agonist superiority data should be more widely disseminated. The drug holiday strategy for tachyphylaxis is clinically pragmatic.
