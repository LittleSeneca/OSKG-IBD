# acg-cd2018-19: IV corticosteroids are first-line for severe/fulminant disease; anti-TNF agents (especially infliximab) may be effective (R32-R34)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

IV corticosteroids are first-line for severe/fulminant disease; anti-TNF agents (especially infliximab) may be effective (R32-R34)

## Evidence

Parenteral methylprednisolone 40-60 mg/day is effective for acute severe disease. Important caveats: pivotal anti-TNF trials excluded patients with CDAI >450 (the most severe patients). Clinical experience suggests some severely symptomatic patients respond. For fulminant disease, infliximab (weight-based dosing, generally producing higher drug exposure) may be effective, whereas adalimumab and certolizumab pegol efficacy in fulminant cases is less certain. Corticosteroids can also be used as pre-medication before infliximab infusion to reduce anti-drug antibody formation.

## Assessment

The distinction between "severe" and "fulminant" disease is clinically important but poorly defined. The guideline's caution about adalimumab/certolizumab in fulminant disease reflects the fixed-dose limitation vs. infliximab's weight-based dosing. This is a practical clinical pearl: if you have a truly sick inpatient, infliximab is the anti-TNF of choice, both because of dosing flexibility and because it's the only anti-TNF studied in the inpatient setting.
