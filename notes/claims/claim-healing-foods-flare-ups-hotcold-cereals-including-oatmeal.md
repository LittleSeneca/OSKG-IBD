# foote-cookbook-4: During flares, a low-residue diet with specific healing foods should be followed

**Source:** [[Foote 2020 - Crohn's Disease Cookbook]] — Foote — Crohn's Disease AIP Cookbook (2020)

## The Claim

The 10 healing foods for flare-ups: hot/cold cereals (including oatmeal), white pasta, white rice, white bread, potatoes, bananas, avocado, poultry, fish, well-cooked skinless vegetables. The 10 foods to avoid during flares: whole nuts/seeds, popcorn, high-fiber foods (whole grains, brown rice, raw fruits/vegetables with skins), dairy, spicy foods, high-fat/fried foods, gas-causing foods (beans, legumes, cruciferous vegetables), red meat, added sugars/artificial sweeteners, caffeine/carbonation/alcohol.

## Evidence

Clinical dietetics consensus. The low-residue/low-fiber approach during acute IBD flares is standard practice. The "10 healing foods" list overlaps substantially with the traditional "BRAT" diet (bananas, rice, applesauce, toast) expanded for nutritional adequacy.

## Stakes

The flare diet represents the practical minimum of dietary restriction. Unlike SCD (which restricts grains, potatoes, and many of Foote's "healing foods"), the low-residue approach prioritizes easily digested starches as energy sources during catabolic states.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** SCD would eliminate white pasta, white rice, white bread, cereals, oatmeal, and potatoes -- all of Foote's healing staples. The SCD model says these starches fuel bacterial fermentation and perpetuate the vicious cycle. The low-residue model says they provide needed calories without mechanical irritation. This is a fundamental disagreement about whether fermentable substrate or mechanical irritation is the primary dietary problem in IBD flares.

**Alternative reading:** Both approaches may work for different patients. Patients with stricturing Crohn's (narrowed intestinal lumen) genuinely need low-residue to prevent obstruction regardless of fermentation effects. Patients with inflammatory/microbial-driven disease may benefit more from SCD's fermentation reduction.

## Assessment

This is the central tension in IBD dietary therapy: low-residue (feed the patient, minimize mechanical damage) vs. SCD/low-FODMAP (starve the microbes, reduce fermentation). The two approaches recommend opposite foods for some categories (grains, potatoes). There is no head-to-head trial. Clinical wisdom suggests low-residue for obstructive/stricturing disease and fermentable-carbohydrate restriction for diarrheal/inflammatory/microbial-driven disease, but this is expert opinion, not evidence.
