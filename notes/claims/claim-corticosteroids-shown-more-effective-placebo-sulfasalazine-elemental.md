# yamada-cd64-3: Corticosteroids effectively induce remission but do not produce mucosal healing and should not be used for maintenance

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch64 Crohn's Disease Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

Corticosteroids have been shown to be more effective than placebo, sulfasalazine, and elemental diets for the induction of clinical remission.

## Evidence

Multiple studies comparing corticosteroids to placebo, sulfasalazine, and elemental diets. Population-based data showing "about a third of patients with CD treated with corticosteroids for a disease flare will be steroid dependent over the following year." Budesonide (ileal-release, 9 mg daily) is more effective than placebo for mild-moderate ileal CD but less effective than prednisone, and is not effective for maintenance beyond 6 months.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The steroid paradox (effective for symptoms, ineffective for mucosal healing, toxic long-term) is well-established. The budesonide data is consistent across trials.

## Stakes

Steroid dependence and steroid toxicity remain major clinical problems. The need for corticosteroids is itself "a marker of poor prognosis." Patients who require repeated steroid courses should be escalated to steroid-sparing therapy (immunomodulator or biologic).

## Assessment

The authors present the steroid data clearly and with appropriate clinical guidance. The budesonide section correctly positions it as a first-line agent for mild-moderate ileal/right-sided CD while noting its limitations. The statement that "patients who require a prolonged course or repeated courses of corticosteroids should be considered for maintenance therapy with an immunomodulator or biologic" is the key clinical takeaway.
