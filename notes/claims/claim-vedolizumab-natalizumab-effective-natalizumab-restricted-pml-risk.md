# acg-cd2018-16: Vedolizumab and natalizumab are effective; natalizumab is restricted by PML risk (R27-R29)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Vedolizumab and natalizumab are effective; natalizumab is restricted by PML risk (R27-R29)

## Evidence

Vedolizumab (gut-selective α4β7 integrin inhibitor): No cases of PML reported. May have slower onset than anti-TNF agents, particularly in anti-TNF-experienced patients. A network meta-analysis (Hazlewood 2015) suggested adalimumab or infliximab + azathioprine may be more effective than vedolizumab for induction and maintenance — but no head-to-head trials exist.

Natalizumab (non-selective α4 integrin inhibitor): Effective but carries PML risk as high as 1:100 in JC virus antibody-positive patients with prior immunosuppression and >2 years of use. Restricted to JC virus antibody-negative patients.

## Assessment

Vedolizumab's "high" evidence rating reflects the GEMINI trials (Sandborn 2013). The slower onset is clinically relevant — don't expect vedolizumab to work as quickly as anti-TNF in a hospitalized patient. The positioning relative to anti-TNF agents is the key clinical question this guideline cannot answer: no head-to-head data exist. The guideline states vedolizumab can be used before anti-TNF failure ("not mandatory or advocated" that patients fail anti-TNF first), but the pragmatic reality is that most payers require anti-TNF failure first.
