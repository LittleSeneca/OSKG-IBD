# acg-uc2019-5: Rectal 5-ASA (1 g/d) is first-line for proctitis; combined oral + rectal 5-ASA is superior to oral alone for left-sided disease

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Rectal 5-ASA (1 g/d) is first-line for proctitis; combined oral + rectal 5-ASA is superior to oral alone for left-sided disease

## Evidence

Meta-analysis of 11 RCTs: 5-ASA superior to placebo for induction (RR failure to remit 0.79; NNT=6). For proctitis/left-sided: rectal 5-ASA pooled OR 8.30 for symptomatic remission, 5.31 for endoscopic remission vs placebo. Rectal 5-ASA superior to rectal steroids (OR 1.65). Combination oral + rectal 5-ASA: RR induction failure 0.65 (95% CI 0.47-0.91) vs oral alone. No significant differences by dose (1 g vs 4 g rectal) or formulation (liquid, gel, foam, suppository).

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 (AGA UC 2020) makes essentially identical recommendations: rectal 5-ASA for proctitis, combined for left-sided, oral for extensive. G9 (ECCO 2022) similarly endorses 5-ASA as first-line with combined therapy for distal disease.

## Assessment

The "high quality of evidence" for R6 (rectal 5-ASA for proctitis) is one of only two high-quality ratings in the entire guideline -- a testament to the maturity of the 5-ASA evidence base after decades of use. The conditional rating for combination therapy (R8) is due to heterogeneity in the 4 RCTs rather than weak effect -- the NNT is compelling. The preference for 5-ASA over rectal steroids is well-supported, but the guideline appropriately notes rectal steroids remain an option for patients who cannot retain enemas or are 5-ASA intolerant.
