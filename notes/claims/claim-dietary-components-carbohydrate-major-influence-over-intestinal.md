# gottschall-ch35-4: Undigested carbohydrates are fermented by intestinal microbes, producing acids, gas, and microbial by-products that injure the intestine

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch3-5 The Vicious Cycle Mechanism]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 3-5

## The Claim

Of all dietary components, carbohydrate has the major influence over intestinal microbes. Through a process of fermentation of available carbohydrates remaining in the intestinal tract, microbes obtain energy for continued maintenance and growth.

## Evidence

The lactose example: lactose in one ounce of milk, if undigested, produces about 50 ml of gas in normal people; under abnormal conditions with small intestinal microbes, hydrogen gas production may increase over one hundred-fold. Reference to lactic acid causing "abnormal brain function and behavior" which could explain behavioral problems accompanying intestinal disorders.

## Stakes

This is the physiological mechanism that connects carbohydrate malabsorption to symptoms: gas → bloating/pain, organic acids → mucosal injury, osmotic effect → diarrhea. If this mechanism is correct, the therapeutic strategy follows: reduce fermentable substrate reaching the colon.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The mechanism is not disputed. Debate centers on whether carbohydrate fermentation is a sufficient explanation for IBD pathogenesis (vs. immune dysregulation, genetic susceptibility, barrier dysfunction). Gottschall presents fermentation as the primary driver; modern IBD models treat it as one factor among many.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

This is solid biochemistry. The fermentation model is the conceptual foundation shared by SCD, low-FODMAP, and LFE diets. The differences between these diets are in which carbohydrates they classify as fermentable, not in the underlying mechanism.
