# aga-cd2021-2: Infliximab, adalimumab, and ustekinumab are strongly recommended over no treatment; vedolizumab is conditionally recommended; natalizumab is recommended against (R1A-D)

**Source:** [[AGA Crohn's 2021 - Feuerstein]] — Feuerstein et al. — AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines: Crohn's Disease (2021)

## The Claim

Infliximab, adalimumab, and ustekinumab are strongly recommended over no treatment; vedolizumab is conditionally recommended; natalizumab is recommended against (R1A-D)

## Evidence

13 RCTs for induction (4-12 weeks), 9 RCTs for maintenance (22-54 weeks). All interventions were superior to placebo. Key RRs for failure to induce remission: infliximab 0.54 (95% CI 0.39-0.75), adalimumab 0.82 (0.75-0.89), certolizumab pegol 0.92 (0.86-0.98), vedolizumab 0.92 (0.87-0.97), ustekinumab 0.90 (0.85-0.94). Certolizumab and vedolizumab did not meet the MCID of 10% absolute benefit for induction — hence the downgraded ratings. Natalizumab did show benefit (induction RR 0.88, maintenance RR 0.58) but the PML risk and availability of safer alternatives drove the recommendation against use.

## Assessment

The natalizumab reversal is the most clinically consequential single recommendation. It effectively removes natalizumab from the CD armamentarium for all but the most exceptional cases. The differential treatment of anti-TNF agents (infliximab/adalimumab "recommended" vs. certolizumab "low quality") foreshadows the formal ranking in R2A. The ustekinumab "strong" recommendation at the same tier as anti-TNF agents was forward-looking in 2021 — ustekinumab was the newest biologic and this recommendation positioned it as a legitimate first-line option, not just a second-line after anti-TNF failure.
