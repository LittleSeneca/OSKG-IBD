# acg-cd2018-6: NSAIDs may exacerbate disease and should be avoided; smoking cessation is critical (R6-R7)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

NSAIDs may exacerbate disease and should be avoided; smoking cessation is critical (R6-R7)

## Evidence

NSAIDs damage small intestinal mucosa (ulcerations, erosions, webs) and increase mucosal permeability. Non-selective NSAIDs (naproxen, nabumetone) caused 17-28% relapse rate within 9 days in quiescent IBD (Takeuchi 2006). Recent NSAID use associated with increased emergency hospital admissions. Selective COX-2 inhibitors have not been shown to exacerbate UC in short-term studies, but CD-specific data are lacking.

**Recommendation 7:** Cigarette smoking exacerbates disease activity and accelerates disease recurrence; active smoking cessation programs should be encouraged. **Strong recommendation, low level of evidence.**

**Evidence summary:** Smoking is associated with increased surgical intervention, hospitalizations, peripheral arthritis, penetrating phenotype, and relapse after anti-TNF discontinuation. Smoking cessation decreases flares, steroid need, and immunomodulator requirement. Smoking is the only modifiable risk factor for postoperative recurrence.

## Assessment

Both are "strong, low evidence" — the evidence quality is low because RCTs of NSAID avoidance and smoking cessation in CD are neither feasible nor ethical, but the observational signal is consistent and the recommendations are unequivocal. The NSAID recommendation is clinically challenging: what do you tell a CD patient with severe arthritis? The guideline doesn't fully address this, though it hints that COX-2 inhibitors may be safer (UC data only).
