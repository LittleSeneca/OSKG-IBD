# foote-cookbook-6: Structured meal planning for both flare and remission states improves dietary adherence and nutritional adequacy

**Source:** [[Foote 2020 - Crohn's Disease Cookbook]] — Foote — Crohn's Disease AIP Cookbook (2020)

## The Claim

Two distinct one-week meal plans are provided: a "Meal Plan for Flare-Ups" (low-fiber, dairy-free, gentle foods) and a "Meal Plan for Remission" (more variety, more fiber, more protein sources). The flare plan emphasizes: oatmeal, white rice, white pasta, potatoes, poultry, fish, melon, banana, avocado, well-cooked vegetables. The remission plan adds: berries, peanut butter, eggs, pork, beef, tofu, tortillas, pitas, a wider range of vegetables.

## Evidence

Practical dietetics. No comparative trial data on meal planning vs. ad hoc eating in IBD.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** LOW for the specific meal plans being optimal. HIGH for the general principle that planned eating improves nutritional adequacy. The meal plans are illustrative, not prescriptive -- Foote explicitly s

## Stakes

The meal plans operationalize the dietary principles from Ch2. They demonstrate that a Crohn's diet can be varied, appealing, and nutritionally complete -- countering the perception that IBD diets are necessarily bland or restrictive.

## Assessment

The meal plans are pedagogically useful but not clinically validated. The flare plan provides ~2000 calories/day (estimated from recipe portions) and the remission plan ~2200-2400, which is appropriate for weight maintenance in adults. The key contribution is demonstrating feasibility: a Crohn's diet doesn't have to be miserable.
