# pimentel-ch1-5: The Rome Criteria never diagnosed IBS -- they only excluded other diseases

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Intro Ch1 IBS and SIBO Overlap]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Intro + Ch1

## The Claim

The main problem with the Rome Criteria is that they never really diagnosed IBS, as the criteria required first eliminating other diseases. For example, 70 to 80 percent of people with Crohn's disease meet the Rome Criteria, making the Rome Criteria useful only after the clinician eliminates other diseases.

## Evidence

The Rome Criteria evolution: Rome I (1988), Rome II (1998), Rome III (2008), Rome IV (2016). Rome IV removed "abdominal discomfort," retaining only "abdominal pain." Pimentel notes the Rome IV criteria: "if you have persistent, weekly abdominal pain for more than three months that's associated with a change in bowel habits, you fit the diagnosis of IBS. No diagnostic testing or biomarkers have yet been incorporated." The 70-80% overlap with Crohn's disease demonstrates the criteria's non-specificity.

## Stakes

This directly supports Pimentel's argument that IBS needs a positive biomarker (the breath test for SIBO). Without it, IBS remains defined by what it is not rather than what it is.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The Rome Foundation would argue that the criteria have improved diagnostic specificity with each iteration, and that symptom-based criteria remain the international standard because no biomarker has been validated for clinical use. They would note that the Crohn's overlap is a feature of symptom-based criteria in general, not a flaw specific to IBS.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Pimentel's critique of Rome is valid but overstated. The Rome Criteria provide a positive case definition (not purely exclusionary) -- the criteria specify what IBS IS (abdominal pain + altered bowel habits for 3+ months), not just what it isn't. The problem is that the positive definition is insufficiently specific without the exclusion step. Pimentel's contribution is proposing a biomarker (breath test) that could add specificity to the Rome framework.
