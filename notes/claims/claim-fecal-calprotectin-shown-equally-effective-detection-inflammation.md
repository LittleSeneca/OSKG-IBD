# yamada-cddx64-6: Fecal calprotectin is useful for noninvasive monitoring but shows equal efficacy for colonic and small bowel CD

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch64 Crohns Disease Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

Fecal calprotectin has been shown to be equally effective for the detection of inflammation in both colonic and small bowel CD... Changes in stool calprotectin have been positively associated with variability in the disease course, and calprotectin has been shown to increase to more than normal values several months before clinical signs of a disease flare are apparent both in ulcerative colitis and in CD.

## Evidence

Calprotectin correlates with serum CRP, endoscopic activity, and histological inflammation. It predicts postoperative recurrence after ileocecal resection. It rises months before clinical flare, suggesting a role in proactive monitoring. However, it is "relatively nonspecific and can be elevated in a variety of conditions in addition to IBD, including infection and malignancy." The chapter notes that some patients may not mount a CRP response despite active inflammation, making calprotectin particularly useful in this subgroup.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The calprotectin data for CD is robust, though the sensitivity is somewhat lower than for UC (the Ch 63 meta-analysis showed calprotectin is more sensitive in UC than CD).

## Stakes

The finding that calprotectin rises months before clinical symptoms is clinically significant -- it supports proactive monitoring with preemptive treatment adjustment rather than waiting for symptoms, which would align with the "treat to target" paradigm of targeting mucosal healing. However, the nonspecificity means an elevated calprotectin should trigger endoscopic confirmation, not blind treatment escalation.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The optimal calprotectin threshold for CD is debated. The chapter does not specify thresholds as explicitly as Ch 63 did for UC (50/250), reflecting the greater heterogeneity in CD.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Calprotectin monitoring is especially valuable in CD because clinical symptoms correlate poorly with endoscopic activity -- a patient can have active small bowel inflammation with normal stool frequency and no rectal bleeding. The "months before symptoms" lead time is the strongest argument for serial calprotectin monitoring in high-risk patients.
