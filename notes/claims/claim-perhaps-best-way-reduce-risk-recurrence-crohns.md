# steinhart-surg-6: Smoking cessation is the most effective intervention for preventing post-surgical Crohn's recurrence

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch8 Surgical Treatment]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 8

## The Claim

Perhaps the best way to reduce the risk of recurrence of Crohn's disease after surgery is not to smoke cigarettes. Although it has not been proven that for smokers who quit after surgery the risk of recurrent disease is lower, it has been shown that smokers tend to have earlier and more severe recurrences after surgery.

## Evidence

Observational data. Steinhart lists the medication options for post-operative prevention: 5-ASA (reduces recurrence by "about 10% to 12%"), antibiotics ("not clear whether the beneficial effect... extends past 1 year after surgery"), thiopurines ("evidence supporting their use is not entirely clear"), and infliximab ("some evidence that it is an effective preventive treatment after surgery").

## Stakes

Post-operative recurrence prevention is the unsolved problem in Crohn's surgery. The 5-ASA benefit (10-12% risk reduction) is modest. The thiopurine evidence is mixed. Anti-TNF agents have the strongest evidence. The decision to use prophylaxis versus surveillance colonoscopy at 6-12 months with treatment only for endoscopic recurrence (the "treat when you see it" strategy) remains debated.

## Assessment

Steinhart correctly identifies smoking as the single most impactful modifiable risk factor. His review of pharmacologic options accurately reflects the 2018 evidence base. Since then, the PREVENT trial and other studies have strengthened the case for post-operative anti-TNF prophylaxis in high-risk patients. The surveillance colonoscopy approach he describes (scope at 6-12 months, treat endoscopic recurrence before symptoms) has become standard practice.
