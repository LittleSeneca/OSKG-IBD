# yamada-cddx64-5: Serological markers have prognostic but NOT diagnostic utility in CD; they should not be used for screening or diagnosis

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch64 Crohns Disease Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

Because of low sensitivity, screening for IBD in the general population is not appropriate... serological testing is insufficiently sensitive and specific to accurately diagnose IBD independent of clinical, endoscopic, and histological criteria.

## Evidence

IBD-associated antibodies (ASCA IgA/IgG, anti-OmpC, anti-CBir1, anti-I2) have >90% specificity but low sensitivity for distinguishing IBD from non-IBD. Diagnostic accuracy improves when markers are combined. Small bowel CD is associated with higher ASCA, CBir1, OmpC, and I2 levels; colonic CD and UC are associated with higher ANCA levels. Prognostic value: complicated CD (stricturing/penetrating) is associated with both increased number and higher titers of antibodies. These markers can predict future complicated disease when drawn at diagnosis. Postoperative recurrence in pediatric CD is associated with anti-CBir1. High ANCA levels are associated with non-response to anti-TNF therapy. However: "There has been little work in determining the role of serologies in ethnically diverse populations, but the limited data suggest that differences exist."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for prognostic associations. HIGH for the recommendation against diagnostic use.

## Stakes

Commercial serological panels (Prometheus IBD sgi Diagnostic, etc.) are marketed as diagnostic tools, but the evidence does not support this use. The chapter is unambiguous: serology is adjunctive, not diagnostic. The prognostic utility is more promising -- a patient with high-titer multiple antimicrobial antibodies at diagnosis may warrant more aggressive initial therapy.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some clinicians use serological panels in IBD-U to guide classification (ASCA+/pANCA- favors CD; pANCA+/ASCA- favors UC). The chapter acknowledges this limited role but does not endorse it as standard practice.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

This is a consistent message across chapters 62, 63, and 64: IBD serology is for prognosis, not diagnosis. The prognostic value in CD is better established than in UC. The lack of data in non-European populations is a significant gap -- most serological studies were conducted in Caucasian cohorts, and the limited data suggest different performance characteristics in African American and Asian populations.
