# gottschall-ch35-6: The final step of carbohydrate digestion occurs at the microvilli of intestinal absorptive cells, and this step is vulnerable to damage

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch3-5 The Vicious Cycle Mechanism]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 3-5

## The Claim

Carbohydrates must be digested to monosaccharides before absorption. Starch is broken down by salivary and pancreatic amylases to disaccharides (maltose, isomaltose). Disaccharides (lactose, sucrose, maltose, isomaltose) must be split by disaccharidase enzymes (lactase, sucrase, maltase, isomaltase) embedded in the microvilli membranes of intestinal absorptive cells. "The structure of the intestinal surface is dramatically altered during intestinal disease and, as a result, digestive activity is seriously inhibited."

## Evidence

Diagram of healthy vs. injured microvilli. Listing of conditions associated with disaccharidase deficiencies: celiac disease, malnutrition, tropical sprue, cholera, gastroenteritis, infant diarrhea, pellagra, irritable colon, post-gastrectomy, soy protein intolerance, cow's milk protein intolerance, intractable diarrhea of infancy, Giardia, cystic fibrosis, Crohn's disease, and ulcerative colitis.

## Stakes

This provides the rationale for WHY disaccharide-containing foods must be restricted during active intestinal disease. Lactose intolerance is the most obvious clinical manifestation -- lactase is the first enzyme lost and the last to recover.
