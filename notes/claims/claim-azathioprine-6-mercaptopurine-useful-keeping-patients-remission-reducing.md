# steinhart-drug-5: Thiopurines (azathioprine/6-MP) are effective steroid-sparing agents for maintenance but carry rare but serious risks

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch7 Drug Therapy]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 7

## The Claim

Azathioprine and 6-mercaptopurine... are useful for keeping patients in remission and reducing the need, over time, for other medications, such as steroids.

## Evidence

Decades of clinical use since the 1960s-70s. The SONIC trial (combination therapy superiority). Side effect data: pancreatitis in ~3%, allergic reactions, bone marrow suppression, liver enzyme abnormalities. Cancer risk: "the risk of lymphoma... is extremely small. It has been calculated that azathioprine would need to be taken for more than 4,300 years for someone between the ages of 20 and 29 before observing one extra case of lymphoma."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

## Stakes

Thiopurines remain the most commonly used immunomodulator worldwide due to cost and oral administration. The risk/benefit calculus changes with the availability of biosimilars (cheaper biologics may displace thiopurines) and with the recognition of lymphoma risk in young males on combination therapy.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some pediatric gastroenterologists avoid thiopurines entirely in young males due to HSTCL risk, preferring methotrexate or early biologic monotherapy.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Steinhart presents an even-handed assessment. He does not minimize the risks but places them in the context of absolute magnitude. His recommendation for TPMT testing before starting, regular blood monitoring, and minimum 4-year treatment duration reflects standard practice.
