# pimentel-ch2-3: The gut has five natural protective factors against SIBO, and failure of any can permit overgrowth

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch2 Gut Anatomy and MMC]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 2

## The Claim

Pimentel lists five natural defenses (Table 2.2): (1) Phase III migrating motor complexes (housekeeper waves), (2) antimicrobial effects of bile acid and pancreatic secretions, (3) the ileocecal valve (preventing colonic reflux into small intestine), (4) stomach acid (killing ingested bacteria before they reach the small intestine), and (5) mucosal immunity (GALT, Peyer's patches, tight junctions).

## Evidence

Standard GI physiology. The stomach produces "two liters, or a half gallon, a day" of highly acidic fluid "strong enough to eat through a sheet of steel" that kills most bacteria. The ileocecal valve "has an important role in separating the colon microbiome from that of the small intestine, which contains less than 1000 bacteria per mL."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. These protective factors are standard GI physiology. Pimentel's contribution is synthesizing them into a unified SIBO defense framework and showing how failure of any one can permit overgrowth.

## Stakes

This framework explains why PPI use (reducing stomach acid) is a SIBO risk factor, why ileocecal valve resection causes SIBO, and why pancreatic insufficiency predisposes to SIBO. It also implies that SIBO treatment should address the underlying protective factor deficit, not just the bacterial overgrowth.
