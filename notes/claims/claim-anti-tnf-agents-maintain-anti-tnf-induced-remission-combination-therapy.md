# acg-cd2018-23: Anti-TNF agents maintain anti-TNF-induced remission; combination therapy reduces immunogenicity (R49-R50)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Anti-TNF agents maintain anti-TNF-induced remission; combination therapy reduces immunogenicity (R49-R50)

## Evidence

Multiple RCTs (ACCENT I, CHARM, PRECiSE) demonstrate maintenance efficacy. A meta-analysis of 21 studies (5,356 patients) found anti-TNF therapy did not increase risk of serious infection, malignancy, or death vs. placebo (though lymphoma and TB were noted at higher rates in individual trials). Combination therapy reduces immunogenicity; however, some evidence (SONIC *post hoc* analysis) suggests that maintaining robust trough levels with monotherapy may achieve the same effect. Risks of combination therapy: higher lymphoma risk (especially hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma in young males on thiopurine + anti-TNF), though no higher risk of opportunistic infections vs. either agent alone.

## Assessment

R49 and R50 are the maintenance counterparts to R24-R26. The "high" evidence for anti-TNF maintenance is well-established. R50 introduces the therapeutic tension: combination therapy is more effective but carries more risk. The emergent TDM paradigm (referenced but not detailed in this guideline) offers a potential resolution: use combination therapy initially, then consider withdrawing the immunomodulator if trough levels are consistently adequate. This is where later guidelines will provide more granular guidance.
