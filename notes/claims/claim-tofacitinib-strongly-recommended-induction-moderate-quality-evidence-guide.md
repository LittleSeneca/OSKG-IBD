# ecco-uc2022-11: Tofacitinib is strongly recommended for induction with moderate-quality evidence; the guideline explicitly addresses emerging safety concerns (herpes zoster, VTE, MACE, malignancy) that post-date the ACG 2019 guideline

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Tofacitinib is strongly recommended for induction with moderate-quality evidence; the guideline explicitly addresses emerging safety concerns (herpes zoster, VTE, MACE, malignancy) that post-date the ACG 2019 guideline

## Evidence

Two RCTs (n=1,220): OCTAVE 1 and 2. Clinical response RR 1.79 (1.49-2.14), clinical remission RR 3.26 (1.95-5.43), endoscopic response RR 5.18 (2.12-12.69). Half of patients had prior anti-TNF failure; efficacy was similar regardless of anti-TNF exposure status. Network meta-analyses (Singh 2020): no statistical difference between tofacitinib and anti-TNF agents or ustekinumab in biologic-naive patients, possible benefit over adalimumab or vedolizumab in anti-TNF-exposed patients.

**Safety — the most detailed safety discussion of any agent in this guideline:** Herpes zoster: RR 6.53 across JAK inhibitor trials (Olivera 2020), incidence rate 4.1/100 person-years in UC program, dose-dependent (higher with 10 mg BID), more common in older patients and those on concomitant corticosteroids. VTE: ORAL Surveillance in RA patients ≥50 years with cardiovascular risk factors — significantly increased VTE risk with tofacitinib 10 mg BID vs anti-TNF; not seen at 5 mg BID. EMA recommended using lowest efficacious dose and avoiding 10 mg BID maintenance in patients with VTE risk factors. The RIVETING trial (Vermeire 2021): patients in sustained remission ≥6 months on 10 mg BID randomized to continue or de-escalate to 5 mg BID — remission rates 90% vs 77% at 6 months, numerically fewer herpes zoster cases at 5 mg BID. Post-marketing surveillance also suggested increased cardiac events and malignancies (EMA 2021). The guideline concludes: "the efficacy data, including in patients with previous anti-TNF exposure, along with the benefits associated with oral dosing and lack of immunogenicity, support our recommendations for tofacitinib as a treatment option... with the risks and benefits to be considered for each patient."

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
This is where the publication timeline matters most. G2 (ACG 2019) made strong recommendations for tofacitinib in both anti-TNF-naive and anti-TNF-exposed patients (R27, R28) with moderate quality evidence and minimal safety caveats — the OCTAVE data were fresh and the ORAL Surveillance results were not yet available. The ECCO guideline, published 3 years later, incorporates the full post-marketing safety picture. The recommendation remains strong, but the supporting text is notably more cautionary. G5 (AGA 2020) was intermediate: conditional recommendations, preference for infliximab or vedolizumab before tofacitinib in biologic-naive patients. ECCO's approach — strong recommendation but with detailed risk stratification — represents a practical middle ground: tofacitinib works well, but patient selection matters.

## Assessment

The tofacitinib section is the guideline's most careful piece of writing. The panel walked a difficult line: maintaining a strong recommendation (because the efficacy data are robust) while honestly presenting safety concerns that have reshaped prescribing. The actionable guidance is in the details: use 5 mg BID for maintenance when possible, avoid 10 mg BID in patients with VTE risk factors, counsel about herpes zoster (consider recombinant zoster vaccine), and weigh the oral convenience against safety in older patients. The mention of the RIVETING de-escalation data (90% vs 77% remission) is particularly clinically useful — it suggests that many patients on 10 mg BID can safely step down.
