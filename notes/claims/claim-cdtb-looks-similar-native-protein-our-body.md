# pimentel-ch4-2: CdtB toxin from food poisoning bacteria triggers autoimmunity against vinculin via molecular mimicry -- this is the unifying mechanism for post-infectious IBS

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch4 Food Poisoning Autoimmunity]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 4

## The Claim

CdtB looks similar to a native protein in our body called vinculin. This 'molecular mimicry' can fool our body to produce anti-vinculin antibodies... Vinculin is an important protein in our body that ultimately helps the muscles of our gut contract efficiently. If there are antibodies attacking the vinculin in our body, gut function is impaired and ultimately leads to abnormal movements of gut, including the cleaning waves.

## Evidence

Animal model: rats infected with Campylobacter jejuni developed IBS-like bowel patterns, increased white blood cells, bacterial overgrowth, and nerve damage to gut cleaning waves three months after infection cleared. The toxin identification: "We looked for a toxin common to these bacteria and found cytolethal distending toxin (Cdt)." Cross-reactivity: anti-CdtB antibodies "reacted with nerves in the rat's gut, even in the rats that had never had a Campylobacter infection." The molecular mimicry: CdtB and vinculin share structural similarity at a specific molecular weight.

## Stakes

This is the most important mechanistic claim in SIBO research. If CdtB-vinculin autoimmunity causes SIBO in the majority of IBS patients, then: (a) the blood test is a true diagnostic, not just a biomarker, (b) treatments should target the autoimmune component (unclear how currently), (c) SIBO may be more analogous to autoimmune diseases like rheumatoid arthritis than to infectious diseases, and (d) preventing post-infectious autoimmunity (e.g., early antibiotic treatment of Campylobacter) could prevent IBS.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some researchers question whether anti-vinculin antibodies are causal rather than epiphenomenal. The specificity of the blood test (can it distinguish IBS from other causes of diarrhea?) needs broader validation outside Cedars-Sinai. The molecular mimicry hypothesis requires structural confirmation of the CdtB-vinculin cross-reactivity at the atomic level.

**Alternative reading:** Anti-vinculin antibodies may be a marker of gut damage rather than its cause. The immune response to food poisoning may be neuroinflammatory rather than specifically autoimmune -- bacterial toxins may directly damage enteric nerves without antibody involvement. The CdtB-vinculin autoimmunity may explain a subset of post-infectious IBS but not all cases.

## Assessment

The CdtB-vinculin autoimmunity model is elegant and well-supported by Pimentel's animal and human data. It is the most mechanistically complete explanation for IBS ever proposed and explains the epidemiological link between food poisoning and IBS that had been observed for decades without a mechanism. The model's weakness is that it has not been independently replicated at scale, and the blood test is not yet in widespread clinical use. If validated by independent groups, this would be Nobel Prize-level work in gastroenterology.
