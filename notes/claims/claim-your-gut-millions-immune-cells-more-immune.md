# pimentel-ch2-4: The gut is the body's largest immune organ, and GALT/Peyer's patches form the intestinal immune surveillance system

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch2 Gut Anatomy and MMC]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 2

## The Claim

Your gut has millions of immune cells -- more immune cells than anywhere else in your body. The intestinal immune system encounters more antigens -- toxins or other foreign substances that induce an immune response -- than any other part of the body. That's because you're bringing the outside world into your body by eating.

## Evidence

Standard immunology. The epithelial barrier consists of a single cell layer with tight junctions that "only allow microorganisms to enter through them and not in-between them."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for the immunological anatomy. The "leaky gut" concept (disrupted tight junctions) is more debated but mechanistically plausible.

## Stakes

The gut's immune primacy explains why food poisoning can trigger autoimmunity against gut structures (anti-vinculin antibodies, Chapter 4). If the gut contains the body's largest immune cell population, an infection there can produce systemic autoimmune consequences.
