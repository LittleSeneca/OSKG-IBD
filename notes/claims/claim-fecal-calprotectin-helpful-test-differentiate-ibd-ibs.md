# acg-cd2018-3: Fecal calprotectin is a helpful test to differentiate IBD from IBS (R1)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Fecal calprotectin is a helpful test to differentiate IBD from IBS (R1)

## Evidence

Fecal calprotectin (a calcium-binding protein derived from neutrophils) and fecal lactoferrin serve as noninvasive markers of intestinal inflammation. Multiple studies demonstrate utility in differentiating IBD from IBS (references 38-42). Additional uses: monitoring postoperative recurrence (calprotectin >100 μg/g indicates endoscopic recurrence with ~89% sensitivity), predicting relapse after anti-TNF withdrawal (calprotectin >160 μg/g: sensitivity 91.7%, specificity 82.9%).

**Initial laboratory evaluation (Summary Statements 19-20):** Should include evaluation for inflammation (CRP, ESR), anemia, dehydration, malnutrition. Stool testing should include fecal pathogens, *C. difficile* testing, and may include fecal calprotectin. CRP has a short half-life (19h) making it useful for monitoring; however, up to 40% of IBD patients with mild inflammation may have normal CRP/ESR.

**Serologic and genetic testing (Summary Statements 21-23):** Routine use of serologic markers (anti-glycan antibodies, etc.) for diagnosis is not indicated (low sensitivity). Genetic testing is not indicated for diagnosis — >200 IBD-associated loci and >71 CD susceptibility loci exist (NOD2, IL23R, ATG16L1), but no single variant has high enough frequency for diagnostic utility. NOD2 variants predict complicated disease behavior (ileal involvement, stenosis, penetrating disease, need for surgery) but testing remains a research tool.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G6 (AGA 2021) also endorses fecal calprotectin for diagnosis and monitoring. G8 (ECCO 2020) provides more detailed cut-off recommendations. The ACG guideline is notably restrained on genetic/serologic testing — even more so than G6.

## Assessment

This is one of the few "strong, moderate" recommendations in the diagnostic section. The high sensitivity/specificity for IBD vs. IBS differentiation makes fecal calprotectin a high-value, low-cost triage tool. The guideline appropriately cautions against over-reliance on serologic and genetic panels that are commercially marketed but lack clinical validation for diagnosis.
