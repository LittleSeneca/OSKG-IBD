# pimentel-ch5-5: The 2020 terminology change from "methane-dominant SIBO" to "Intestinal Methanogen Overgrowth (IMO)" is scientifically necessary because methane producers are archaea, not bacteria

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch5 SIBO Definition and Diagnosis]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 5

## The Claim

Methane production involves "Methanobrevibacter smithii (M. smithii for short), which is not bacteria but rather archaea." The diagnostic criterion: "If at any point over two hours your methane level exceeds 10 ppm, this is considered positive for methane and you are diagnosed with what is now called intestinal methanogen overgrowth (IMO)."

## Evidence

The three-step pathway: sugar → hydrogen-producing bacteria (E. coli, Klebsiella) → hydrogen → methanogenic archaea (M. smithii) → methane. Methanogens are archaea, a distinct domain of life separate from bacteria. Despite the name change, the clinical presentation (constipation, bloating) and treatment implications remain similar.

## Stakes

The IMO terminology resolves a semantic inaccuracy that had persisted for years. It also has practical implications: antibiotics that target bacterial cell walls (e.g., penicillins) will not affect archaea, which have different cell wall structures. Rifaximin, which has broad activity, may have different efficacy against IMO than against hydrogen SIBO.

## Assessment

The IMO renaming is a scientifically necessary correction that Pimentel's group championed. It reflects the growing recognition that the gut microbiome includes archaea and fungi, not just bacteria. Clinically, the key distinction is that IMO may require different antimicrobial approaches (e.g., neomycin or metronidazole added to rifaximin) than hydrogen-predominant SIBO.
