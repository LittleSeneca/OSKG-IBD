# yamada-surg65-1: Up to 80% of CD patients still require surgery despite biologic therapy; early laparoscopic resection may be a valid alternative to biologic escalation

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch65 Surgical Treatment of IBD]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 65

## The Claim

Although biologics have largely replaced corticosteroids as the cornerstone of medical therapy, the natural history of the disease has not been significantly altered, with up to 80% of patients still requiring surgical intervention at some point in their disease course because of primary and secondary loss of response to biologic medication.

## Evidence

Cited references [1,2] for the 80% surgical rate. Reference [19] for the RCT on early laparoscopic resection vs biologic escalation. The authors note that 90% of CD patients have endoscopic recurrence within 1 year of surgery and up to 35% require repeat resection by 10 years.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH for the 80% surgical rate (these are pre-biologic and early biologic era estimates; modern rates are lower). HIGH for the early laparoscopic resection RCT evidence. HIGH for the recurrence

## Stakes

The claim that biologics haven't "significantly altered" the natural history of CD with respect to surgery is provocative but supported by population-level data showing surgical rates have declined but not collapsed. The early laparoscopic resection option challenges the default of escalating biologics in limited ileocecal disease.

## Assessment

This is a surgical perspective that usefully complicates the medical narrative. The RCT supporting early laparoscopic resection for limited ileocecal CD is an important counterpoint to the "biologics first" approach. The surgical recurrence data (90% endoscopic at 1 year) underscores that surgery is not curative and postoperative prophylaxis is essential.
