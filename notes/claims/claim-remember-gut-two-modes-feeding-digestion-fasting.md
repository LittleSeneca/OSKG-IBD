# pimentel-ch2-1: The gut operates in two modes -- feeding (digestion) and fasting (cleaning via MMC) -- and SIBO results from failure of the fasting-mode cleaning function

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch2 Gut Anatomy and MMC]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 2

## The Claim

Remember, the gut has two modes: feeding (digestion) and fasting (cleaning). Two to four hours after you eat and digest a meal, the feeding mode turns off and fasting mode begins... During the fasting mode, waves of electrical activity induce the gut to sweep residual undigested material through the digestive tract. This 'housekeeping' role is known as the migrating motor complex.

## Evidence

The MMC mechanism is well-characterized in the gastrointestinal physiology literature. Pimentel cites standard GI physiology: phase III of the MMC is the "housekeeper" phase, occurring every 90-120 minutes during fasting, lasting 5-8 minutes, and originating in the stomach or proximal small intestine. The feeding/fasting mode switch is vagus-nerve mediated.

## Stakes

The MMC model is the mechanistic foundation for the entire SIBO treatment paradigm. If MMC impairment causes SIBO, then treatments should (a) reduce bacterial load (antibiotics), (b) restore MMC function (prokinetics), and (c) avoid suppressing MMC (meal spacing, no snacking). Every dietary and pharmacological recommendation in the book traces back to this mechanism.

## Assessment

The MMC model is elegant and well-supported. Its strength is that it provides a unified mechanism for disparate SIBO risk factors: gastroenteritis (anti-vinculin antibodies damage the interstitial cells of Cajal that pace MMC), opioid use (slows gut transit), diabetes (autonomic neuropathy impairs MMC), and anatomic abnormalities (adhesions, strictures physically obstruct clearance). The model explains why SIBO recurs after antibiotics (MMC remains impaired) and why prokinetics reduce recurrence.
