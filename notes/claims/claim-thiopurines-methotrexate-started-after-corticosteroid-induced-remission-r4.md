# acg-cd2018-21: Thiopurines or methotrexate should be started after corticosteroid-induced remission (R44-R45)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Thiopurines or methotrexate should be started after corticosteroid-induced remission (R44-R45)

## Evidence

Three scenarios for thiopurine use after steroid induction: (1) at first corticosteroid course, (2) after repeated courses/steroid dependence, (3) as concomitant medication with anti-TNF. Scenario 1 is supported by pediatric data (Markowitz 2000) but an adult RCT (Cosnes 2013) could not demonstrate benefit in time in clinical remission. Scenario 2 (steroid-dependent patients) has the strongest evidence. Scenario 3 (combination with anti-TNF) is addressed in R50.

## Assessment

The steroid-sparing imperative is clear: steroids work fast but are toxic, and the goal is to get patients off steroids and onto a sustainable maintenance strategy. The ambiguity about scenario 1 (start thiopurine at first steroid course in adults) reflects the Cosnes 2013 trial and the emerging evidence that early biologic therapy may outperform early thiopurine therapy. This is an area where G6 (AGA 2021) will likely have a more definitive position.
