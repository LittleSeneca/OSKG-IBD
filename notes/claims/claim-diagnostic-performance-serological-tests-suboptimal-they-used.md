# yamada-ucdx63-8: Serological markers (pANCA, ASCA) have suboptimal diagnostic performance and should NOT be used to screen for or diagnose UC

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch63 Ulcerative Colitis Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

The diagnostic performance of these serological tests is suboptimal and they should not used to screen for, or diagnose, ulcerative colitis (or Crohn's disease).

## Evidence

pANCA is present in 50-80% of UC patients but also in up to 31% of CD patients (especially colonic CD with UC-like endoscopic appearance). ASCA is present in 20% of UC patients. Antinuclear antibodies present in 40-50% of UC but also in healthy subjects and other autoimmune diseases. The combination of pANCA+/ASCA- may help distinguish UC from CD or indeterminate colitis, but "this is rarely necessary in clinical practice." Antimicrobial antibody panels may have prognostic value in CD but prognostic utility in UC "has not been examined."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. This is consistent with every major guideline. The chapter is explicit that serology should NOT be used diagnostically.

## Stakes

Commercial IBD serology panels (Prometheus, etc.) are still marketed and used. Patients with positive pANCA are sometimes labeled as having UC on the basis of serology alone -- this is incorrect and potentially harmful. The chapter's clear language ("should not be used to screen for, or diagnose") is a necessary corrective.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some clinicians use serology in the setting of indeterminate colitis to guide surgical decision-making (pANCA+/ASCA- favors UC and thus IPAA surgery; ASCA+/pANCA- favors CD and thus cautions against IPAA). The chapter acknowledges this limited role.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

This is one of the most practically important claims in the chapter. Serology for IBD diagnosis is a persistent source of misuse in community practice. The chapter's unambiguous recommendation against diagnostic use is well-supported by the evidence.

**Strongest section:** The integrated diagnostic framework (Claims 1-5). The chapter builds from clinical presentation through laboratory testing to endoscopy and histology in a logical sequence, with quantitative performance characteristics for each diagnostic tool. The calprotectin threshold data is particularly well-presented with clinical decision-making implications.

**Weakest section:** The differential diagnosis (Claim 7) is comprehensive but necessarily shallow for each entity. A reader trying to distinguish ischemic colitis from UC based on this chapter alone would need to consult additional sources for the ischemic colitis diagnostic criteria.

**Cross-reference opportunities:** The endoscopic features in Table 63.1 should be cross-referenced with Ch 64 (CD clinical, which covers the CD-specific endoscopic features in more detail). The fecal calprotectin data connects to the clinical guidelines (AGA, ACG, ECCO) which provide more specific cut-point recommendations. The histological remission data (63% risk reduction) is an emerging concept that will likely feature in future treatment guidelines.
