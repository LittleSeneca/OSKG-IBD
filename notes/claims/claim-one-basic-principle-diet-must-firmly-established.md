# gottschall-ch910-5: The SCD food classification is based on carbohydrate structure: monosaccharides allowed, most disaccharides and polysaccharides forbidden

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch9-10 Implementing the SCD]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 9-10

## The Claim

One basic principle of the diet must be firmly established: no food should be ingested that contains carbohydrates other than those found in fruits, honey, properly-prepared yoghurt, and those vegetables and nuts listed.

## Evidence

The complete food lists (summarized below). The classification logic is: monosaccharide sources (honey, fruit, properly fermented dairy) = allowed; disaccharide sources (milk, table sugar, most processed foods) = forbidden; polysaccharide sources (grains, potatoes, corn, rice) = forbidden, with exceptions for properly prepared legumes.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low
**Rationale:** LOW for the classification system as a whole. While the monosaccharide/disaccharide distinction is biochemically clear, the SCD's specific food classifications don't always follow it. For example:

## Stakes

The food lists ARE the SCD. The theory may be elegant, but the practical diet is defined by what patients can and cannot eat. The exceptions and inconsistencies reveal that SCD is more empirical than its theoretical framing suggests.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Low-FODMAP classification (Monash University) uses direct fermentation testing of individual foods, producing different classifications. Many foods forbidden on SCD are allowed on low-FODMAP (e.g., oats, rice, some grains in small portions). Many foods allowed on SCD are high-FODMAP (e.g., honey, apples, some legumes).

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

The SCD food lists are a mix of biochemical reasoning, historical precedent (Haas's original diet), and clinical experience. They are internally consistent with the SCD's monosaccharide principle if you accept its premises about which foods contain which carbohydrates. The practical problem is that the lists are static (1994) while food manufacturing has changed dramatically. Many modern processed foods require label-reading detective work that Gottschall's appendix cannot cover.

## SCD Food Lists (from Ch10)

### Allowed Proteins
Fresh/frozen beef, lamb, pork, poultry, fish (including shellfish), eggs, natural cheeses (see Appendix list), homemade yoghurt (24hr fermentation), dry curd cottage cheese, canned fish in oil or water.

### Forbidden Proteins
Processed meats (hot dogs, bologna, turkey loaf, spiced ham), breaded fish, canned fish with sauces, processed cheeses, smoked meats (unless verified sugar-free), canned meats of any kind. Most processed meats contain starch, whey powder, lactose, or sucrose.

### Allowed Vegetables (fresh or frozen, no added sugar/starch)
Artichoke (French), asparagus, beets, dried white (navy) beans/lentils/split peas (soaked 10-12 hrs), broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, carrots, celery, cucumbers, eggplant, garlic, kale, lettuce, lima beans (dried and fresh), mushrooms, onions, parsley, peas, peppers (all colors), pumpkin, spinach, squash (summer and winter), string beans, tomatoes, turnips (after improvement), watercress.

### Forbidden Vegetables/Grains
All grains (wheat, barley, corn, rye, oats, rice, buckwheat, millet, triticale, bulgur, spelt) and products made from them. Potatoes (white and sweet), yams, parsnips, okra, chick peas, bean sprouts, soybeans, mungbeans, fava beans, garbanzo beans. Amaranth, quinoa, cottonseed. Wheat germ. Seaweeds. Couscous and other grain-like ingredients.

### Allowed Fruits
Fresh, raw/cooked, frozen (no added sugar), dried. Canned fruits "packed in own juice" only. Apples, avocados, apricots, ripe bananas (brown-spotted, no green tips), all berries, cherries, fresh/unsweetened coconut, loose California dates only, grapefruit, grapes, kiwi, kumquats, lemons, limes, mangoes, melons, nectarines, oranges, papayas, peaches, pears, pineapple, prunes, raisins (preferably dark), rhubarb, tangerines.

### Allowed Nuts
Almonds, pecans, Brazil nuts, filberts (hazelnuts), walnuts, unroasted cashews, boiled chestnuts. Peanut butter (no additives). Roasted peanuts in shell (after 6 months, when diarrhea gone). Nuts must be finely ground ("nut flour") until diarrhea clears.

### Allowed Beverages
Tomato juice (canned, salt only). Fresh-squeezed orange/grapefruit juice (not from concentrate; avoid orange juice in morning during active diarrhea). Grape juice (bottled, no added sugar). Pineapple juice (no added sugar). Apple cider (verify no added sweetener). Fresh vegetable juices (allowed vegetables only). Weak tea or very weak coffee (no milk/cream). Peppermint/spearmint herb teas. Milkshakes with homemade yoghurt. Very dry wine. Occasionally gin, rye, Scotch, bourbon, vodka (no sherry, cordials, liqueurs, brandy).

### Forbidden Beverages
Fluid milk of any kind. Dried milk solids. Commercial acidophilus milk, buttermilk, sour cream, yoghurt. Soybean milk. Instant tea/coffee. Coffee substitutes (most contain malt). Postum. Beer.

### Key Additional Rules
- NO grains, cereals, bread, pasta of any kind, ever.
- NO white/brown sugar, molasses, corn syrup, maple syrup.
- NO chocolate or carob.
- NO cornstarch, arrowroot, tapioca, sago, or any starch thickener.
- NO baking powder (use baking soda).
- NO agar-agar, carrageenan, or pectin.
- NO canned vegetables (added sugar/starch).
- NO commercial ketchup (~40% sugar).
- NO ice cream unless homemade (commercial is high lactose/sucrose).
- NO FOS (fructooligosaccharides/inulin) -- "may feed harmful bacteria as well as 'friendly' bacteria."
- Butter, not margarine. Cultured butter recommended.
- Olive oil highly recommended. Corn and soybean oils permitted.
- Saccharin permitted (other artificial sweeteners avoided).
- Diet soft drinks: saccharin-sweetened OK (2-3/week); aspartame-sweetened limit to 1/week (may contain lactose).
- Homemade yoghurt MUST ferment minimum 24 hours to eliminate lactose.

**Strongest section:** The Ch10 food lists -- they are specific, actionable, and have been the practical backbone of the SCD for 30 years.

**Weakest section:** The recovery timeline claims -- completely unevidenced, arbitrary durations presented as requirements.

**Cross-reference note -- SCD vs. low-FODMAP overlap and divergence:**

| Category | SCD | Low-FODMAP | Note |
|----------|-----|------------|------|
| Grains | ALL forbidden | Some allowed (oats, rice, quinoa in portions) | Major divergence |
| Dairy | 24hr yoghurt OK; no fluid milk | Lactose-free OK; hard cheeses OK | SCD stricter on dairy type, low-FODMAP on lactose content |
| Honey | Allowed (primary sweetener) | High-FODMAP, restricted | Major divergence -- opposite classifications |
| Legumes | Some allowed after soaking | Most restricted (GOS content) | SCD more permissive |
| Apples | Allowed | High-FODMAP (polyols, excess fructose) | SCD more permissive |
| Garlic/Onion | Allowed | High-FODMAP, restricted | SCD more permissive |
| Potatoes | Forbidden | Allowed | Opposite classifications |
| White sugar | Forbidden (sucrose) | Forbidden during elimination | Same practical outcome, different rationale |

The SCD and low-FODMAP diets converge on eliminating fermentable carbohydrates but arrive at substantially different food lists because they use different classification systems: SCD uses chemical structure (mono/di/poly), low-FODMAP uses direct fermentation measurement. A patient following both simultaneously faces severe restriction. The SIBO Specific Food Guide (Siebecker, in Sarna 2021) attempts to harmonize them.
