# pimentel-ch5-4: Lactulose breath testing is superior to glucose breath testing for SIBO diagnosis because it assesses the full length of the small intestine

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch5 SIBO Definition and Diagnosis]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 5

## The Claim

Glucose is very easy for humans to digest... the body is designed to absorb glucose in the first few feet of the small intestine, but it's absorbed so fast that any bacteria below four feet in the small intestine won't receive any glucose. If you have bacterial overgrowth below those four feet of small intestine (which, remember, is 20 feet long), we won't be able to assess three-quarters of the small intestine using glucose in the breath test. Hence, we believe lactulose is a better substrate for breath testing.

## Evidence

Physiological argument: glucose is absorbed in the proximal small intestine; lactulose is non-absorbable and therefore available to bacteria throughout the small intestine. The 90-minute cutoff for hydrogen rise: "If your hydrogen levels rise by 20 parts per million (ppm) or more before or at 90 minutes, then we consider this gas derives from excess bacteria in the small intestine... We have shown that the 90-minute cutoff correlates directly with the small bowel microbiome."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM. The glucose vs. lactulose debate is one of the most contentious in SIBO diagnosis. Glucose breath testing has higher specificity (less false positives from rapid transit) but lower sensitivity

## Stakes

If lactulose is truly superior (higher sensitivity, acceptable specificity), then glucose breath testing should be abandoned and the higher SIBO prevalence figures (60-70%) are correct. If glucose is superior (higher specificity, acceptable sensitivity), then lactulose overdiagnoses SIBO and the true prevalence is lower.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Many academic gastroenterologists prefer glucose breath testing for its higher specificity. The North American Consensus on breath testing (2017) acknowledged both substrates as acceptable. The debate reflects a deeper tension: is it worse to miss SIBO (false negative with glucose) or to overdiagnose it (false positive with lactulose)?

## Assessment

Pimentel's argument for lactulose is physiologically sound -- distal SIBO is common, and glucose simply can't reach it. The specificity concern (false positives from rapid transit) is real but may be overstated in clinical practice. The 90-minute cutoff is designed to minimize colonic fermentation, and dual-peak patterns (early rise, fall, late colonic rise) can help distinguish small intestinal from colonic gas production. For clinical purposes, lactulose's higher sensitivity is preferable: missing SIBO is worse than a false positive that leads to a treatment trial.
