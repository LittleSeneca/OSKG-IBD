# acg-sibo2020-2: Bloating is the most common symptom of SIBO; vitamin deficiencies are uncommon except in structural/surgical cases

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

Key Concept 1. "The most common symptom of SIBO is bloating." Key Concept 2: "Vitamin deficiencies in SIBO are not common and are usually seen in patients with an iatrogenic or structural abnormality of the bowel such as blind loop syndrome." Note: folate may be ELEVATED in SIBO as bacteria produce folate.

## Evidence

**GRADE rating:** Key concept (expert consensus, not GRADEable).

Abdominal pain, bloating, gas, distension, flatulence, and diarrhea are prevalent in >2/3 of patients (references 19-21). Severe cases with steatorrhea, weight loss, anemia, and fat-soluble vitamin deficiencies are associated with extraordinary causes (postsurgical blind loop, scleroderma). The cited study by Jacobs et al. found no difference in symptom intensity, frequency, or duration between culture-positive and culture-negative patients undergoing antroduodenal manometry -- underscoring that symptoms alone cannot distinguish SIBO from other conditions.

## Assessment

The elevation of folate in SIBO is a clinically useful pearl -- if a patient with bloating has HIGH serum folate (without supplementation), SIBO should be on the differential. The fact that even culture-positive vs culture-negative patients have indistinguishable symptoms is sobering and supports the guideline's emphasis on objective testing before treatment.
