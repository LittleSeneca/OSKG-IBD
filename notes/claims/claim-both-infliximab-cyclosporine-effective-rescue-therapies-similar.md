# acg-uc2019-15: Both infliximab and cyclosporine are effective rescue therapies with similar outcomes; choice depends on provider experience and patient-specific factors

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Both infliximab and cyclosporine are effective rescue therapies with similar outcomes; choice depends on provider experience and patient-specific factors

## Evidence

The CYSIF trial (Laharie 2012, n=115): treatment failure at day 98 was 60% (cyclosporine) vs 54% (infliximab), non-significant difference. Long-term follow-up: 5-year colectomy-free survival 61.5% (cyclosporine) vs 65.1% (infliximab), P=0.97. CONSTRUCT trial (n=270): no differences in quality-adjusted survival, colectomy, time to colectomy, or adverse events. ENEIDA registry (n=740): similar efficacy; lower adverse events with cyclosporine. Cyclosporine dose: 2 mg/kg (not 4 mg/kg -- similar efficacy, fewer adverse events). Target levels 200-400 ng/mL. Infliximab in ASUC: RCT (Jarnerot 2005) showed 29% vs 67% colectomy at 3 months (P=0.017). Accelerated infliximab dosing may improve short-term colectomy rates but not long-term outcomes. Post-hoc analysis: infliximab favored in patients with albumin <23 g/L.

**Key Concept 41:** "Infliximab and cyclosporine do not increase postoperative complications of colectomy, and surgery should not be deferred based on this exposure."

**Evidence summary (cont.):** Sequential salvage (cyclosporine → infliximab or vice versa): colectomy rate ~60% at 12 months, severe adverse events in 16% including 1 death. Calcineurin inhibitor + vedolizumab bridge: case series of 11 patients, 55% steroid-free remission at week 14, 45% at 1 year.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 (AGA 2020) is less prescriptive, recommending either infliximab or cyclosporine without preference. G9 (ECCO 2022) provides the most detailed rescue therapy algorithm, including considerations for accelerated infliximab dosing and the calcineurin-vedolizumab bridge.

## Assessment

The CYSIF and CONSTRUCT trials provide the best head-to-head data in all of UC guideline-land and justify the equipoise between infliximab and cyclosporine. However, real-world practice heavily favors infliximab due to provider familiarity and the simplicity of continuing it as maintenance. Cyclosporine use is concentrated at a handful of expert centers. The guideline's advice to choose based on "provider experience" is pragmatic but effectively means most US gastroenterologists will use infliximab. The calcineurin-vedolizumab bridge data (KC 46, R46) is the most innovative ASUC strategy -- using cyclosporine or tacrolimus for rapid induction while vedolizumab's slower onset takes over -- but the evidence is limited to small case series.
