# ecco-uc2022-8: Systemic corticosteroids (prednisolone) are strongly recommended for induction despite very low-quality evidence — a paradox explained by clinical experience, large effect size, and limited alternatives at the time of original trials

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Systemic corticosteroids (prednisolone) are strongly recommended for induction despite very low-quality evidence — a paradox explained by clinical experience, large effect size, and limited alternatives at the time of original trials

## Evidence

Only two placebo-controlled RCTs met the panel's PICO criteria (Truelove & Horler 1955, Lennard-Jones 1960), yielding RR 2.83 (1.79-4.46) for clinical remission. Quality downgraded for risk of bias, indirectness, and imprecision. No AE data from these trials. The guideline emphasizes: corticosteroids have no maintenance role, courses should be limited to 3 months maximum, and a corticosteroid-sparing agent should be initiated for any patient requiring >1 course per year or flaring during taper. Corticosteroid-free remission is the desired outcome given irreversible side effects of prolonged exposure.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019, R11) also makes a strong recommendation with low quality evidence. G5 (AGA 2020) concurs. The 3-month maximum and "one course per year" trigger for steroid-sparing therapy are consistent across all guidelines.

## Assessment

The strong/very-low paradox is more pronounced in ECCO than ACG because ECCO's methodologists were stricter about evidence quality. The real message is in the qualifying text, not the recommendation: steroids are a bridge to something else, never a destination. The explicit "3 months maximum" and ">1 course per year = escalate" guidance is more operationally useful than the GRADE rating.
