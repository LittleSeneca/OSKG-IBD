# gottschall-ch910-2: The SCD has a staged introduction: basic diet (severe symptoms) → gradual vegetable/fruit addition → legumes at 3 months

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch9-10 Implementing the SCD]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 9-10

## The Claim

The initial basic diet for 1-5 days during severe diarrhea and cramping consists of: dry curd cottage cheese with homemade yoghurt, eggs, diluted apple cider or grape juice, homemade gelatin, homemade chicken soup with pureed carrots, broiled beef patty or fish, and cheese cake (custard consistency, no lemon). As diarrhea subsides: add cooked peeled fruits, ripe banana, additional vegetables (not cabbage family until diarrhea substantially subsided). Dried legumes "may be added cautiously after being on the diet for about three months."

## Evidence

Clinical experience. The staging is based on tolerability testing: fruits and raw vegetables are laxative and should be avoided during active diarrhea; cabbage family vegetables are gas-producing; legumes require soaking and partial digestion.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM. The staging logic is clinically sensible: start with the most easily digested foods, add complexity as the gut heals. But there is no controlled evidence that this specific staging is optimal 

## Stakes

The staged introduction is the practical bridge between the SCD theory and its implementation. Without clear staging guidance, patients might introduce foods in the wrong order and attribute the resulting symptoms to diet failure rather than staging error.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The GAPS protocol (Campbell-McBride) uses a more elaborate six-stage introduction. Crohn's Disease Cookbook (Foote 2020) has a 2-week meal plan. There is no consensus on optimal staging.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

The staging is rational but underspecified. Gottschall provides principles (cook fruits, avoid raw during diarrhea, introduce one food at a time) but leaves the details to individual tolerance testing. This is wise -- IBD is heterogeneous, and no single staging protocol will work for everyone. The weakness is the lack of structured reintroduction guidance for after remission, which Thompson (IBD Remission Diet) provides more systematically.
