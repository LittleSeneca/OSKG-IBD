# ecco-uc2022-2: The ultimate treatment goal is health-related quality of life and avoidance of disability — endoscopic healing is the key intermediate target, with histologic healing emerging

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

The ultimate goal of treatment in UC is to maintain health-related quality of life [QoL] and avoid disability. To achieve this, it is important to not only provide rapid relief of clinical symptoms, but also achieve endoscopic healing where possible, as this is associated with improved long-term outcomes.

## Evidence

The endorsement of STRIDE-II (Turner et al., 2021) is notable — this is the 2021 update that elevated histologic healing from "not a target" to "an adjunctive measure of remission," a significant evolution from STRIDE-I (2015) which the ACG 2019 guideline cited. The panel selected endoscopic and clinical outcomes as being of "critical importance" in their GRADE process.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019) set mucosal healing (Mayo 0 or 1) as the treatment target but explicitly rejected histologic healing as a validated endpoint. G5 (AGA 2020) aligned with ACG. ECCO is the only one of these three to reference STRIDE-II and its histologic healing upgrade, reflecting the guideline's later publication date.

## Assessment

The reference to STRIDE-II is an important temporal advantage — this guideline could incorporate evidence that was unavailable when ACG and AGA were written. However, the guideline doesn't make histologic healing a formal recommendation; it simply notes the shift. The gap between "endoscopic healing is the target" and "histologic healing is emerging" will likely close in the next ECCO update.
