# pimentel-ch1-1: SIBO is present in approximately 75% of IBS patients

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Intro Ch1 IBS and SIBO Overlap]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Intro + Ch1

## The Claim

Through our research, we have found that about three-quarters of patients -- as many as 30 million Americans -- who have met the diagnostic criteria for IBS have taken a breath test that suggests they have SIBO.

## Evidence

The authors cite their own research at Cedars-Sinai over 20 years. They reference their first book (Pimentel 2006) and note "many new discoveries about the bacterial composition of the small intestine, the autoimmune cause of SIBO, and the treatment for SIBO" since then. Specific supporting data: the breath test data from their MAST Program patient population. They note that in 1999 "the connection between IBS and SIBO was officially established."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Pimentel's estimate of 60-70% SIBO prevalence in IBS is supported by multiple independent meta-analyses (ACG 2020 guideline cites 4-78% range, with the higher figures from studies using j

## Stakes

If SIBO causes IBS in the majority of patients, then treating SIBO should resolve IBS in those patients. This reframes IBS from a chronic, idiopathic condition to a treatable bacterial overgrowth disorder. The 75% claim is the book's central thesis and drives all subsequent treatment recommendations.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some gastroenterologists argue SIBO prevalence in IBS is overestimated due to limitations of lactulose breath testing (high false positive rate from rapid transit). The Rome Foundation's 2017 working team report on SIBO was more cautious, noting the heterogeneity of study methodologies. Some argue that breath test abnormalities in IBS may reflect dysbiosis or rapid transit rather than true SIBO.

**Alternative reading:** SIBO may be one of several IBS subtypes rather than the majority cause. The 75% figure may represent selection bias at a tertiary referral center. The causal direction (SIBO causes IBS vs. IBS creates conditions favorable to SIBO) remains debated.

## Assessment

Pimentel's 75% claim is on the high end of published estimates but reflects the largest single-institution dataset. The meta-analytic range (4-78%) reflects methodological heterogeneity more than genuine uncertainty. Pimentel's insistence on glucose breath testing (which he argues is more specific) and his lab's standardized technique may produce higher detection rates. The ACG 2020 guideline supports the IBS-SIBO connection as clinically important even while acknowledging the prevalence uncertainty.
