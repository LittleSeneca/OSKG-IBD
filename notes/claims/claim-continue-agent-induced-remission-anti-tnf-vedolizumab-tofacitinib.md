# ecco-uc2022-13: Continue the agent that induced remission; anti-TNF, vedolizumab, tofacitinib, and ustekinumab all receive strong maintenance recommendations; there is no role for 5-ASA or methotrexate in maintenance after advanced therapy

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Continue the agent that induced remission; anti-TNF, vedolizumab, tofacitinib, and ustekinumab all receive strong maintenance recommendations; there is no role for 5-ASA or methotrexate in maintenance after advanced therapy

## Evidence

Anti-TNF maintenance meta-analysis (10 RCTs): clinical remission RR 1.98 (1.60-2.45), steroid-free clinical remission RR 2.86 (1.67-4.90), sustained clinical remission RR 2.76 (1.78-4.28), QoL improvement RR 1.71 (1.27-2.32). This is the only "high-quality" evidence rating for an advanced therapy in the guideline. Vedolizumab maintenance (3 RCTs, n=441, 52-60 weeks): clinical remission RR 2.37 (1.74-3.23), sustained clinical remission 20.7% vs 9.4% (RR 2.16; 1.34-3.50). Subcutaneous vedolizumab maintenance showed similar efficacy to IV (Sandborn 2020). Tofacitinib maintenance (OCTAVE Sustain, n=593): clinical remission 5 mg BID or 10 mg BID superior to placebo (RR 3.37; 2.23-5.10), endoscopic remission RR 3.88 (1.90-7.95). Ustekinumab maintenance (UNIFI): 90 mg SC q8w — clinical remission RR 1.82 (1.33-2.49), steroid-free remission RR 1.79 (1.30-2.47), endoscopic remission RR 1.79 (1.36-2.36). 90 mg SC q12w also superior to placebo, but numerically lower than q8w, especially in biologic-refractory patients. No guideline addresses concomitant 5-ASA with advanced therapy — this question was not formulated as a PICO.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019) makes equivalent strong maintenance recommendations for anti-TNF, vedolizumab, and tofacitinib (R34-36). ACG additionally recommends against concomitant 5-ASA with biologics (R30, conditional) and against methotrexate for maintenance (R33, conditional) — recommendations ECCO doesn't make, likely because these weren't prioritized as PICO questions. The ECCO maintenance recommendations benefit from including ustekinumab and the vedolizumab SC data.

## Assessment

The universal "continue what induced remission" principle is consistent across guidelines and makes clinical sense. The more interesting question — what to do when the induction agent loses efficacy — is addressed only indirectly through the drug monitoring discussion. The guideline's silence on concomitant 5-ASA with biologics contrasts with ACG's explicit recommendation against it, but this reflects the PICO-driven structure rather than a difference in clinical opinion.
