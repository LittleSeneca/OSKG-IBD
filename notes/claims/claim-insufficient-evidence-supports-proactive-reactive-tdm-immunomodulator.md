# ecco-cd2020-9: Insufficient evidence supports proactive or reactive TDM; immunomodulator withdrawal after achieving remission on combination therapy is suggested (R2.8-R2.9, R2.11-R2.13)

**Source:** [[ECCO Crohn's Medical 2020 - Torres]] — Torres et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Crohn's Disease: Medical Management (2020)

## The Claim

Insufficient evidence supports proactive or reactive TDM; immunomodulator withdrawal after achieving remission on combination therapy is suggested (R2.8-R2.9, R2.11-R2.13)

## Evidence

**Proactive TDM (TAXIT, TAILORIX):** Two RCTs found no advantage of proactive TDM over clinically-based dosing. TAXIT (n=273): clinical remission at 1 year — 66% clinically-based vs. 69% TDM-based (no significant difference), though TDM group had fewer relapses (7% vs. 17%; p=0.018). TAILORIX (n=122): no difference in sustained steroid-free clinical remission with mucosal healing across three arms (33% TDM1, 27% TDM2, 40% control; p=0.50).

**Reactive TDM:** Only one RCT (n=69) compared TDM-guided vs. symptom-based dose intensification in secondary IFX failure — no difference in regaining clinical response (57.6% vs. 52.8%; RR 1.09). However, observational data consistently show TDM-guided approaches are more cost-effective (>10% cost savings) and may improve endoscopic outcomes.

**Immunomodulator withdrawal:** Cochrane review of 2 RCTs — same relapse rate with infliximab monotherapy vs. combination therapy (48% vs. 49%; RR 1.02). For adalimumab, meta-analysis of 9 studies: no difference in maintenance of remission (RR 1.01; 95% CI 0.91-1.13) or serious AEs (RR 0.88).

**Anti-TNF withdrawal:** Systematic review of 23 observational studies (n=920): overall relapse rate 44% (95% CI 36-51%). Relapse rates: 38% at 6 months, 40% at 12 months, 49% at >25 months after discontinuation. No controlled studies exist.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
This is the area of greatest methodological divergence from G1. G1 (ACG 2018) explicitly deferred TDM entirely to a separate guideline (AGA 2017). G8 includes formal GRADE recommendations — even though both are "insufficient evidence" statements. The proactive TDM recommendation (R2.8) has **moderate-quality evidence** (higher than expected) because two large RCTs exist — they're just negative. G8's immunomodulator withdrawal recommendations (R2.11-R2.12) have no direct counterpart in G1. G6 (AGA 2021) will likely have stronger TDM guidance.

## Assessment

The TDM recommendations are methodologically honest but clinically unsatisfying. "Insufficient evidence" is the right GRADE conclusion from the TAXIT/TAILORIX data, but it leaves clinicians without guidance on one of the most common management questions. The caveat that reactive TDM may offer cost savings is a pragmatic acknowledgment that, even without clinical outcome superiority, knowing drug levels helps avoid futile dose escalation. R2.11-R2.12 (suggesting monotherapy after achieving remission on combination) directly contradicts the G1 approach of continuing combination therapy — this is a major philosophical divergence with practical implications for lymphoma risk.
