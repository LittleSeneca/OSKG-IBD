# yamada-uc63-6: Reactive therapeutic drug monitoring is recommended for anti-TNF agents in UC; proactive TDM lacks strong evidence

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch63 UC Clinical Manifestations and Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

Current guidelines therefore recommend that drug concentration monitoring only be done reactively when a patient is observed to have active persistent disease despite therapy to assess for whether they have adequate drug exposure and are a true nonresponder, and/or if they have developed antidrug antibodies as a source of nonresponse.

## Evidence

The TAXIT trial (n=263 IBD patients, 85 UC): drug concentration-based management achieved similar remission rates to clinically-based management (69% vs 66%) but lower relapse rates (7% vs 17%, p=0.018). However, a subsequent Crohn's trial comparing symptom-based, biomarker-based, and drug concentration-based dosing found no significant difference. The authors conclude "there appears to be a lack of strong evidence in support of proactive therapeutic drug monitoring."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for reactive TDM (assessing drug levels/antibodies when disease is active). MEDIUM for proactive TDM (routine dose adjustment to target levels). The evidence is mixed and guideline recommendation

## Stakes

Reactive TDM prevents inappropriate dose escalation in patients with adequate drug levels and identifies antibody-mediated loss of response that requires a switch in drug class rather than dose increase. Proactive TDM (routine monitoring in well patients) is more controversial -- it could prevent future loss of response but may also lead to unnecessary dose escalation.

## Assessment

This is a nuanced and evidence-based discussion of TDM. The distinction between reactive and proactive TDM is clinically important but often conflated. The authors' conclusion that proactive TDM lacks strong evidence and should not be routine is consistent with current guidelines.
