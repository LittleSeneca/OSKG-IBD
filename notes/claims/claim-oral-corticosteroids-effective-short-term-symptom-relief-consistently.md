# acg-cd2018-12: Oral corticosteroids are effective for short-term symptom relief but do not consistently achieve mucosal healing (R17-R18)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Oral corticosteroids are effective for short-term symptom relief but do not consistently achieve mucosal healing (R17-R18)

## Evidence

Prednisone equivalent doses of 40-60 mg/day (or 1 mg/kg/day) are effective for inducing symptomatic response. Typical taper: maintain initial dose 1-2 weeks, then taper by 5 mg weekly until 20 mg, then 2.5-5 mg weekly; total taper should not exceed 3 months. Despite symptomatic efficacy: ~20% of patients are steroid-refractory, ~33% become steroid-dependent. Corticosteroids are implicated in perforating complications and are relatively contraindicated in fistulizing disease. Nearly half of patients receiving steroids in the pre-biologic era were steroid-dependent, refractory, or required surgery within 1 year.

**Summary Statement 41:** Systemic corticosteroids are ineffective for maintenance therapy. Topical corticosteroids are of limited benefit in CD.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G6 (AGA 2021) and G8 (ECCO 2020) share the view that steroids are a bridge, not a destination. G8 in particular emphasizes early introduction of steroid-sparing agents. The ACG guideline's phrasing "should be used sparingly" (R18) is stronger than its "weak" GRADE rating suggests — the panel clearly wants to reduce steroid exposure.

## Assessment

Corticosteroids remain the fastest-acting treatment for severe symptoms, but they are the clinical equivalent of a payday loan: quick relief with accumulating long-term costs. The 3-month taper limit is a practical benchmark that every clinician should track. The mismatch between symptom relief and mucosal healing is the fundamental problem — this is where the "symptoms ≠ inflammation" principle from the diagnosis section becomes directly actionable.
