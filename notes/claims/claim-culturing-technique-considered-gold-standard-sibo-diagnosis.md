# pimentel-ch5-6: Jejunal aspirate culture -- the traditional "gold standard" -- is unreliable due to contamination and sampling limitations

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch5 SIBO Definition and Diagnosis]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 5

## The Claim

This culturing technique... has been considered the gold standard of SIBO diagnosis. That said, it can't really be the ultimate gold standard, as laboratories have differing culture techniques; furthermore, not all bacteria are culturable. Aspirations are taken from only a few inches of the small intestine, which may not represent the whole small intestine.

## Evidence

Lacy/Mayo Clinic contamination data (20%). Pimentel's own experience with sterile catheter technique. The broader limitations of culture-based methods: many gut bacteria are unculturable; sampling only the proximal small intestine misses distal overgrowth; standard lab processing (treating aspirate like urine culture with >100,000 threshold) is inappropriate.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for the limitations of aspirate culture. The 20% contamination rate is published data. The claim that breath testing correlates with treatment response and is therefore clinically valid is well-s

## Stakes

If the "gold standard" is unreliable, then breath testing -- which is non-invasive, cheaper, and assesses the full small intestine -- may be the superior diagnostic tool despite its own limitations. This inverts the traditional diagnostic hierarchy.

## Assessment

Pimentel's critique of aspirate culture is well-founded. The field has long treated jejunal aspirate as an imperfect gold standard while acknowledging its limitations. The question is whether breath testing is good enough to replace it. Pimentel's argument that breath testing correlates with treatment response is the most clinically relevant standard: a test that predicts who will respond to treatment is more useful than a test that meets an arbitrary microbiological threshold.

**Strongest section:** The three-gas model and IMO terminology. These represent genuine conceptual advances that have practical implications for diagnosis and treatment. The "foxes and wolves" ecological competition model is a useful clinical heuristic.

**Weakest section:** The lactulose vs. glucose debate. Pimentel's argument for lactulose is physiologically reasonable but the field has not reached consensus, and the ACG guideline does not endorse one substrate over the other.

**Historical note:** The 2022 book's SIBO chapter represents a substantial evolution from the 2006 book. In 2006, SIBO was presented as a single entity diagnosed by lactulose breath test and treated with antibiotics. In 2022, SIBO is a family of conditions (hydrogen SIBO, IMO, hydrogen sulfide SIBO) with distinct microbial populations and gas profiles, diagnosed by multi-gas breath testing, and treated with gas-type-specific antibiotic combinations. The three-gas model did not exist in 2006. This is the most important conceptual evolution in Pimentel's thinking between the two books.
