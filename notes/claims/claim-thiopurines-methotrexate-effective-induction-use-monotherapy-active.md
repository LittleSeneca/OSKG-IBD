# acg-uc2019-10: Thiopurines and methotrexate are NOT effective for induction -- do not use as monotherapy in active disease

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Thiopurines and methotrexate are NOT effective for induction -- do not use as monotherapy in active disease

## Evidence

Thiopurines are slow-acting (8-12 weeks to onset) and do not induce remission in active UC. The European METEOR trial: parenteral methotrexate 25 mg/week showed numerically higher steroid-free remission at week 16 but did not reach statistical significance vs placebo. Two meta-analyses of methotrexate induction were negative.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 and G9 both agree: thiopurines and methotrexate are maintenance agents, not induction agents. This is uncontroversial.

## Assessment

This is a "strong" recommendation that should have been stronger in the previous guideline. The persistence of thiopurine monotherapy for active UC in community practice represents guideline nonadherence. The "low quality" rating reflects the small, older trials rather than equipoise about efficacy -- we know these drugs don't work fast enough for active disease.
