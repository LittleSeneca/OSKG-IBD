# aga-cd2021-4: Thiopurines are not recommended for induction but suggested for maintenance; SC/IM methotrexate is suggested; oral methotrexate is recommended against (R3A-D, R4)

**Source:** [[AGA Crohn's 2021 - Feuerstein]] — Feuerstein et al. — AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines: Crohn's Disease (2021)

## The Claim

Thiopurines are not recommended for induction but suggested for maintenance; SC/IM methotrexate is suggested; oral methotrexate is recommended against (R3A-D, R4)

## Evidence

**Recommendation 3A:** "The AGA suggests against the use of thiopurines monotherapy over no treatment for achieving remission." **Conditional, very low certainty.** 5 trials, 380 patients — thiopurines did not increase corticosteroid-free remission vs. placebo. Evidence rated down for serious bias, indirectness, and serious imprecision.

## Assessment

The oral methotrexate recommendation (R3D) is the most practically useful immunomodulator guidance. Many clinicians prescribe oral methotrexate for convenience, unaware that the only RCT used a dose (12.5 mg/week) that may be subtherapeutic. The guideline is honest about this limitation — it's possible that oral MTX 25 mg/week would work, but there are zero data. The separation of induction from maintenance for thiopurines (R3A vs. R3B) highlights the 8-12 week lag to clinical effect — a point ACG 2018 also made but AGA 2021 formalizes with separate GRADE ratings. The "biologic over thiopurine" recommendation (R4) effectively closes the door on thiopurine monotherapy as a first-line strategy for moderate-severe CD.
