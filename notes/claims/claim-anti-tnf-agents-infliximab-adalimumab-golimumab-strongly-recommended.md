# ecco-uc2022-9: Anti-TNF agents (infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab) are strongly recommended for induction; network meta-analyses suggest infliximab superiority, and combination therapy with thiopurines improves outcomes with infliximab

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Anti-TNF agents (infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab) are strongly recommended for induction; network meta-analyses suggest infliximab superiority, and combination therapy with thiopurines improves outcomes with infliximab

## Evidence

Nine RCTs pooled: clinical remission RR 2.23 (1.81-2.76), clinical response RR 1.56 (1.38-1.76), mucosal healing RR 1.49 (1.32-1.68). Safety data from large cohort studies were reassuring. No head-to-head anti-TNF trials exist. Network meta-analyses (Bonovas 2018, Singh 2020): infliximab superior to adalimumab for clinical remission (OR 2.10; 1.21-3.64) and to both adalimumab and golimumab for clinical response and mucosal healing. In patients with prior biologic failure, adalimumab showed markedly lower efficacy (non-significant vs placebo in subgroup analysis). The combination of infliximab + azathioprine is more effective than infliximab alone (UC-SUCCESS trial, Panaccione 2014). For patients with loss of response and anti-drug antibodies, adding a thiopurine when switching to a second anti-TNF reduces immunogenicity (Roblin 2020).

**Key omission:** Unlike ACG 2019 (R24, strong recommendation for IFX + thiopurine combination), ECCO does NOT issue a formal PICO-based combination therapy recommendation. The evidence is discussed in the supporting text but not elevated to a standalone recommendation — a notable structural difference.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019, R22) makes the same strong recommendation with HIGH quality evidence — ECCO's "moderate" is a downgrade from ACG's "high." The difference likely reflects ECCO's more conservative approach to indirectness in the evidence base. G5 (AGA 2020) also strongly recommends anti-TNF therapy. No guideline makes a formal recommendation for one anti-TNF over another, though the network meta-analysis data favoring infliximab is acknowledged by all.

## Assessment

The absence of a formal combination therapy recommendation is the most interesting structural difference from ACG. It's not that ECCO disagrees with combination therapy — the text clearly states IFX + AZA is more effective — but the panel chose not to formulate a PICO question around it. This may reflect the European trend toward vedolizumab monotherapy as first-line biologic (avoiding the combination therapy question entirely) or the panel's focus on questions with direct RCT evidence. The network meta-analysis data favoring infliximab is well-presented and clinically useful, though the guideline appropriately stops short of making a formal ranking recommendation.
