# yamada-cd64-6: Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) is a cost-effective strategy for optimizing biologic therapy

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch64 Crohn's Disease Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

Measurement of drug concentration and anti-drug antibody levels has been shown to be a cost-effective strategy given the ability to optimize treatment and potentially prevent CD-associated complications.

## Evidence

Trough level thresholds: infliximab >=5 ug/mL, adalimumab >=7.5 ug/mL, certolizumab >=20 ug/mL for clinical remission. Loss of response: 13% risk per year for infliximab, 24% per year for adalimumab. Addition of immunomodulator reduces antibody formation. HLA-DQA1*05 allele associated with immunogenicity. Dose escalation strategies: infliximab 5 mg/kg q8wk -> 10 mg/kg q8/6/4wk; adalimumab 40 mg q2wk -> 40 mg weekly.

## Stakes

TDM transforms biologic therapy from empiric ("it stopped working") to mechanistic ("your drug level is subtherapeutic due to antibodies -- let's add an immunomodulator or switch mechanism of action"). This prevents futile dose escalation and identifies patients who need a different drug class.

This section is notably thorough and practical.
