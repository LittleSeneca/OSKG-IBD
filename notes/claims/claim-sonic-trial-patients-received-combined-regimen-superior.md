# yamada-cd64-4: Combination therapy (anti-TNF + thiopurine) is superior to either agent alone for induction and maintenance in CD

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch64 Crohn's Disease Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

In the SONIC trial, "patients who received the combined regimen had superior rates of corticosteroid-free clinical remission at 6 months relative to patients treated with AZA or infliximab alone (57% vs 44% vs 30%)." Furthermore, "patients treated with combination therapy were more likely to achieve mucosal healing." The concomitant use of AZA is "the only clinically modifiable factor definitively associated with an increased likelihood of response to induction and maintenance infliximab therapy."

## Evidence

SONIC trial (n > 500, infliximab + AZA vs infliximab vs AZA). Combotherapy patients had lower anti-infliximab antibodies and higher infliximab drug levels. The COMMIT trial (methotrexate + infliximab vs infliximab monotherapy) did not show superiority for the primary endpoint (treatment failure) but did show lower antibody formation with combotherapy (4% vs 20%, p = 0.01).

## Stakes

Combination therapy improves efficacy but increases immunosuppressive burden (infection risk, lymphoma risk -- particularly HSTCL in young males). The risk-benefit calculus differs by patient age, sex, and disease severity. The boxed warning for HSTCL on anti-TNF labels is a direct consequence of combination therapy in young males.

## Assessment

The authors present the SONIC data as transformative, which it was (2010). Their handling of the risk-benefit tension is appropriately nuanced: "the potential gain of using combination therapy needs to be carefully weighed against the theoretical risk of using two immunosuppressive agents." The COMMIT discussion is honest about the negative primary endpoint while acknowledging the antibody benefit.
