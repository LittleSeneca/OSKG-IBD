# acg-uc2019-14: IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/d or hydrocortisone 100 mg TID-QID for 3-5 days is the standard; nonresponders need rescue therapy or surgery -- do not delay

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/d or hydrocortisone 100 mg TID-QID for 3-5 days is the standard; nonresponders need rescue therapy or surgery -- do not delay

## Evidence

ASUC defined as ≥6 bloody stools/day plus systemic toxicity (Truelove and Witts). Up to 25% of UC patients develop ASUC; 40% require colectomy. IV steroid response rate: 67% (meta-analysis of 1,948 patients). No benefit to doses above methylprednisolone 60 mg/d. Response plateau at 3-5 days; additional response after 7 days unlikely. The Oxford index: >8 stools on day 3 OR 3-8 stools + CRP >45 mg/L predicted colectomy in 85%. UCEIS ≥7 had higher PPV for colectomy than Mayo endoscopic score 3. CMV affects up to one-third of steroid-refractory ASUC; biopsies from ulcer bases with immunohistochemistry are the diagnostic standard. Delayed surgery associated with increased postoperative complications.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 and G9 both endorse the 3-5 day IV steroid window with early surgical consultation. The Oxford index is cited by all. The CMV evaluation recommendation varies slightly -- G9 is more aggressive about empiric ganciclovir in refractory cases.

## Assessment

The ASUC section is the guideline's most protocolized and practically useful. The 3-5 day steroid window with mandatory surgical consultation at day 3 for nonresponders is the single most important clinical decision rule in UC -- missing this window increases postoperative mortality. The strong recommendation for VTE prophylaxis (R37) with low-quality evidence reflects the 2-3 fold increased VTE risk in hospitalized IBD patients, which persists despite active bleeding. The "against TPN for bowel rest" recommendation (R41) is appropriately strong given two negative RCTs.
