# ecco-diag2019-8: Assessment of disease activity by endoscopy and/or imaging is necessary before withdrawal of maintenance therapy; TDM may guide management in loss of response

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

Assessment of disease activity by endoscopy and/or imaging is necessary before withdrawal of maintenance therapy; TDM may guide management in loss of response

## Evidence

Meta-analysis (Torres 2015): ~75% relapse within 5 years after stopping immunomodulator monotherapy. ~50% maintained remission 24 months after anti-TNF discontinuation, decreasing over time. GETAID infliximab withdrawal trial (Louis 2012): absence of MH strongly associated with relapse (HR 2.6). Meta-analysis (Gisbert 2016): 1-year relapse 42% after anti-TNF discontinuation, decreasing to 26% when endoscopic remission required.

**Statement 2.1.9:** TDM might be beneficial in non-response to thiopurines [EL3] or anti-TNF [EL2]; mandatory during calcineurin inhibitor therapy [EL2]. TDM led to therapeutic changes in >70% of patients with secondary loss of response (Yanai 2015). High infliximab trough levels correlate with good clinical response and low CRP (meta-analysis of 22 trials, 3,483 patients). Higher adalimumab trough levels associated with MH (median 14.7 vs. 3.4 µg/mL; p<0.001). Primary non-response to anti-TNF in severe UC associated with faecal loss of infliximab (Brandse 2015).

## Assessment

The 26% vs. 42% 1-year relapse rate (with vs. without endoscopic remission confirmation) makes the case for objective assessment before withdrawal. TDM integration reflects the European approach of treating drug monitoring as a diagnostic tool.
