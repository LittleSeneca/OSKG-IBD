# pimentel-ch2-2: IBS/SIBO patients have 60% fewer MMC phase III waves than healthy controls

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch2 Gut Anatomy and MMC]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 2

## The Claim

In a clinical study published in 2002, we found that IBS and SIBO patients had 60 percent fewer housekeeping waves compared to healthy controls, meaning that the small bowel motility of these IBS patients was insufficient to completely clean out their small intestine.

## Evidence

Pimentel's 2002 study (likely Pimentel et al., American Journal of Gastroenterology, 2002). The mechanism: "On a regular basis, bacteria in the colon creep back up to the small intestine in search of more food. However, the small intestine pushes them back to the colon with housekeeper waves. If these waves are impaired, bacteria can build up in the small intestine. This is also known as small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. This is Pimentel's foundational finding and has been replicated in subsequent studies. The 60% reduction is a large effect size.

## Stakes

This is the empirical core of Pimentel's motility-based SIBO theory. Without MMC impairment, SIBO would be a different disease (possibly anatomical, not functional). The MMC-SIBO connection justifies prokinetic therapy as a core treatment pillar.

## Assessment

The 2002 paper is arguably Pimentel's most important single contribution. It shifted the SIBO paradigm from structural causes (blind loops, strictures) to functional motility disorders as the primary etiology. However, the causal direction is worth noting: does impaired MMC cause SIBO, or does SIBO (through inflammation or gas production) impair MMC? Pimentel argues the former based on the anti-vinculin antibody mechanism (Chapter 4), which provides a pre-SIBO cause of MMC damage.
