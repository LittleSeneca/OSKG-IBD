# acg-cd2018-26: Mesalamine is minimally effective; imidazole antibiotics have short-term benefit but poor tolerability (R55-R56)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Mesalamine is minimally effective; imidazole antibiotics have short-term benefit but poor tolerability (R55-R56)

## Evidence

Mesalamine: Cochrane analysis — reduced clinical recurrence (RR 0.76, NNT 12) and severe endoscopic recurrence (RR 0.50, NNT 8) vs. placebo. Meta-analysis of 11 RCTs (1,282 patients): reduced relapse (RR 0.86, NNT 13). Sulfasalazine specifically did not prevent relapse. Metronidazole (20 mg/kg/day): reduced severe endoscopic recurrence at 3 months and clinical recurrence at 1 year, but benefit lost when stopped. Nearly 50% intolerant to antibiotics. Combining metronidazole × 3 months + azathioprine × 12 months reduces endoscopic recurrence at 1 year vs. metronidazole alone.

## Assessment

Mesalamine's postoperative role is marginal — NNT of 12-13 means treating many patients for minimal benefit. The guideline's endorsement of "no treatment" as an option for truly low-risk patients is more honest. Antibiotics have a real effect but intolerability limits practical use. The metronidazole + azathioprine bridge strategy (antibiotics for early protection while thiopurine kicks in) is an elegant clinical solution.
