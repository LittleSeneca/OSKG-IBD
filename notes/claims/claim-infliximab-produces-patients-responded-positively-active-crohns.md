# steinhart-drug-6: Anti-TNF biologics (infliximab, adalimumab) are the most effective drug class for moderate-severe IBD, capable of achieving mucosal healing

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch7 Drug Therapy]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 7

## The Claim

Infliximab produces "60% to 80% of patients responded positively" in active Crohn's. "Repeated dosing of infliximab can produce complete healing of the inner lining of the intestine, something that is not consistently observed with any of the previous medical therapies." It is effective for fistulizing Crohn's ("the first treatment shown to cause fistulas to stop draining") and for treatment-resistant UC. Adalimumab, a fully human anti-TNF, offers the convenience of subcutaneous self-injection.

## Evidence

Clinical trial data. Steinhart references the ACT trials for UC, the fistula trials for Crohn's, and general clinical experience. He notes the infusion reaction rate (5-10%), the tuberculosis reactivation risk (now mitigated by pre-treatment screening), and the antibody formation problem (which reduces drug levels and can cause loss of response).

## Stakes

Anti-TNF agents remain first-line biologics in most guidelines. The development of biosimilar infliximab has dramatically reduced cost, making these drugs accessible to more patients. Antibody formation and loss of response remain the major clinical challenge -- therapeutic drug monitoring (drug levels + antibody testing) has become standard to manage this.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The debate is not about efficacy but about positioning: should anti-TNF be first biologic (current practice) or should vedolizumab (better safety) or ustekinumab be used first in appropriate patients?

## Assessment

Steinhart presents infliximab as transformative, which it was. The case study of Naveen (19-year-old with extensive Crohn's, recommended for early biologic) illustrates the top-down logic effectively. The antibody/loss-of-response problem is well-explained, and the practical distinction between infliximab (IV infusion, every 6-8 weeks) and adalimumab (subcutaneous, every 1-2 weeks) matters for patient preference.
