# acg-cd2018-7: Antibiotics should not be restricted solely to prevent CD flares; stress/depression assessment is part of comprehensive care (R8-R9)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Antibiotics should not be restricted solely to prevent CD flares; stress/depression assessment is part of comprehensive care (R8-R9)

## Evidence

Paradoxically, a case-crossover study from the General Practice Research Database found antibiotic exposure within 60 days was associated with *reduced* risk of flare. However, antibiotic-associated dysbiosis increases *C. difficile* risk, and IBD patients have higher carriage rates of toxigenic *C. difficile*. The guideline's practical point: don't withhold necessary antibiotics, but test for *C. difficile* if diarrhea worsens post-antibiotics.

**Recommendation 9:** Perceived stress, depression, and anxiety are common in IBD and lead to decreased health-related quality of life and lower adherence. Assessment and management of stress, depression, and anxiety should be included as part of comprehensive care. **Strong recommendation, very low level of evidence.**

**Evidence summary:** Depression is associated with CD flares. Perceived stress has a strong relationship to IBD symptoms, though increased bowel symptoms are not always associated with increased inflammation. CD patients with major depression and anxiety have greater risk of surgery and higher healthcare utilization. Cognitive behavioral therapy reduced symptomatic disease activity in depressed children with CD vs. supportive listening.

## Assessment

R9 is philosophically important — it elevates mental health to a GRADE recommendation level despite "very low" quality evidence. This reflects the panel's clinical experience that psychological comorbidity is underdiagnosed and undertreated in IBD. R8 (antibiotics) is practically useful: many clinicians unnecessarily restrict antibiotics in IBD patients out of fear of triggering a flare.
