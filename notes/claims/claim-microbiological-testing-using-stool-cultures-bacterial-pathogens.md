# yamada-ucdx63-2: Stool testing to exclude infection is mandatory before diagnosing UC, and C. difficile co-infection portends a worse prognosis

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch63 Ulcerative Colitis Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

Microbiological testing using stool cultures for bacterial pathogens, panel-based nucleic acid amplification tests for potential bacterial, viral, and parasitic pathogens and, most importantly, polymerase chain reaction for Clostridium difficile toxin are indicated in all patients with diarrhea with suspected ulcerative colitis.

## Evidence

Approximately 10-15% of UC patients with diarrhea test positive for C. difficile, and another 15-20% may test positive for other organisms. C. difficile infection in UC patients is associated with higher disease severity, hospitalization risk, colectomy risk, and mortality. Multiplex gastrointestinal pathogen panels offer advantages (shorter turnaround, consolidated workflow, increased sensitivity) but cannot distinguish viable organisms from remnant nucleic acid, leading to potential overdiagnosis and inappropriate antibiotic use. Non-C. difficile infections do not necessarily portend a poorer prognosis but often lead to treatment delays.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The prevalence data and prognostic implications are from multiple cohort studies.

## Stakes

Missing C. difficile in a UC flare leads to corticosteroid escalation when the patient actually needs antibiotics -- a dangerous clinical error. Conversely, attributing a UC flare to a non-pathogenic PCR finding delays UC-directed therapy. The 10-15% C. difficile prevalence means this is not rare -- it should be tested in every suspected UC presentation and every flare.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Debate about whether universal multiplex PCR panels are superior to targeted C. difficile testing plus selective bacterial culture. The multiplex panels' inability to distinguish colonization from infection is the main argument against universal adoption.

## Assessment

The chapter's recommendation to test all patients is correct. The yield (10-15% for C. difficile alone) justifies universal testing. The caveat about multiplex panel overdiagnosis is important and well-stated.
