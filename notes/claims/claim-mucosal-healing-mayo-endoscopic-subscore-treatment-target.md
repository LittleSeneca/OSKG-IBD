# acg-uc2019-3: Mucosal healing (Mayo endoscopic subscore 0 or 1) is the treatment target -- but histologic healing is not yet a validated endpoint

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Mucosal healing (Mayo endoscopic subscore 0 or 1) is the treatment target -- but histologic healing is not yet a validated endpoint

## Evidence

The STRIDE consensus (2015) established composite endpoints (resolution of rectal bleeding + normalization of bowel habits + Mayo 0-1) as treatment targets. Endoscopic improvement (Mayo 0 or 1) rather than complete healing (Mayo 0) may be sufficient. FC pooled sensitivity for endoscopic inflammation in UC is 87.3%, specificity 77.1%, AUC 0.91 -- but optimum cutoffs vary widely (50-250 mg/g). Histologic inflammation (mucosal neutrophils) predicts clinical relapse and CRC risk, but no validated histologic index exists for clinical use.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 (AGA UC 2020) aligns strongly on mucosal healing as the treatment target and also demurs on histologic healing as a formal endpoint. G9 (ECCO 2022) goes further, endorsing histologic remission as an adjunctive target in selected patients (especially after biologic therapy), reflecting the European trend toward histologic endpoints. STRIDE-II (2021, postdating this guideline) elevated histologic healing from "not a target" to "an adjunctive measure of remission."

## Assessment

The guideline's position on histologic healing was conservative but appropriate for 2019. Since publication, the evidence base has shifted significantly: multiple studies now correlate histologic remission with reduced relapse, hospitalization, and CRC risk. STRIDE-II (2021) partially closed this gap. The FC recommendation as a surrogate is pragmatic but the "very low quality of evidence" rating for R5 reflects that no prospective trial has used FC-guided treatment escalation as a primary strategy. This is a major evidence gap in 2019 that remains partially unfilled.
