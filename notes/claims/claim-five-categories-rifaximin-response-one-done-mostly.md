# pimentel-ch6-2: Rifaximin produces five response patterns; approximately one-third of patients have durable response ("one and done")

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch6 Three Pillars of SIBO Management]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 6

## The Claim

Five categories of rifaximin response: (1) "One and done" -- mostly symptom-free for months or years; (2) "One and better, relapse in time" -- "generally accounts for about 70 percent of patients" who feel better then relapse within 6 months; (3) "Feel better, relapse quickly" -- within one month, suggesting mechanical cause; (4) "Partial response" -- requires further investigation; (5) "No response" -- suggests alternative diagnosis.

## Evidence

TARGET 3 clinical trial data. Clinical experience at Cedars-Sinai. A sub-study showing rifaximin was "even more effective among IBS with diarrhea patients who had a positive breath test for SIBO."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for the TARGET 3 data (published RCT). MEDIUM for the five-category clinical stratification (clinical observation, not formally validated).

## Stakes

The 44% response rate in TARGET 3 is modest but clinically meaningful in a condition with few effective treatments. The finding that breath-test-positive patients had higher response rates supports Pimentel's thesis that SIBO-positive IBS is a distinct, treatment-responsive subgroup. The five-category framework helps clinicians set realistic expectations and identify patients who need more aggressive investigation (quick relapsers, non-responders).

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some critics note that the 44% response rate means 56% did not respond, and the placebo response rate in IBS trials is typically 30-40%. Rifaximin's net benefit over placebo is modest (~10-15%). The durable response (>6 months) in ~15% of treated patients is more impressive but represents a minority.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Rifaximin's efficacy is real but modest. The key clinical insight is that response to rifaximin is a diagnostic test in itself: response suggests SIBO; non-response suggests alternative pathology. The breath-test-positive subgroup's higher response rate is the strongest evidence for breath-test-guided antibiotic therapy.
