# pimentel-ch2-5: Bile acid diarrhea (BAD) is distinct from but exacerbated by SIBO, and SIBO is a main cause of BAD

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch2 Gut Anatomy and MMC]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 2

## The Claim

When bacteria encounter bile, they convert it to toxic bile acids (such as deoxycholic acid and lithocholic acid), which are believed to cause diarrhea. This is known as bile acid diarrhea (BAD) and is different from SIBO, but it's worse when SIBO is present. One of the main causes of bile acid diarrhea is SIBO.

## Evidence

The biochemical pathway: bacteria deconjugate bile acids to toxic secondary bile acids (deoxycholic acid, lithocholic acid) that are pro-secretory in the colon. The connection to gallbladder: "If your gallbladder is removed or is not functioning properly, bile will flow straight from the liver into the small intestine and eventually to the colon, often resulting in diarrhea."

## Stakes

BAD is an underdiagnosed cause of chronic diarrhea that may be misattributed to IBS-D. If SIBO truly causes BAD in a substantial subset of patients, treating the SIBO should resolve the BAD without bile acid sequestrants. This also explains why some IBS-D patients respond to rifaximin (treating the SIBO) while others respond to cholestyramine (binding bile acids) -- they may have different underlying mechanisms.

## Assessment

The SIBO-BAD connection is plausible but understudied. The SeHCAT test (gold standard for BAD diagnosis) is unavailable in the US, so the true prevalence of BAD in SIBO is unknown. Pimentel's claim that SIBO causes BAD may be true for a subset but likely overstates the causal relationship.
