# foote-cookbook-5: Food triggers should be identified through systematic single-food elimination, not blanket restriction

**Source:** [[Foote 2020 - Crohn's Disease Cookbook]] — Foote — Crohn's Disease AIP Cookbook (2020)

## The Claim

I recommend that you only eliminate one food (or a food grouping, like dairy or wheat) at a time. Eliminating multiple foods at a time makes it difficult to determine which food is bothering you.

## Evidence

Clinical dietetics practice standard. No trials comparing elimination strategies for Crohn's.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM. The single-food elimination approach is methodologically sound for identifying triggers but assumes triggers are independent (not synergistic) and that symptoms respond on a timescale of days-

## Stakes

This is the pragmatic alternative to protocol diets. Instead of eliminating entire food categories based on a theory (SCD, low-FODMAP), Foote advocates empiric, individualized trigger identification. This is more tailored but requires more patient effort and discipline.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Protocol diet advocates would argue that systematic elimination of all potential triggers simultaneously is more efficient and that individual trigger identification is confounded by delayed reactions and interaction effects. The SCD and low-FODMAP eliminate broad categories first, then reintroduce individually.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Foote's approach is the dietitian's standard of care and is appropriate for patients who can tolerate structured self-experimentation. For patients in severe flares who cannot afford months of trial-and-error, a protocol diet that eliminates known triggers preemptively may be more practical. The approaches are complementary: start with a protocol diet, then use single-food reintroduction to personalize.
