# ecco-cd2020-1: CD is a progressive disease requiring early intensive monitoring and individualized risk-stratified therapy

**Source:** [[ECCO Crohn's Medical 2020 - Torres]] — Torres et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Crohn's Disease: Medical Management (2020)

## The Claim

CD is a progressive disease requiring early intensive monitoring and individualized risk-stratified therapy

## Evidence

The guideline opens with an explicit statement of the modern CD treatment paradigm: chronic untreated inflammation results in progressive bowel damage, even when asymptomatic. This is the foundational premise driving early intervention and tight control. The guideline emphasizes stratifying patients by prognostic risk factors (age, disease extent, perianal involvement, deep ulcerations, complicated phenotype) and individualizing therapy. Because of the well-documented disconnect between symptoms and inflammation, objective monitoring (endoscopy, CRP, fecal calprotectin, imaging) is mandatory. The treat-to-target concept is endorsed though the STRIDE-II update had not yet been published.

**Key patient-centered additions:** Four EFCCA patient representatives participated in all face-to-face meetings — a first for ECCO guideline development. The guideline explicitly incorporates patient values and preferences into recommendation strength, consistent with GRADE methodology.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G1 (ACG 2018) shares the progressive-disease premise but phrases it less forcefully. G8 is more assertive about early biologic use in high-risk patients. G6 (AGA 2021) likewise endorses treat-to-target but with STRIDE-II targets. The ECCO emphasis on individualized risk stratification is more developed than ACG's simpler low/moderate/high-risk framework.

## Assessment

The paradigm statement is the most important section of the guideline — it justifies the entire treatment algorithm. The inclusion of patient representatives is methodologically admirable but its practical impact on the final recommendations is unclear. The guideline's emphasis on cost considerations alongside efficacy reflects the European multi-payer reality and is a genuine differentiator from US guidelines.
