# pimentel-ch1-7: IBS-C is possibly a different disease from IBS-D and IBS-M

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Intro Ch1 IBS and SIBO Overlap]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Intro + Ch1

## The Claim

We now consider IBS-D and IBS-M as one type of IBS disease -- Non-constipation IBS -- and IBS-C is possibly a different disease, Constipation IBS.

## Evidence

IBS subtyping: IBS-C 35%, IBS-D 40%, IBS-M 23%. The rationale for splitting C from D/M appears to be based on differential SIBO prevalence and treatment response. The authors note that SIBO more commonly presents with diarrhea (IBS-D) or mixed symptoms, and that the constipation-predominant subtype may have a different pathophysiology (possibly related to methanogen overgrowth rather than hydrogen-producing bacterial overgrowth -- a concept developed in later chapters).

## Stakes

If IBS-C is a different disease, then treatments should differ: rifaximin for IBS-D (hydrogen SIBO), different antimicrobials or approaches for IBS-C (methane-predominant IMO). This would explain the differential drug response patterns seen in clinical trials.

## Assessment

This is one of Pimentel's more speculative claims but has received substantial subsequent support. The 2020 renaming of "methane-dominant SIBO" to "Intestinal Methanogen Overgrowth (IMO)" (which Pimentel himself championed) provides a mechanism: archaeal methanogens (not bacteria) produce methane, which slows transit and causes constipation. This is covered in Chapter 5. The IBS-C = different disease claim makes sense within this framework.

**Strongest section:** The diagnosis-by-exclusion critique. The 4-6 year diagnostic delay, $20,000 individual costs, $21 billion societal burden, and 79% community clinician exclusion-diagnosis rate are damning. Pimentel's alternative (positive SIBO testing) is not yet standard of care, but the critique of the current paradigm stands regardless.

**Weakest section:** The categorical reversal of the anxiety-IBS relationship. While Pimentel is correct that the historical psychosomatic framing was harmful and wrong, his either/or framing undersells the genuine bidirectionality of the gut-brain axis. The Talley and Walker data support gut→brain dominance in a subset of patients but not universal unidirectionality.

**Historical note:** This combined Introduction + Chapter 1 establishes the book's argumentative foundation that Pimentel 2006 (A New IBS Solution) first proposed. The 2022 book differs from the 2006 book in three key ways visible even in these opening chapters: (1) the SIBO-IBS connection is now presented as established fact rather than emerging hypothesis, (2) the treatment armamentarium has expanded (8 FDA drugs vs. few in 2006), and (3) the autoimmunity mechanism (anti-vinculin antibodies from food poisoning) is now the unifying pathophysiology rather than a speculative mechanism. The rhetorical confidence shift from 2006 (tentative, hypothesis-forward) to 2022 (declarative, evidence-forward) reflects 16 years of published research.
