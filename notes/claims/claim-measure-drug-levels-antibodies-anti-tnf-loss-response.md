# acg-uc2019-13: Measure drug levels and antibodies in anti-TNF loss of response; primary nonresponders with adequate drug levels should switch mechanism, not cycle within class

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Measure drug levels and antibodies in anti-TNF loss of response; primary nonresponders with adequate drug levels should switch mechanism, not cycle within class

## Evidence

Up to 20% of patients are primary nonresponders to anti-TNF; an additional 10-15% lose response annually. Causes include: inadequate drug levels (increased clearance from high inflammatory burden, fecal drug loss, neutralizing antibodies), concurrent infection, or mechanistic failure. The approach integrates clinical evaluation, objective inflammation confirmation (endoscopy, CRP, FC), exclusion of enteric infections, and therapeutic drug monitoring.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 (AGA 2020) makes a stronger recommendation for therapeutic drug monitoring in active disease. G9 (ECCO 2022) has the most detailed algorithm for reactive TDM. The distinction between primary nonresponse (switch class) and secondary loss of response (optimize or switch within class) is consistent across all guidelines.

## Assessment

The "very low quality of evidence" for R29 is honest -- no prospective RCT randomizes patients to TDM-guided vs empiric dose escalation in UC (though such trials exist in CD). The conceptual framework (primary nonresponse = mechanism failure; secondary LOR = pharmacokinetic problem) is sound and clinically useful. The recommendation against using a biosimilar to the failed originator (KC 25) is obvious -- they're the same molecule -- but worth stating given physician confusion about biosimilars.
