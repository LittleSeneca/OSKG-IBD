# acg-cd2018-18: Cyclosporine, mycophenolate mofetil, and tacrolimus should NOT be used for Crohn's disease (R31)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Cyclosporine, mycophenolate mofetil, and tacrolimus should NOT be used for Crohn's disease (R31)

## Evidence

Cyclosporine, despite efficacy in UC, has not been demonstrated to be effective in CD (two negative RCTs). Mycophenolate mofetil was inferior to azathioprine in a small RCT. Tacrolimus has only small uncontrolled case series with suggestion of benefit for luminal disease but insufficient evidence. Thalidomide may be effective in severe CD but should be used only in exceptional circumstances due to serious adverse effects (sedation, constipation, peripheral neuropathy, severe birth defects).

## Assessment

This is a clinically important negative recommendation, particularly for hospital-based practice where cyclosporine is occasionally used as salvage therapy for severe UC. The guideline clearly states: what works in UC doesn't necessarily work in CD. The note on tacrolimus creates a minor inconsistency — R39 (later) recommends tacrolimus for short-term fistulizing disease treatment, so the molecule isn't entirely useless in CD, just not for luminal disease.
