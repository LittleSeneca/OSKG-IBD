# gottschall-ch35-7: Starches are incompletely digested even by healthy people, and certain starch structures resist enzymatic breakdown

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch3-5 The Vicious Cycle Mechanism]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 3-5

## The Claim

Some starchy foods which were assumed to be digested completely are, in fact, incompletely digested by most people. In those people with intestinal disorders, the digestibility of starch is even further affected.

## Evidence

References to incomplete starch digestion in healthy people, genetic variation in starch types (rice, corn strains), and the amylose/amylopectin distinction. Notes that some intestinal bacteria are "made more virulent by the presence of undigested cornstarch in the intestine."

## Stakes

This is the SCD's justification for banning all grains, potatoes, corn, and rice. If starches are incompletely digested in everyone (not just IBD patients), then starch restriction should benefit anyone with fermentation-sensitive guts, not just those with diagnosed disaccharidase deficiency.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Resistant starch researchers note that incomplete starch digestion is not inherently harmful -- resistant starch feeds beneficial bacteria and produces butyrate, a colonocyte fuel and anti-inflammatory SCFA. The SCD bans all starches, potentially eliminating beneficial substrates along with harmful ones.

**Alternative reading:** The problem is not starch per se but rapidly fermentable starch reaching an overgrown small intestinal bacterial population. Some starches (resistant starch type 2-3) may actually be therapeutic for colonic health while being problematic in SIBO. The distinction between small intestinal and colonic fermentation is critical but absent from Gottschall's model.

## Assessment

Gottschall correctly identified that starch digestion is variable and incomplete. But her all-or-nothing approach (ban all grains and starchy vegetables) is too blunt. Modern approaches (low-FODMAP, LFE) make finer distinctions based on fermentability testing rather than chemical class, and some allow limited starch while restricting specific fermentable carbohydrates.
