# aga-cd2021-7: Corticosteroids are suggested for induction, strongly recommended against for maintenance; 5-ASA and sulfasalazine are recommended against (R8A-B, R9)

**Source:** [[AGA Crohn's 2021 - Feuerstein]] — Feuerstein et al. — AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines: Crohn's Disease (2021)

## The Claim

Corticosteroids are suggested for induction, strongly recommended against for maintenance; 5-ASA and sulfasalazine are recommended against (R8A-B, R9)

## Evidence

**Recommendation 8A:** "The AGA suggests corticosteroids over no treatment for induction of remission." **Conditional, moderate certainty.** Prednisone up to 60 mg/day: superior to placebo (RR 0.57, 0.45-0.73). Budesonide: superior to placebo (RR 0.74, 0.60-0.91), though 2 of 3 studies enrolled mild-moderate CD — indirectness concern. Budesonide was inferior to systemic corticosteroids for induction (RR for failure 1.20, 1.01-1.44), but patients with distal ileal/ascending colon disease who prioritize safety over efficacy may reasonably choose budesonide.

## Assessment

These negative recommendations are the least controversial section of the guideline — there is broad consensus across all CD guidelines that steroids don't maintain remission and 5-ASA doesn't work for CD. The budesonide caveat (patients who prioritize safety may reasonably choose it despite lower efficacy) is a practical acknowledgment that shared decision-making sometimes favors less effective but safer options. The frank statement about budesonide's inferiority to systemic steroids is useful — budesonide is often presented as "just as good but safer," which is not what the data show.
