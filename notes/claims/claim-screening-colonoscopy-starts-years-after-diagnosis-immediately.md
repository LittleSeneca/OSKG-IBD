# acg-uc2019-16: Screening colonoscopy starts at 8 years after diagnosis (immediately if PSC); surveillance intervals 1-3 years based on risk factors; high-definition white-light or chromoendoscopy are both acceptable

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Screening colonoscopy starts at 8 years after diagnosis (immediately if PSC); surveillance intervals 1-3 years based on risk factors; high-definition white-light or chromoendoscopy are both acceptable

## Evidence

Cumulative CRC risk in UC: 1% at 10 years, 2% at 20 years, 5% beyond 20 years. St. Mark's surveillance program: CRC incidence 4.7 per 1,000 patient-years. Risk declining over time. CRC risk factors: disease duration, inflammatory activity, young age at diagnosis, extensive colitis, PSC, family history of CRC. No prospective study demonstrates mortality benefit from surveillance. A Cochrane analysis of 3 small studies found no significant mortality reduction. For PSC-UC: annual surveillance starting at diagnosis. Dye spray chromoendoscopy detects more dysplasia than standard-definition white light. With high-definition scopes: a 2018 RCT (Bisschops) showed similar detection between HD-NBI and HD-chromoendoscopy.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The SCENIC consensus (2015) recommended chromoendoscopy over white-light for all surveillance -- a stronger position than R49. G5 (AGA 2020) similarly suggests either HD-white-light or chromoendoscopy. G9 (ECCO 2022) recommends chromoendoscopy as preferred but allows HD-white-light as an alternative. The 8-year screening start is consistent across guidelines. The 1-3 year surveillance interval is broader than earlier guidelines (which often said 1-2 years for extensive disease) and reflects risk-stratification principles.

## Assessment

This section navigates the tension between the SCENIC consensus (strong chromoendoscopy recommendation) and practical realities (limited adoption, time burden, emerging HD data). The conditional recommendation for R49 effectively says: "if you have HD scopes, use them however you prefer; if you have SD scopes, you must use dye spray." This is a reasonable compromise. The acknowledgement that random biopsies may no longer be necessary (KC 47) reflects a major shift -- the "DALM" era of mandatory random biopsies and automatic colectomy for any dysplasia is over. The guideline's endorsement of endoscopic resection and surveillance (rather than automatic colectomy) for discrete, fully resected dysplastic lesions is the most important clinical change in the CRC section.
