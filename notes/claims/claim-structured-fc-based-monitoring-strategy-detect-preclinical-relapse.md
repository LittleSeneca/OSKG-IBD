# ecco-diag2019-7: A structured FC-based monitoring strategy can detect preclinical relapse; two consecutively elevated FC levels are the best predictor of clinical flare

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

A structured FC-based monitoring strategy can detect preclinical relapse; two consecutively elevated FC levels are the best predictor of clinical flare

## Evidence

The four-phase FC monitoring model (Figure 2): Phase I (diagnosis), Phase II (induction), Phase III (remission — detect drift from target range), Phase IV (therapy adjustment). FC in target range → retest 3 months; action range → adjust treatment, retest 1 month; uncertain range → retest 1 month before decision. Two consecutively elevated FC best predicts clinical relapse. FC rises ~3 months before clinical relapse (Zhulina 2016). A single FC cannot predict course over 12 months. CALM study (Colombel 2018) demonstrated feasibility of tight-control management. CRP ≥5 mg/L has high specificity but poor sensitivity; almost two-thirds of asymptomatic patients with normal CRP have active endoscopic lesions. CRP is inferior to FC for postoperative recurrence or small bowel CD follow-up.

**Statement 2.2.2:** Asymptomatic patients with abnormal biochemical parameters — after excluding infection, perform endoscopic or cross-sectional imaging [EL5].

## Assessment

The CALM study validates tight-control FC monitoring. The 3-month lead time before clinical relapse and the "two consecutively elevated" rule are clinically actionable. Implementation barriers: patient compliance with serial stool testing and lack of standardized action thresholds.
