# acg-sibo2020-3: Glucose or lactulose hydrogen breath testing is suggested for SIBO diagnosis in IBS, suspected motility disorders, and post-surgical patients

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

Glucose or lactulose hydrogen breath testing is suggested for SIBO diagnosis in IBS, suspected motility disorders, and post-surgical patients

## Evidence

Breath testing is inexpensive, noninvasive, and widely available. Sensitivity and specificity vs small bowel aspirate culture: lactulose sensitivity 31-68%, specificity 44-100%; glucose sensitivity 20-93%, specificity 30-86% (Khoshini et al. systematic review). The guideline acknowledges these wide ranges and the low quality of evidence. The glucose breath test may miss distal SIBO (glucose absorbed in proximal duodenum). The lactulose breath test may produce false positives from accelerated transit and colonic fermentation. Methodological standardization from the North American Consensus: 75g glucose or 10g lactulose, measure hydrogen AND methane, avoid antibiotics 4 weeks prior, avoid promotility agents/laxatives 1 week prior, low-fermentable diet day before, 8-12 hour fast, avoid smoking and physical exertion during test.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The AGA Practice Update (2020) is more cautious, noting that breath tests "lack sensitivity and specificity" and that "no test can be considered the gold standard." The Rome Foundation Report (2017) provides the most detailed critique of breath testing methodology and emphasizes the need for standardized protocols. The North American Consensus (Rezaie 2017) provided the standardization that this ACG guideline adopts.

## Assessment

All three recommendations are "conditional, very low evidence" -- the weakest possible GRADE rating. This is honest. The guideline is saying: "breath testing is the best noninvasive tool we have, but it's not great, and we're recommending it because the alternatives (small bowel aspirate) are invasive and impractical for widespread use." The wide sensitivity/specificity ranges reflect the fundamental problem: we're comparing an indirect test (breath gas) against an imperfect gold standard (culture of a single duodenal aspirate that may miss patchy overgrowth).
