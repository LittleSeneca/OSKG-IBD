# foote-cookbook-2: Malabsorption in IBD affects specific nutrients depending on inflammation location

**Source:** [[Foote 2020 - Crohn's Disease Cookbook]] — Foote — Crohn's Disease AIP Cookbook (2020)

## The Claim

Inflammation in the small intestine can cause malabsorption of nutrients, while inflammation in the large intestine can inhibit the absorption of water and electrolytes.

## Evidence

Standard nutritional physiology. Signs of malnutrition listed: fatigue, light-headedness, paleness, numbness, tingling, bruising, bone/muscle pain, hair loss, muscle cramping, appetite loss, taste/smell changes.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. Site-specific malabsorption is well-documented. Small intestinal Crohn's → fat-soluble vitamin and B12 malabsorption; colonic disease → water and electrolyte losses. The nutrient list is consist

## Stakes

This maps the nutritional consequences of IBD to specific dietary targets. Unlike SCD which attempts to correct malabsorption by carbohydrate restriction, Foote's approach is to supplement around the malabsorption while eating whatever foods can be tolerated.
