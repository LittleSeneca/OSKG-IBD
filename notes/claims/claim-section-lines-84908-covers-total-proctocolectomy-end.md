# yamada-surg65-3: The IPAA procedure transforms UC from a disease cured by proctocolectomy to one managed with a functional pouch, with specific complications

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch65 Surgical Treatment of IBD]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 65

## The Claim

The UC section (lines 84908+) covers total proctocolectomy with end ileostomy as the historical standard and IPAA as the current restorative procedure of choice. The chapter covers technical aspects: pouch construction, temporary diverting ileostomy, and staged procedures for sick/malnourished patients.

## Evidence

Surgical technique descriptions and outcome data. The chapter acknowledges indeterminate colitis as a diagnostic challenge: patients with indeterminate colitis undergoing IPAA have "only a slightly higher risk of pouch failure" compared to UC patients, but known CD patients are "usually not candidates for pelvic pouch reconstruction surgery, except in special circumstances." CD of the pouch after IPAA has "a poor prognosis, with up to 50% of affected patients requiring ileal diversion, with or without pouch resection."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for the technical descriptions and complication rates. HIGH for the CD-in-pouch prognosis.

## Stakes

The distinction between UC and CD is surgically critical. Misclassifying Crohn's colitis as UC and performing IPAA can lead to pouch failure in up to 50% of cases. The indeterminate colitis category represents a gray zone where shared decision-making with informed consent about higher failure risk is essential.

## Assessment

This chapter provides the surgeon's technical perspective that complements the patient-facing Steinhart Ch 8. The IPAA section is notably thorough on the staged approach (3-stage for sick patients: colectomy, then pouch creation with ileostomy, then ileostomy closure). The CD-in-pouch prognosis data (50% require diversion) quantifies the risk that Steinhart describes qualitatively.

**Strongest section:** The operative morbidity data for specific procedures (strictureplasty, ileocecectomy, IPAA) provides quantitative risk estimates useful for surgical consent. The risk factor identification (albumin, weight loss, steroids, abscess) for strictureplasty complications has direct preoperative optimization implications.

**Weakest section:** The chapter has limited discussion of the combined medical-surgical approach to perianal CD, which is the standard of care. The medical management of postoperative recurrence is referenced only briefly and largely deferred to Chapter 64.

**Overall quality:** A technically thorough surgical reference that complements the medical chapters. The authors' willingness to present early laparoscopic resection as an alternative to biologic escalation for limited ileocecal CD is a useful surgical perspective that challenges medical default pathways.
