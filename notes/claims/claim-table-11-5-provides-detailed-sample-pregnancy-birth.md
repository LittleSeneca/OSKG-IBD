# chutkan-rewild-5: A microbiome-preserving birth plan should prioritize vaginal delivery, avoidance of unnecessary interventions, immediate skin-to-skin contact, and breastfeeding

**Source:** [[The Microbiome Solution - Chutkan - Ch11 Rewilding Illness]] — Chutkan — The Microbiome Solution (2015), Ch 11

## The Claim

Table 11-5 provides a detailed "Sample Pregnancy and Birth Plan/Goals" covering pregnancy (avoid antibiotics and medications, avoid early induction), labor (no IV/catheter unless necessary, dim lights, freedom of movement, avoid Pitocin, pain management choice), delivery (immediate skin-to-skin, breastfeeding within first hour, no baby antibiotics without consent, no antibacterial products on baby), C-section (vaginal swabbing of baby if C-section unavoidable, immediate breastfeeding post-surgery), and breastfeeding (no formula without medical necessity, rooming-in).

## Evidence

Based on the microbiome seeding literature (Ch 2, 7): vaginal delivery inoculates the infant with maternal vaginal and fecal bacteria; C-section babies are colonized by skin and environmental bacteria instead; breastfeeding provides human milk oligosaccharides (HMOs) that selectively feed Bifidobacterium species.

## Assessment

This is one of the book's strongest practical contributions. The birth plan synthesizes microbiome evidence into actionable patient communication. The inclusion of vaginal swabbing for C-section babies (a practice that was gaining attention in 2015) is appropriately cautious in its language ("please consider allowing").

**Strongest sections:** The 10 questions for antibiotic prescribing and the birth plan. Both are practical, evidence-grounded, and give patients language for clinical conversations they might otherwise not know how to have.

**Weakest section:** The dietary and supplement recommendations during antibiotics (beyond probiotics) rely more on traditional remedies and mechanistic reasoning than on antibiotic-specific clinical trials. They are unlikely to harm and may help, but the evidence base is thin.

**Clinical significance for IBD/SIBO:** The acne-antibiotic-IBD claim is the most important takeaway for the knowledge graph. Ch 11 also provides the practical bridge between recognizing dysbiosis (Ch 5) and doing something about it — the patient-empowerment scripts are useful for translating microbiome knowledge into clinical action.
