# acg-cd2018-4: Ileocolonoscopy with biopsies is essential for diagnosis; small bowel imaging is required for complete evaluation

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Summary Statements 24-35. Not GRADE-rated.

## Evidence

- **Ileocolonoscopy with biopsies** (Summary 24-25): Should be performed in all patients with suspected CD. Disease distribution and severity should be documented at diagnosis. Biopsies of uninvolved mucosa are recommended to identify extent of histologic disease. Granulomas are present in only a minority (up to 33%).
- **Upper endoscopy** (Summary 26): Only in patients with upper GI signs/symptoms. Asymptomatic CD patients may have mild endoscopic (64%) and histologic (70%) upper GI changes, but these have no clinical significance in adults.
- **Video capsule endoscopy** (Summary 27-28): Useful adjunct when high index of suspicion for small bowel CD. Superior to small bowel barium studies, CTE, and ileocolonoscopy (incremental diagnostic yield of 32%, 47%, and 22%, respectively). High negative predictive value (96%). Capsule retention risk: 0-5.4% in suspected CD; use patency capsule or imaging first in patients with obstructive symptoms.
- **Deep enteroscopy** (Summary 29): Not routine — reserved for when tissue sampling or therapeutic intervention (stricture dilation) is needed.
- **Cross-sectional imaging** (Summary 30-35): Small bowel imaging should be part of initial diagnostic workup. CTE and MRE have comparable sensitivity (~90% for detecting CD lesions). MRE preferred in patients <35 years and those needing serial exams, due to absence of radiation. MRI pelvis and/or endoscopic ultrasound for characterizing perianal disease (>90% accuracy).

## Assessment

The diagnostic algorithm is well-reasoned: start with ileocolonoscopy + small bowel imaging, add capsule endoscopy if high suspicion persists. The guideline correctly cautions against capsule endoscopy without patency assessment in obstructive symptoms — capsule retention requiring surgery is a preventable catastrophe. The preference for MRE over CTE in young patients is an important radiation stewardship principle. Missing: the guideline doesn't provide a formal diagnostic algorithm diagram (ECCO 2020 does).
