# gottschall-ch35-2: Antibiotic therapy is of limited usefulness for chronic intestinal disorders

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch3-5 The Vicious Cycle Mechanism]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 3-5

## The Claim

A simple approach to minimizing the undesirable activities of intestinal microbes would seem to be through the use of antibiotics. This approach is often tried but, unfortunately, in most chronic intestinal disorders, it has limitations.

## Evidence

References to microbial adherence mechanisms: "there is evidence that intestinal microbes will not cause disease unless they develop methods of adhering to the gut wall." Antibiotic therapy "is of limited usefulness while other drugs of the cortisone and sulfa families have side-effects if continued too long."

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM in 1994; now MEDIUM-LOW given rifaximin's development. Gottschall wrote before rifaximin became the standard SIBO antibiotic. Her point about antibiotic limitations was valid in the era of broa

## Stakes

The SCD vs. antibiotic debate is still live. Pimentel's protocol uses rifaximin followed by the LFE diet to prevent recurrence. Gottschall's model would argue that diet alone should suffice if strictly followed, which Pimentel's clinical experience contradicts for many patients.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Pimentel and the Cedars-Sinai group: antibiotics (rifaximin) are necessary to reduce the bacterial load initially; diet maintains the reduction. Gottschall's claim that diet alone can starve out an established overgrowth overestimates dietary control of a complex ecosystem.

**Alternative reading:** Both are partially right. Diet can reduce fermentable substrate but may not eliminate an entrenched overgrowth that has adapted to mucosal adherence. Combined antibiotic + dietary therapy may be optimal for SIBO, as Pimentel's current protocol suggests.

## Assessment

Gottschall's antibiotic skepticism was well-founded for the 1994 antibiotic landscape. Rifaximin changes the calculus. The modern synthesis is: antibiotics for initial decontamination, diet for maintenance.
