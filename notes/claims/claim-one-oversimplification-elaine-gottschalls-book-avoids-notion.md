# gottschall-fw-2: The SCD is broader than food allergy approaches and addresses carbohydrate intolerance more comprehensively

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Foreword]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Foreword

## The Claim

One oversimplification Elaine Gottschall's book avoids is the notion that food allergy is the source of many gastrointestinal complaints... The more sophisticated belief that it is not individual foods themselves but the byproducts of ingestion of certain foods that cause intestinal problems is fast replacing the concept of food allergy.

## Evidence

Hoffman references Dr. J.O. Hunter's 1991 Lancet article (not directly cited, but referenced as "landmark") and "another recent Lancet article" on intolerances to corn, wheat, milk, potatoes, and rye.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The distinction between food allergy (IgE-mediated) and food intolerance (fermentation-mediated) is well-established in modern gastroenterology. Hunter's work on enterometabolic disorders

## Stakes

This reframes dietary therapy from "avoid what you're allergic to" to "avoid what your microbes ferment." This is the conceptual bridge between SCD and low-FODMAP/LFE diets -- they share the fermentation-prevention mechanism despite different food lists.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The food allergy model remains dominant in functional medicine (e.g., IgG food sensitivity testing). Mainstream gastroenterology has largely moved toward the fermentation model (low-FODMAP).

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Hoffman is correct that the fermentation model is more sophisticated than the allergy model. But his framing of SCD as uniquely addressing this is overstated -- low-FODMAP and LFE also target fermentation, just with different carbohydrate classifications.
