# steinhart-surg-2: Approximately 70-80% of Crohn's patients and 20-40% of UC patients will require surgery

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch8 Surgical Treatment]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 8

## The Claim

Approximately 70% to 80% of people suffering from Crohn's disease and approximately 20% to 40% of people suffering from ulcerative colitis will require surgery at some point following their diagnosis. These estimates are based on information from previous decades and do not necessarily reflect the effect of recent advances in drug therapy. There is some indication that surgical rates are coming down.

## Evidence

Historical surgical epidemiology. Steinhart acknowledges the limitation: these are pre-biologic era estimates, and rates are declining with earlier and more effective medical therapy.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM for current applicability. Modern cohort studies show surgical rates for Crohn's have decreased to approximately 40-50% at 10 years with biologic use. For UC, colectomy rates have dropped to ap

## Stakes

If surgical rates are declining, the calculus for delaying surgery shifts -- biologics may truly prevent or postpone the need. But for the substantial minority who still need surgery, delay can lead to worse outcomes (poorer nutrition, more emergencies, more complications).
