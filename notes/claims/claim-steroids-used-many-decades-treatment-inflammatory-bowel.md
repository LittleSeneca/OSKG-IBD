# steinhart-drug-4: Corticosteroids are rapidly effective but carry unacceptable long-term risk; budesonide offers a safer alternative for mild-moderate disease

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch7 Drug Therapy]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 7

## The Claim

Steroids have been used for many decades in the treatment of inflammatory bowel disease. For most of this time, they have been the most consistently effective class of medications... they generally work very quickly.

## Evidence

Steinhart provides an unusually detailed side effect catalogue, distinguishing common/reversible effects (moon face, acne, weight gain, insomnia, mood swings) from uncommon/possibly irreversible effects (osteoporosis, cataracts, diabetes, hypertension, avascular necrosis). He estimates that "approximately half of patients who take steroids experience at least one side effect." He presents budesonide (Entocort for ileal/right colon Crohn's; Uceris/Cortiment for UC) as a safer alternative with first-pass hepatic metabolism that minimizes systemic exposure.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for prednisone's efficacy and side effect profile. MEDIUM-HIGH for budesonide's safety advantage. The budesonide claims are well-supported by clinical trials showing similar efficacy to prednison

## Stakes

Steroid dependence and steroid side effects remain major clinical problems in IBD. Approximately 20-30% of patients become steroid-dependent or steroid-refractory. The availability of budesonide and the push toward early biologic use are both responses to steroid toxicity.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No one disputes steroid efficacy or toxicity. The debate is about how quickly to transition patients off steroids and onto steroid-sparing agents (thiopurines, biologics).

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Steinhart's treatment of steroids is thorough and honest. The side effect list does not sugarcoat. The distinction between common/reversible and uncommon/irreversible effects is clinically useful. The budesonide section correctly positions it as a bridge therapy for mild-moderate disease, not a replacement for prednisone in severe flares.
