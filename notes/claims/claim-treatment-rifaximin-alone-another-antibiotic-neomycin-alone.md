# pimentel-ch6-3: IMO (methane) requires dual antibiotic therapy -- rifaximin + neomycin achieves ~80% response

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch6 Three Pillars of SIBO Management]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 6

## The Claim

Treatment with rifaximin alone or with another antibiotic (neomycin) alone did not work well. However, in the laboratory, a combination of both antibiotics (rifaximin and neomycin) appeared more effective. As a result of this finding, we compared neomycin alone versus neomycin plus rifaximin in a double-blind study and found that nearly 80 percent of IBS patients taking both drugs felt better, and the methane-producing microorganisms were eradicated or diminished substantially.

## Evidence

Double-blind study of neomycin ± rifaximin. Laboratory data on M. smithii antibiotic sensitivity. Clinical observation that "methane seems to relapse sooner and more often" -- "constipation symptoms returned in one month in a large number of patients who initially responded."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for the dual-therapy superiority over monotherapy. MEDIUM for the specific 80% figure (one study, modest sample size). LOW for rifaximin + metronidazole (no published data).

## Stakes

IMO treatment is fundamentally different from hydrogen SIBO treatment. Treating IMO with rifaximin alone (the standard hydrogen SIBO approach) is ineffective. The clinical implication is that methane-positive breath tests mandate dual therapy. The rapid IMO relapse rate (within one month) is a major clinical challenge that Pillar 3 (prokinetics) aims to address.

## Assessment

The dual-therapy finding for IMO is clinically important and explains why many SIBO patients treated with rifaximin alone fail to improve (they had undiagnosed IMO). The rapid relapse rate highlights the need for aggressive Pillar 3 prevention. The rifaximin + metronidazole alternative, while lacking published data, is clinically useful given neomycin's ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity risks.
