# pimentel-ch6-1: SIBO is not a primary disease -- it is a secondary phenomenon caused by an underlying motility, structural, or autoimmune defect

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch6 Three Pillars of SIBO Management]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 6

## The Claim

The first step in treating SIBO is to understand that SIBO is not a primary disease; in fact, SIBO is a secondary phenomenon that's caused by other diseases... Understanding the basic principle that SIBO is a phenomenon from a separate root cause will help you answer questions like 'Why is my SIBO coming back so soon?' and 'Why did the doctor give me a prokinetic?'

## Evidence

Clinical experience at Cedars-Sinai. Modifiable causes: narcotic use, diabetes, active inflammation (Crohn's, celiac), pancreatic insufficiency. Non-modifiable causes: gastric bypass surgery, small bowel diverticulum, scleroderma. The anti-vinculin antibody levels predict treatment needs: "elevated levels of anti-vinculin antibody predict the need for antibiotics and other medications to improve motility."

## Stakes

This is the most important clinical insight in SIBO management. If SIBO is a secondary phenomenon, the treatment endpoint is not "bacteria eradicated" but "underlying cause addressed." This explains why some patients are "one and done" with rifaximin (transient MMC impairment from recent food poisoning) while others relapse repeatedly (permanent anti-vinculin nerve damage, structural adhesions, ongoing narcotic use).

## Assessment

The three-pillar framework is elegant, evidence-based, and clinically practical. It represents the maturation of SIBO treatment from a simple "antibiotics for SIBO" approach to a systems-based management strategy. The framework's strength is that it predicts treatment failure modes: if you only do Pillar 2 (antibiotics), SIBO will recur because the underlying cause (Pillar 1) and prevention strategy (Pillar 3) were not addressed.
