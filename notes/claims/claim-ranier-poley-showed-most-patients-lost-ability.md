# gottschall-ch12-5: Disaccharidase deficiency and mucus barrier dysfunction prevent carbohydrate digestion in intestinal disease

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch1-2 Origins and Scientific Evidence]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 1-2

## The Claim

Dr. J. Ranier Poley showed "most patients have lost the ability to digest disaccharides because of excessive mucus production by intestinal cells. An abnormally thick layer of surface mucus appears to be preventing contact between the disaccharides and the digestive enzymes of the absorptive cells."

## Evidence

Poley's microscopic examination of intestinal surfaces in patients with celiac disease, soy-protein intolerance, cow's milk protein intolerance, intractable diarrhea of infancy, chronic diarrhea in children, Giardia, cystic fibrosis, and Crohn's disease. Gottschall notes that lactase is the first enzyme to suffer damage and the last to return.

## Stakes

This provides the physiological basis for why disaccharide restriction helps: it's not that disaccharides are inherently harmful, but that the inflamed intestine cannot process them. The SCD is a therapeutic accommodation to intestinal damage, not a permanent dietary prescription.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The mucus barrier mechanism may be secondary. Primary disaccharidase deficiency in IBD is likely due to villous atrophy and brush border damage (documented in active Crohn's), with mucus playing a protective/confounding role. The net effect on carbohydrate digestion is the same regardless of which mechanism dominates.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Solid physiological reasoning consistent with modern understanding. The Poley mucus findings are particularly relevant for SIBO where mucus trapping of bacteria is a key pathology (Pimentel's work on reduced migrating motor complex and mucus clearance). Gottschall connected dots that mainstream gastroenterology is still connecting.

**Cross-reference note:** The disaccharidase deficiency mechanism (Claim 5) connects directly to the low-FODMAP diet rationale.
