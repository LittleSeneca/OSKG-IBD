# acg-cd2018-17: Ustekinumab is effective for moderate-to-severe CD, including in anti-TNF failures and anti-TNF-naive patients (R30)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Ustekinumab is effective for moderate-to-severe CD, including in anti-TNF failures and anti-TNF-naive patients (R30)

## Evidence

Based on the UNITI trials (Feagan 2016). Ustekinumab, an anti-p40 antibody inhibiting IL-12 and IL-23, demonstrated efficacy in both anti-TNF-failure and conventional therapy-failure populations. The psoriasis safety database shows an excellent safety profile without apparent increase in serious infections or malignancies. No head-to-head studies compare ustekinumab with vedolizumab or anti-TNF agents.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
This was the newest biologic at the time this guideline was published. G6 (AGA 2021) and G8 (ECCO 2020) have more mature positioning data for ustekinumab, including real-world evidence. The guideline's "can be used in anti-TNF-naive patients" statement was forward-looking for 2018.

## Assessment

The UNITI data is solid (high quality evidence), but the positioning question remains unanswered by direct comparison trials. The favorable safety profile is a genuine differentiator, particularly for patients concerned about infection/malignancy risk. The guideline's inclusion of anti-TNF-naive patients in the indication was progressive — ustekinumab was initially positioned primarily as a second-line biologic.
