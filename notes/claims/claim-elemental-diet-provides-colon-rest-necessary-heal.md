# thompson-een-3: The elemental diet works through three mechanisms: bowel rest, bacterial starvation, and mucosal healing supplementation

**Source:** [[Thompson 2013 - Elemental Diet Protocol]] — Thompson — The IBD Remission Diet (2013)

## The Claim

An elemental diet "provides the colon with the rest necessary to heal its wounds unhindered." Because nutrients are pre-digested, "there is very little undigested matter passing into the colon" -- providing bowel rest. The bacterial population "has less to eat" making it "easier to eliminate undesirable micro-organisms." Combined with antimicrobials and probiotics, the protocol "eliminates a lot of the bad bacteria in your gut."

## Evidence

Clinical reasoning and Thompson's personal experience. The bowel rest concept is standard surgical rationale. The bacterial starvation concept echoes Gottschall's SCD mechanism.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM. Bowel rest is a valid physiological concept (used in NPO/bowel rest protocols). Bacterial substrate reduction is plausible. But the relative contribution of each mechanism is unknown. EEN also

## Stakes

If bowel rest is the primary mechanism, any elemental/polymeric formula should work and food reintroduction timing matters. If bacterial starvation is primary, the protocol's antimicrobial component (Wild Oregano Oil) is essential, not adjunctive.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Not documented.

**Alternative reading:** EEN may work primarily by eliminating dietary emulsifiers, food additives, and antigens that drive inflammation, rather than by bowel rest or bacterial starvation. The exclusion of ALL normal food may be the active ingredient.

## Assessment

Thompson's mechanistic model is coherent but unproven. The three-mechanism framing (rest + starve + supplement) is rhetorically effective but each component's contribution is unknown. The protocol's strength is that it works empirically; the mechanism is secondary.
