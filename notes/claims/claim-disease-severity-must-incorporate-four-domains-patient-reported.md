# acg-uc2019-2: Disease severity must incorporate four domains: patient-reported outcomes, inflammatory burden, disease course, and disease impact -- not just bowel frequency

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Disease severity must incorporate four domains: patient-reported outcomes, inflammatory burden, disease course, and disease impact -- not just bowel frequency

## Evidence

The guideline proposes a new ACG UC Activity Index (Table 4) that stratifies into Remission, Mild, Moderate-Severe, and Fulminant using stools/day, blood in stools, urgency, hemoglobin, ESR/CRP, fecal calprotectin, endoscopic Mayo subscore, and UCEIS. The Truelove and Witts criteria (1955) remain the foundation for defining ASUC (≥6 bloody stools/day plus systemic toxicity). The guideline also endorses the UCEIS (validated 2012) over the Mayo endoscopic subscore for quantifying endoscopic severity, noting superior intra- and inter-observer reliability. Poor prognostic factors (Table 8) include: age <40 at diagnosis, extensive colitis, severe endoscopic disease (Mayo 3, UCEIS ≥7), hospitalization for colitis, elevated CRP, and low serum albumin.

## Assessment

The four-domain severity concept is the guideline's most important conceptual advance. It explicitly says: a patient with "mild" current symptoms who was hospitalized last year and is steroid-dependent should be treated as moderate-severe. This closes a dangerous gap where rigid severity definitions let high-risk patients languish on step therapy. The UCEIS endorsement is forward-looking (most trials still use Mayo) but practically helpful for endoscopists.
