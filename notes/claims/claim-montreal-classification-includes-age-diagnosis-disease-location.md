# yamada-cddx64-4: The Montreal Classification is the standard CD classification system, categorizing by age at diagnosis, disease location, and disease behavior

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch64 Crohns Disease Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

The Montreal classification includes age at diagnosis, disease location, and the presence of perianal disease and is the most recognized CD classification in clinical research.

## Evidence

Montreal Classification components: Age (A1 <16, A2 17-40, A3 >40), Location (L1 ileal, L2 colonic, L3 ileocolonic, L4 isolated upper disease [modifier added to L1-L3]), Behavior (B1 non-stricturing non-penetrating, B2 stricturing, B3 penetrating), with "p" modifier for perianal disease. Example: A2L1B2 = diagnosed age 17-40, ileal disease, stricturing behavior. The chapter acknowledges that "the Montreal classification does not capture the nuances of phenotypic differences between patients that may reflect differences in pathophysiology or inflammatory pathways." Disease behavior changes over time: at diagnosis, 56-81% have inflammatory phenotype (B1), ~5% stricturing (B2), ~25% penetrating (B3); over 20 years, many progress to B2/B3, especially with ileal involvement.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. Montreal is the universally accepted classification in CD research.

## Stakes

Classification drives treatment decisions. B1 disease may be managed with conventional therapy; B2/B3 disease or perianal disease (p modifier) generally warrants earlier biologic therapy. The behavior modifier is dynamic -- a patient classified as B1 at diagnosis may develop a stricture years later and become B2, which is why ongoing reassessment is needed.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** There is growing recognition that Montreal is insufficient for precision medicine. Genetic, serological, and microbiome subtyping may eventually supplement or replace anatomic classification. The chapter notes: "it is widely accepted that the Montreal classification does not capture the nuances of phenotypic differences."

## Assessment

Montreal is practical and widely used but is essentially descriptive, not mechanistic. The chapter's acknowledgment of its limitations is appropriate. The important clinical point is that behavior (B1 vs B2/B3) matters more for prognosis and treatment than location (L1 vs L2 vs L3).
