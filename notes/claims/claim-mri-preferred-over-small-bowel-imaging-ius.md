# ecco-diag2019-4: MRI is preferred over CT for small bowel imaging; IUS has comparable diagnostic accuracy and is radiation-free

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

MRI is preferred over CT for small bowel imaging; IUS has comparable diagnostic accuracy and is radiation-free

## Evidence

CT and MRI have comparable high sensitivity/specificity for small intestinal lesions. Direct comparison: IUS sensitivity 94%, specificity 97% vs. MRE 96%, 94% (Castiglione 2013, 234 patients). IUS less accurate for disease extent (r=0.69) but high concordance for location (κ=0.81). **METRIC trial** (Taylor 2018, 284 patients): MRE significantly greater sensitivity for small bowel disease extent (80% vs. 70%, p=0.027) and presence (97% vs. 92%, p=0.025); MRE specificity 95% vs. IUS 81%. For colonic disease, IUS had superior sensitivity (67% vs. 47%). Due to absence of radiation, MRI should be preferred over CT, particularly in young patients.

## Assessment

The METRIC trial provides the most rigorous comparison — MRE is better for small bowel extent (80% vs. 70%), but the difference may not justify MRE in all cases. IUS may suffice for confirming active disease; MRE is better for detailed mapping. The IUS disparity between European and North American guidelines reflects local expertise and reimbursement structures more than evidence.
