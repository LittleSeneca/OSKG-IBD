# acg-cd2018-10: Metronidazole, ciprofloxacin, and antimycobacterial therapy should NOT be used as primary therapy for luminal CD (R13-R15)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Metronidazole, ciprofloxacin, and antimycobacterial therapy should NOT be used as primary therapy for luminal CD (R13-R15)

## Evidence

Despite the rationale of targeting bacterial triggers, antibiotics have not demonstrated consistent benefit for luminal CD. Metronidazole was not superior to placebo (Sutherland 1991). Ciprofloxacin was equivalent to mesalamine (which itself is no better than placebo — see R11). Antimycobacterial therapy (multi-drug regimens targeting *M. avium paratuberculosis*) has been conclusively negative across multiple trials and meta-analyses. Antibiotics do have roles in pyogenic complications (abscesses) and postoperative prevention (see later sections).

## Assessment

These three negative recommendations close the door on a therapeutic avenue that many patients and some clinicians continue to pursue. The antimycobacterial recommendation is particularly important — the MAP hypothesis drove decades of investigation and occasional off-label treatment. This guideline says: it's over, the evidence is negative.
