# yamada-uc63-1: Combined oral and rectal 5-ASA is the most effective induction strategy for mild-moderate UC

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch63 UC Clinical Manifestations and Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

Based on a network metaanalysis of 48 induction trials including 8020 participants... combined oral and rectal 5-ASA and high-dose mesalamine (>3 g/day) were ranked most efficacious in inducing remission.

## Evidence

Network meta-analysis of 48 trials. The comparative efficacy data (Figure 63.16) shows combined oral+rectal 5-ASA with OR 0.21 (95% CI 0.11-0.40) vs placebo -- the strongest effect of any 5-ASA strategy. Standard-dose mesalamine (2-3 g/day) is the recommended starting point for most patients per AGA guidelines. Once-daily dosing is recommended over divided dosing to improve adherence. For maintenance, standard-dose mesalamine is sufficient; high-dose offers no additional maintenance benefit.

## Stakes

5-ASA is the cornerstone of UC therapy. Optimizing 5-ASA delivery (combined routes, adequate dosing, once-daily administration, dose escalation guided by fecal calprotectin) could reduce the number of patients who escalate to immunomodulators or biologics unnecessarily.

## Assessment

This is the strongest section of the chapter. The authors present a data-driven algorithm (Figure 63.15) with specific evidence rankings. The fecal calprotectin-guided dose optimization study (escalating mesalamine for elevated FC >50 in symptomatic remission reduced clinical relapse) is a practical application of treat-to-target principles to 5-ASA therapy. The reminder that "patients who fail one optimized mesalamine do not benefit from switching to equivalent doses of another mesalamine" counters a common clinical impulse to cycle through 5-ASA formulations.
