# ecco-uc2022-10: Vedolizumab is strongly recommended for induction with low-quality evidence; the weak recommendation favoring vedolizumab over adalimumab (from the VARSITY trial) is the guideline's most distinctive feature

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Vedolizumab is strongly recommended for induction with low-quality evidence; the weak recommendation favoring vedolizumab over adalimumab (from the VARSITY trial) is the guideline's most distinctive feature

## Evidence

Two placebo-controlled RCTs (n=620): clinical remission RR 2.14 (1.03-4.43), clinical response RR 1.51 (0.99-2.29) — not significant. GEMINI I: week 6 endoscopic remission 40.9% vs 24.8% (p=0.001). Heterogeneity between the two RCTs drove inconsistency ratings down. SAEs: RR 0.71 (0.39-1.30), favorable safety profile confirmed in large cohort studies (Kirchgesner 2020: lower serious infection rates vs anti-TNF).

**VARSITY trial (Sands 2019):** The only head-to-head biologic trial in UC. Vedolizumab vs adalimumab over 52 weeks: clinical remission RR 1.39 (1.10-1.76), endoscopic remission RR 1.43 (1.17-1.75), clinical response RR 1.46 (1.29-1.67) — all favoring vedolizumab. Corticosteroid-free clinical remission was numerically lower with vedolizumab (RR 0.58; 0.32-1.05), but evidence quality was low due to sparse data and wide confidence intervals. Dose escalation was not permitted in VARSITY, despite evidence of improved outcomes with dose escalation for both drugs.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
This is ECCO's unique contribution — no other major society guideline makes an explicit head-to-head recommendation between biologics. G2 (ACG 2019) predates VARSITY. G5 (AGA 2020) references VARSITY in its evidence review but does not make a vedolizumab-over-adalimumab recommendation, instead conditionally recommending infliximab or vedolizumab before adalimumab in biologic-naive patients using a broader evidence synthesis. The ECCO R19 is the most "European" feature of the guideline — it formalizes the European preference for gut-selective therapy first.

## Assessment

The VARSITY-based R19 is the guideline's most important and most debated recommendation. It has several limitations acknowledged by the panel: (a) no dose escalation was permitted — real-world adalimumab optimization (weekly dosing, combination with immunomodulator) might close the gap; (b) the corticosteroid-free remission paradox (numerically favoring adalimumab) complicates the narrative; (c) VARSITY compared vedolizumab to adalimumab, not infliximab, and infliximab showed superiority to adalimumab in network meta-analyses — so the vedolizumab-over-adalimumab finding cannot be extrapolated to vedolizumab-over-infliximab. Nevertheless, for anti-TNF-naive patients where the choice is between vedolizumab and adalimumab, VARSITY provides the only head-to-head data in UC and the direction is clear. The weak strength of the recommendation appropriately reflects the limitations.
