# yamada-uc63-4: Vedolizumab offers gut-selective immunosuppression with a favorable safety profile

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch63 UC Clinical Manifestations and Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

Vedolizumab suppresses inflammation in the gastrointestinal tract without inducing the systemic immunosuppression that characterizes the anti-alpha4 integrin monoclonal antibody natalizumab. This gut-selective targeting of inflammation potentially offers a distinct advantage of treating intestinal mucosal inflammation while avoiding off-target side-effects seen with TNF-alpha antagonists.

## Evidence

GEMINI trial: clinical response at week 6 in 47.1% vs 25.5% placebo (p<0.001). Maintenance at week 52: remission in 42-45% (vedolizumab q8wk/q4wk) vs 16% placebo. Safety: serious infection rates similar to placebo (4.3 vs 3.8 per 100 PY). PML reported only once in a patient with concurrent AIDS. Immunogenicity is low (~5%). Efficacy is higher in anti-TNF-naive patients.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for safety advantage and moderate efficacy. MEDIUM for positioning relative to anti-TNF -- vedolizumab has a slower onset of action and generally lower efficacy in anti-TNF-experienced patients b

## Stakes

Vedolizumab's gut selectivity makes it particularly attractive for elderly patients, those with infection risk, cancer history, or concerns about systemic immunosuppression. The slower onset means it is not appropriate for hospitalized severe UC (where infliximab is preferred).

## Assessment

The vedolizumab section correctly emphasizes the gut-selectivity advantage. The absence of a boxed warning is clinically meaningful. The authors note that "there is less evidence in support of concomitant immunomodulator therapy improving treatment outcomes with vedolizumab in ulcerative colitis" -- this is an important distinction from anti-TNF, where combination therapy is proven to enhance efficacy.
