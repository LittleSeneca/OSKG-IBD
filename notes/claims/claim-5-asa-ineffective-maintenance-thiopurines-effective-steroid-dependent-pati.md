# ecco-cd2020-7: 5-ASA is ineffective for maintenance; thiopurines are effective for steroid-dependent patients but not for early disease (R2.1-R2.3)

**Source:** [[ECCO Crohn's Medical 2020 - Torres]] — Torres et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Crohn's Disease: Medical Management (2020)

## The Claim

5-ASA is ineffective for maintenance; thiopurines are effective for steroid-dependent patients but not for early disease (R2.1-R2.3)

## Evidence

**5-ASA maintenance:** 11 placebo-controlled trials (1-4 g/day, 4-36 months): no statistically significant benefit (RR 1.03; 95% CI 0.92-1.16). Safety data were very sparse.

**Thiopurines for steroid-dependent patients:** Meta-analysis of 6 trials (n=489): AZA superior to placebo for maintenance of remission (RR 1.19; 95% CI 1.05-1.34). However, serious AEs were significantly higher (9.0% vs. 2.9%; RR 2.45; 95% CI 1.22-4.90): pancreatitis, leukopenia, nausea, allergic reactions, infections.

**Early thiopurine introduction:** The AZTEC trial (n=131, recent diagnosis <8 weeks): no significant benefit for clinical remission at 76 weeks (RR 1.27; 95% CI 0.94-1.72) or sustained corticosteroid-free remission (RR 1.21; 95% CI 0.79-1.84). Serious AEs trended higher (20.6% vs. 11.1%; RR 1.85).

**Long-term thiopurine continuation (R2.10):** Meta-analysis of 4 trials (n=215): discontinuation of AZA increased clinical relapse risk (RR 2.39; 95% CI 1.38-4.13). Trend towards fewer serious AEs with discontinuation but not significant (RR 0.32; 95% CI 0.04-2.92). Data beyond 18 months are lacking.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
This is the most dramatic area of divergence between ECCO and ACG. G1 (ACG 2018, R46) also recommends against 5-ASA maintenance (strong, moderate). However, G1 does NOT have a recommendation against early thiopurine introduction (R2.3 is unique to G8). G1's thiopurine recommendations (R20-R21) are broadly pro-thiopurine for steroid-sparing and maintenance without the early-disease caveat. G8's R2.3 reflects the negative AZTEC trial and represents a more restrained European position — ironic given Europe's historically greater thiopurine use. G6 (AGA 2021) is likely to further de-emphasize thiopurine monotherapy in favor of early biologic use.

## Assessment

R2.3 (against early thiopurine introduction) is the most surprising recommendation in the guideline. It directly contradicts traditional European practice where thiopurines were the default first-line immunomodulator. The AZTEC trial, while negative, was underpowered and studied a low-risk population. This recommendation may represent an overcorrection. However, combined with R2.10 (continue thiopurines once on them), the message is nuanced: don't start thiopurines early in low-risk patients, but once patients are on them and in remission, continuing is better than stopping.
