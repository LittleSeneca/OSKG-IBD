# ecco-uc2022-12: Ustekinumab is strongly recommended for induction — the first major UC guideline to incorporate it, with efficacy in both biologic-naive and biologic-exposed patients

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Ustekinumab is strongly recommended for induction — the first major UC guideline to incorporate it, with efficacy in both biologic-naive and biologic-exposed patients

## Evidence

Single phase 3 RCT (UNIFI, Sands 2019). Ustekinumab 6 mg/kg IV induction: clinical remission 15.5% vs 5.3% placebo (RR 2.91; 1.72-4.94), clinical response 61.8% vs 31.3% (RR 1.97; 1.64-2.37), endoscopic improvement 27.0% vs 13.8% (RR 1.96; 1.41-2.72). Of randomized patients, 51.1% had prior biologic failure (16.6% had failed both anti-TNF and vedolizumab). Benefit vs placebo was observed regardless of biologic exposure history. IBDQ score change from baseline: 35.0 vs 16.16 (p<0.001). Fecal calprotectin reduction: -1368.26 vs +17.92 (p<0.001). SAEs: 5.2% vs 7.9% (RR 0.67; 0.39-1.17). Network meta-analyses: no statistical difference vs anti-TNF or tofacitinib in biologic-naive patients, possible benefit over adalimumab or vedolizumab in anti-TNF-exposed patients.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019) predates ustekinumab's UC approval (October 2019) by 8 months — this is a major temporal gap. G5 (AGA 2020) was published after UNIFI but before ustekinumab received its formal UC recommendation in most guidelines. The ECCO guideline is the first to give ustekinumab a full GRADE-based strong recommendation, making it the most current advanced therapy algorithm.

## Assessment

The 15.5% clinical remission rate is modest in absolute terms but must be interpreted in context: this was a heavily biologic-refractory population. The drug's favorable safety profile (no increased infection signal, minimal immunogenicity) and convenient maintenance dosing (SC every 8-12 weeks) make it an attractive option, particularly for patients who have failed multiple mechanisms. The guideline appropriately doesn't position ustekinumab relative to other biologics — the evidence doesn't support a ranking — but its inclusion expands the treatment armamentarium at a time when sequencing decisions are increasingly complex.
