# ecco-uc2022-1: Disease severity is a continuum; treatment is stratified by both severity and extent — but trial populations and real-world patients differ fundamentally

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Attempts to define disease severity are widely used in setting clinical trial inclusion criteria and can be measured according to several different definitions. Trial populations will inevitably vary, and we reflect the continuum of disease severity by having the moderate disease category span both broad categories.

## Evidence

The guideline divides recommendations into two working groups: mildly-to-moderately active disease and moderately-to-severely active disease, with explicit acknowledgment that these divisions are "somewhat arbitrary, partially overlapping, and inconsistently defined." Disease extent is classified using the Montreal system: proctitis (rectum only), left-sided UC (distal to splenic flexure), extensive UC (proximal to splenic flexure). The panel notes that disease distribution can change over time and proximal extension is a negative prognostic marker.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The continuum model accurately reflects clinical reality and avoids the false precision of rigid severity scores. The acknowledgment that trial populations differ from clinic patients is honest 

## Stakes

Treatment decisions based on rigid severity definitions can undertreat high-risk patients with "mild" current symptoms or overtreat patients with a single severe episode but limited cumulative burden. The continuum model pushes clinicians toward individualized assessment.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No formal disagreement — the continuum concept is widely accepted. However, payers and formularies often require strict severity definitions (e.g., Mayo score cutoffs) for biologic approval, creating tension between guideline philosophy and real-world access.

**Alternative reading:** The ACG 2019 guideline addresses this differently — by proposing a unified four-domain severity classification (PROs, inflammatory burden, disease course, disease impact) rather than simply acknowledging a continuum. The ACG approach is more prescriptive; the ECCO approach is more descriptive.

## Assessment

The continuum model is intellectually honest but operationally vague. The ACG's four-domain framework gives clinicians more concrete guidance for identifying high-risk patients who need early escalation. ECCO's approach risks leaving the decision entirely to clinician judgment, which may disadvantage patients whose physicians are less aggressive about escalation.
