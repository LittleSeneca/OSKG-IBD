# acg-cd2018-1: Crohn's disease is a chronic, progressive, destructive disease — only 20-30% have an indolent course

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Summary Statements 4, 6-8. Not GRADE-rated.

## Evidence

The majority of patients (70-80%) will develop intestinal complications (stricture, fistula, abscess) or require surgery. At diagnosis, 56-81% have inflammatory (B1) disease behavior; 5-25% present with stricturing (B2) or penetrating (B3) disease. Among those with initial B1 behavior, cumulative risk of developing an intestinal complication is 51% at 20 years (Olmsted County data). The Lémann index quantifies cumulative bowel damage. Median cumulative bowel resection length was 64 cm in patients with ≥1 resection.

**High-risk features for progression:** young age at diagnosis, initial extensive bowel involvement, ileal/ileocolonic involvement, perianal/severe rectal disease, penetrating or stenosis phenotype at diagnosis, visceral adiposity.

**Natural history key statistics:**
- 10-year cumulative risk of major abdominal surgery: 40-55% (possibly decreased to ~30% in biologic era)
- 10-year risk of second resection: ~35% (possibly ~30% in recent studies)
- 5-year symptomatic postoperative recurrence: ~50%
- Standardized mortality ratio: 1.4 (slightly increased, particularly GI disease and GI cancer)
- Up to 80% hospitalized at some point; annual hospitalization rate decreases over time
- Perianal fistulizing disease in ~25% of patients (cumulative risk 26% at 20 years)

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G6 (AGA 2021) and G8 (ECCO 2020) both acknowledge the progressive nature but G8 places greater emphasis on early aggressive therapy to alter natural history. The ACG 2018 guideline's natural history data is pre-biologic-era-heavy; newer population-based data in G6 may show lower surgery rates.

## Assessment

The natural history data is the most important section for understanding *why* treatment matters. The 51% complication rate at 20 years for initially inflammatory disease is the number that justifies early effective therapy. However, the biologic-era surgery risk reduction data is noted only tentatively ("may have decreased to 30%") — later guidelines with more mature data should strengthen this claim.
