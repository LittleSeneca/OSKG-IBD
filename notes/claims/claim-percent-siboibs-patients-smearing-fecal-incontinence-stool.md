# pimentel-ch2-6: Fecal incontinence affects up to 30% of SIBO/IBS patients, particularly due to altered stool consistency

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch2 Gut Anatomy and MMC]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 2

## The Claim

Up to 30 percent of SIBO/IBS patients have smearing or fecal incontinence... The stool is more like peanut butter, and if you've had peanut butter on your hands, you know how hard it is to remove. So, if stool like that passes through the anus, it's hard to clean and sometimes it cannot be completely cleaned by just wiping after bowel movements.

## Evidence

Clinical observation at Cedars-Sinai. The altered stool form in SIBO is attributed to incomplete digestion and bacterial fermentation products affecting stool consistency.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM (clinical observation, not published data). The mechanism is plausible: SIBO-induced malabsorption produces softer, stickier stool; rectal hypersensitivity (also noted in this chapter) compound

## Stakes

Fecal incontinence is among the most distressing IBS symptoms and is under-discussed. Normalizing it as a SIBO symptom rather than a separate problem is clinically valuable.

**Who disagrees:** Not documented.

## Assessment

The 30% figure is from Pimentel's clinical experience and should be validated in epidemiological studies. The peanut butter analogy is vivid and clinically useful for patient education.

**Strongest section:** The MMC mechanism and the 2002 finding of 60% reduced housekeeping waves. This is Pimentel's signature contribution and the physiological basis for the entire SIBO treatment model.

**Weakest section:** The bile acid diarrhea connection. While mechanistically plausible, the claim that SIBO is "one of the main causes" of BAD is stated without supporting data on prevalence or treatment response.

**Cross-reference note:** This chapter's MMC framework directly underpins the meal spacing rule in the LFE diet (Ch8), the prokinetic pillar of SIBO treatment (Ch6), and the anti-vinculin autoimmunity mechanism (Ch4). The five natural defenses framework explains why proton pump inhibitors (reducing stomach acid), opioids (slowing motility), and bowel surgery (removing the ileocecal valve) are all SIBO risk factors.
