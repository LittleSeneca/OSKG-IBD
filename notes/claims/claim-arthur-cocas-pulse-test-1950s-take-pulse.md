# thompson-een-7: The Coca Pulse Test can detect food allergies by monitoring heart rate after food ingestion

**Source:** [[Thompson 2013 - Elemental Diet Protocol]] — Thompson — The IBD Remission Diet (2013)

## The Claim

Dr. Arthur Coca's pulse test (1950s): take pulse before eating, then 3 times at 30-minute intervals after eating a single food. If pulse increases beyond normal range (typically >16 BPM variation), food allergy is suspected. Thompson includes the full procedure and claims the test detected triggers for conditions including colitis, GI bleeding, abdominal pain, and many others.

## Evidence

Coca's clinical observations from the 1950s. No modern validation. Thompson acknowledges limitations: not effective for hay fever, asthma, eczema, or contact dermatitis; invalidated by caffeine, smoking, or beta-blockers.

## Stakes

If patients rely on pulse testing instead of systematic symptom tracking, they may misclassify foods. False positives (pulse increase from non-allergic causes) could lead to unnecessary restriction. False negatives could lead to continued consumption of trigger foods.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The entire field of allergy/immunology. Food allergy diagnosis now relies on oral food challenge (gold standard), skin prick testing, and serum IgE testing. Pulse testing is not recognized by any allergy society.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

Including the Coca Pulse Test undermines Thompson's credibility. The rest of the book is grounded in real clinical trial evidence and sensible clinical reasoning. The pulse test is pseudoscientific and should not be presented alongside validated methods without strong caveats about its lack of evidence.

## Protocol Summary

### The IBD Remission Diet Protocol

1. **Elemental Phase (2-12 weeks):** Exclusive Absorb Plus shakes (or equivalent semi-elemental formula) providing 100% of nutrition. Calories: 2000-4400/day depending on weight goals. Shakes consumed every 1.5-2 hours.

2. **Supplementation (concurrent):** Jini's Wild Oregano Oil Protocol (antimicrobial), high-dose probiotics (450+ billion CFU/day recommended), L-glutamine, vitamins/minerals. All supplements emptied into shakes.

3. **Food Reintroduction (variable duration):** Four-phase structured reintroduction. Phase 1: easiest-to-digest foods (cooked vegetables, broths, well-cooked fruits). Continue shakes as calorie base, gradually reducing as solid food increases. Test 1 food per 1-3 days.

4. **Maintenance:** Continued probiotic supplementation, identified safe foods as dietary foundation, gradual expansion of food variety as health improves.

**Strongest section:** The clinical trial evidence for EEN efficacy in Ch1 -- this is real, robust data that validates the entire approach.

**Weakest section:** The Coca Pulse Test -- inclusion of pseudoscientific allergy testing damages credibility of an otherwise evidence-aware book.

**Cross-reference note:** Thompson's elemental diet represents the most intensive dietary intervention in the IBD spectrum -- more restrictive than SCD, more medicalized than any other dietary approach, and with the strongest clinical trial evidence. The irony is that the protocol with the strongest evidence is also the least accessible (cost, compliance, social disruption). The gap between what works in trials (EEN) and what's sustainable (SCD, anti-inflammatory eating) is the central tension in IBD dietary therapy.
