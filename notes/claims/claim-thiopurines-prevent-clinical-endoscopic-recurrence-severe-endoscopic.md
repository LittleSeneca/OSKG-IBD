# acg-cd2018-27: Thiopurines prevent clinical and endoscopic recurrence but not severe endoscopic recurrence; anti-TNF agents are the most effective prophylaxis for high-risk patients (R57-R59)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Thiopurines prevent clinical and endoscopic recurrence but not severe endoscopic recurrence; anti-TNF agents are the most effective prophylaxis for high-risk patients (R57-R59)

## Evidence

Thiopurines: Cochrane analysis — reduced clinical recurrence (RR 0.59, NNT 7) and severe endoscopic recurrence (RR 0.64, NNT 4) vs. placebo, superior to mesalamine. However, one meta-analysis found no significant reduction in clinical recurrence (RR 0.88, p=0.53 at year 1). Adverse events leading to withdrawal were higher with thiopurines (RR 2.57). Anti-TNF agents: Network meta-analysis of 21 controlled trials — anti-TNF monotherapy most effective intervention, reducing clinical relapse (RR 0.04) and endoscopic relapse (RR 0.01) vs. placebo. The PREVENT trial (Regueiro 2016) showed infliximab reduced endoscopic but not clinical recurrence. Authors recommend anti-TNF as first-line prophylaxis for high-risk patients.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
This is the area of greatest anticipated divergence with G6 (AGA 2021) and G8 (ECCO 2020). The AGA 2017 surgical resection guideline (Nguyen 2017, referenced) provides a more detailed algorithm. The PREVENT trial's negative primary endpoint (clinical recurrence) despite positive endoscopic findings is a major point of discussion.

## Assessment

The postoperative section is the most rapidly evolving area in CD management. R58 (anti-TNF within 4 weeks) is based on limited data — primarily one small RCT (Regueiro 2009) and the international PREVENT trial. The "low" evidence quality is honest. The paradox of PREVENT (endoscopic benefit without clinical benefit) raises the question: does treating endoscopic recurrence prevent clinical recurrence, or are we overtreating? R59 (combination therapy in postoperative setting) is essentially extrapolation from luminal disease — there are no postoperative combination trials.
