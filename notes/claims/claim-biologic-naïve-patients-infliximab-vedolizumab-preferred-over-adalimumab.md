# aga-uc2020-3: In biologic-naïve patients, infliximab or vedolizumab are preferred over adalimumab — based on network meta-analysis and the VARSITY head-to-head trial

**Source:** [[AGA UC 2020 - Feuerstein]] — Feuerstein et al. — AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines: Ulcerative Colitis (2020)

## The Claim

In biologic-naïve patients, infliximab or vedolizumab are preferred over adalimumab — based on network meta-analysis and the VARSITY head-to-head trial

## Evidence

VARSITY trial (vedolizumab vs. adalimumab head-to-head): clinical remission at week 52 — 34.2% vedolizumab vs. 24.3% adalimumab (RR 1.41; 1.10-1.81) among biologic-naïve patients. Network meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (3747 biologic-naïve patients): infliximab superior to adalimumab (OR 2.10; 1.16-3.79). Tofacitinib trials were excluded from NMA because they used a stricter outcome definition (rectal bleeding score of zero vs. 0 or 1 in other trials). Important caveat: in the NMA-included trials, anti-TNF treatment was not optimized to target drug concentrations — the guideline acknowledges that "the efficacy of infliximab, adalimumab and golimumab may be comparable in patients who achieve adequate drug concentrations."

## Assessment

The AGA's agent-specific ranking is methodologically sophisticated but clinically fragile. Network meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials is sensitive to differences in trial populations, outcome definitions, and concomitant medication use. The tofacitinib exclusion from NMA illustrates the problem — a different outcome definition was enough to disqualify an entire drug class. The VARSITY head-to-head data are more reliable than the NMA-derived ORs, but VARSITY only compared two agents. The TDM caveat is the key: if you achieve therapeutic drug levels, the anti-TNFs may be equivalent. This means the guideline's ranking may reflect dosing inadequacy rather than intrinsic drug superiority. The ACG's class-based approach is less informative but avoids over-interpreting fragile indirect comparisons.
