# acg-cd2018-20: Infliximab is the best-studied agent for perianal fistulas; combination with seton placement and antibiotics improves outcomes (R35-R43)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Infliximab is the best-studied agent for perianal fistulas; combination with seton placement and antibiotics improves outcomes (R35-R43)

## Evidence

Fistulas occur in ~1/3 of CD patients, with perianal being the most common location. Classification: simple (distal to dentate line, single tract) vs. complex (transsphincteric, suprasphincteric, multiple tracts). Key management principles:
- **Exclude abscess before immunosuppression** (cross-sectional imaging). If present: drain first.
- **Simple fistulas:** May respond to fistulotomy or antibiotics alone. No treatment needed for asymptomatic simple fistulas.
- **Complex fistulas:** Combined medical-surgical approach. Seton placement + anti-TNF (infliximab best evidence). Seton + infliximab demonstrated better healing, longer closure duration, and lower recurrence.
- **Internal fistulas:** Enteroenteric fistulas may be asymptomatic and not require surgery. Major symptomatic fistulas (gastrocolic, coloduodenal) often require surgery if medical therapy fails.
- **Rectovaginal fistulas:** Medical therapy first (immunomodulator ± anti-TNF), then surgical repair (fistula excision + mucosal advancement flap) when mucosa is healed.

Infliximab is the only anti-TNF studied in a prospective RCT for fistula closure (Present 1999, Sands 2004 — ACCENT II). Adalimumab fistula data are from *post hoc* analyses and secondary endpoints. Certolizumab, vedolizumab, and ustekinumab fistula data are from *post hoc* analyses only — no controlled studies demonstrating unequivocal benefit.

## Assessment

This is the most clinically detailed section of the guideline, reflecting the complexity of fistula management. The emphasis on combined medical-surgical care (R42-R43) is crucial: anti-TNF therapy without adequate source control can be dangerous. The evidence hierarchy for fistula efficacy is: infliximab (RCT-level) > adalimumab (secondary endpoint) > certolizumab/vedolizumab/ustekinumab (*post hoc* only). The tacrolimus recommendation (R39) for short-term use is notable — it's the one exception to R31's blanket rejection of calcineurin inhibitors for CD.
