# gottschall-ch910-4: Vitamin and mineral supplementation is necessary due to malabsorption -- specific B12, folate, calcium, and fat-soluble vitamin concerns

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch9-10 Implementing the SCD]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 9-10

## The Claim

Most people with chronic intestinal disorders also suffer from malabsorption and, consequently, are malnourished.

## Evidence

General knowledge about malabsorption in intestinal disease. The folate-B12 warning reflects the clinical pearl that high folate can mask B12 deficiency hematologically while neurological damage progresses.

## Stakes

Nutritional deficiencies compound the morbidity of IBD. Gottschall's emphasis on supplementation alongside diet is an important corrective to the common assumption that "eating healthy" is sufficient during malabsorptive states.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** The specific dosages are conservative by modern standards. Vitamin D recommendations are now much higher (1000-4000 IU daily is common). The B12 injection preference (vs. high-dose oral) reflects 1994 practice; modern evidence supports high-dose oral B12 as effective for most patients.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

The supplementation guidance is sensible for 1994 but dated. The key principle -- that diet alone cannot correct deficiencies during active malabsorption -- remains valid and is often overlooked in dietary intervention protocols.
