# acg-uc2019-1: UC diagnosis requires endoscopic confirmation with histology and exclusion of infectious mimics -- C. difficile testing is mandatory, serologic antibody testing is useless

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

UC diagnosis requires endoscopic confirmation with histology and exclusion of infectious mimics -- C. difficile testing is mandatory, serologic antibody testing is useless

## Evidence

The prevalence of CDI among newly diagnosed or relapsing IBD ranges from 5% to 47%, with a 4-fold increase in mortality when CDI complicates UC. PCR and ELISA testing are standard. Comprehensive PCR-based multiplex pathogen panels are increasingly used, but C. diff remains the predominant infectious determinant of adverse outcomes. pANCA testing has pooled sensitivity too low for clinical utility, and no serologic panel predicts disease course with sufficient accuracy.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 (AGA UC 2020) similarly recommends infectious testing at diagnosis but does not make a specific C. diff recommendation. G9 (ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022) also emphasizes exclusion of enteric infections before diagnosing UC.

## Assessment

These are three of the few "strong" recommendations with "very low" evidence quality -- a paradox that reflects clinical consensus trumping formal evidence: everyone agrees you should test for C. diff and skip pANCA, even though no RCT will ever randomize patients to "no C. diff testing." The strong/very-low combination is appropriately used for actions where the consequences of NOT doing them (missed C. diff with immunosuppression initiation) are catastrophic.
