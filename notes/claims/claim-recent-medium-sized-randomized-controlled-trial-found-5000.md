# pimentel-ch9-5: L-glutamine (5000 mg TID) improved IBS symptoms and intestinal permeability in one RCT

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch9 Refractory SIBO]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 9

## The Claim

A recent medium-sized randomized controlled trial found 5000 mg doses (about 2 teaspoons) of L-glutamine three times daily improved IBS symptoms and intestinal permeability, the so-called leaky gut. Larger trials are needed to confirm this data. L-glutamine is a safe supplement and an option for IBS treatment.

## Evidence

One medium-sized RCT. Mechanism: L-glutamine is an amino acid that may heal the intestinal lining.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

## Assessment

L-glutamine is low-risk and mechanistically plausible. The single positive RCT is encouraging but needs replication. For patients seeking a safe, low-cost supplement with some evidence, L-glutamine is a reasonable option, but expectations should be modest.

## Key Differential Diagnoses (condensed)

Pimentel lists conditions that can mimic or overlap with SIBO:

| Condition | Key Distinguishing Features | Diagnostic Test |
|-----------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Celiac disease | Bloating, diarrhea, fatigue, iron-deficiency anemia | Anti-tTG blood test + duodenal biopsy |
| IBD (Crohn's/UC) | Bloody diarrhea, fever, weight loss, night sweats | Colonoscopy + biopsy |
| Microscopic colitis | Watery diarrhea 5-15x/day, women >60 | Colonoscopy + biopsy (normal gross appearance) |
| Pancreatic insufficiency | Greasy/oily stool, significant abdominal pain | Fecal elastase (low); imaging |
| Bile acid malabsorption | Watery diarrhea; history of ileal resection/Crohn's | SeHCAT (unavailable in US); empiric trial of bile acid sequestrants |
| Pelvic floor dysfunction | Constipation, incomplete evacuation, straining | Anorectal manometry |
| Ehlers-Danlos syndrome | Hypermobile joints, dislocations, visceroptosis | Clinical diagnosis (no genetic test for hypermobile type) |
| Scleroderma | Skin tightening, Raynaud's, highest anti-vinculin levels | Clinical + antibody panel |
| Mast cell activation syndrome | Multiple allergies, flushing, multi-system symptoms | Mast cell biomarkers; biopsy |
| SIFO | Similar to SIBO but antifungal-responsive | Endoscopic jejunal aspirate + fungal culture |

## Chapter 9 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Peppermint oil for IBS symptoms | MEDIUM-HIGH | One positive RCT; symptom management only |
| Antidepressants: no FDA approval for IBS | HIGH (factual) / MEDIUM-HIGH (harm analysis) | ACG guideline still recommends tricyclics |
| >50% IBD patients with persistent symptoms have SIBO | MEDIUM-HIGH | Needs independent validation |
| SIFO: stool tests invalid | MEDIUM | Emerging concept; field still developing |
| L-glutamine for IBS | LOW-MEDIUM | Single RCT; needs replication |

**Strongest section:** The SIBO-IBD overlap. This is an underrecognized clinical problem with immediate practice implications. If validated, it could change IBD management.

**Weakest section:** Some of the pharmacopeia entries (senna, cascara, triphala, stool softeners) are thinly evidenced and read as clinical opinion rather than evidence-based recommendations.
