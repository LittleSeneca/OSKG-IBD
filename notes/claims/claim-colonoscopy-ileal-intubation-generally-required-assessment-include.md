# yamada-cddx64-3: Ileocolonoscopy with biopsies is the primary diagnostic modality for CD; noncaseating granulomas are pathognomonic but present in only ~15% of patients

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch64 Crohns Disease Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

Colonoscopy with ileal intubation is generally required for the assessment of CD and should include biopsies for histopathology assessment to exclude infection and lymphoma and corroborate the severity of inflammation... Noncaseating granulomata are considered pathognomonic for CD in this setting, although they are seen in only about 15% of patients with CD.

## Evidence

Endoscopic findings: erythema, erosions, ulcerations (classified as aphthous, linear, stellate, or serpiginous), strictures. Endoscopic scoring: CDEIS (Crohn's Disease Endoscopic Index of Severity) and SES-CD (Simple Endoscopic Score for CD) are validated for clinical trials. Postoperative recurrence: Rutgeerts score (i0-i4) grades the ileocolonic anastomosis; i2 or higher predicts clinical recurrence and need for therapy. Histology: chronic inflammation markers include lymphoid aggregates, mixed acute/chronic infiltrates, crypt abscesses, neuronal hypertrophy, Paneth cell metaplasia. Granulomas can be found in both inflamed and uninflamed mucosa and should be distinguished from ruptured crypts. Transmural, focal/patchy inflammation is characteristic. "Creeping fat" (mesenteric fat wrapping around inflamed ileum) seen in surgical specimens. Upper endoscopy: advocated because up to 15% of adults with established CD have asymptomatic upper tract involvement (focal non-H. pylori gastritis, esophagitis, duodenitis).

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for endoscopic and histological features. MEDIUM for the 15% granuloma prevalence -- this varies by biopsy protocol (number of biopsies, whether serial sections are examined).

## Stakes

The low sensitivity of granulomas (15%) means their absence does not exclude CD. Conversely, their presence in a patient with colonic inflammation strongly favors CD over UC -- this can be the deciding factor in IBD-U cases. The distinction between a true granuloma and a ruptured crypt is a known pitfall in histopathology. The recommendation for upper endoscopy in suspected CD is not universally followed in practice but may identify subclinical upper tract involvement that confirms the diagnosis.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some pathologists argue that microgranulomas (not just well-formed granulomas) should be considered diagnostic. The 15% figure may underestimate true prevalence if serial sectioning and meticulous search protocols are used.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

The granuloma sensitivity problem illustrates a broader point about CD diagnosis: no single finding is pathognomonic in most patients. The diagnosis rests on a constellation of clinical, endoscopic, histological, and radiological findings, not on any one feature. The Rutgeerts score for postoperative recurrence is one of the most clinically useful endoscopic classification systems in IBD -- it directly guides therapeutic decisions.
