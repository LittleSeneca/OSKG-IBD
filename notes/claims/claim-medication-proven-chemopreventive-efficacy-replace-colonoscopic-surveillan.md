# acg-uc2019-17: No medication has proven chemopreventive efficacy to replace colonoscopic surveillance; 5-ASA and thiopurine chemoprevention data are inconsistent

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

No medication has proven chemopreventive efficacy to replace colonoscopic surveillance; 5-ASA and thiopurine chemoprevention data are inconsistent

## Evidence

Case-control and cohort studies suggest protective effects of 5-ASA and thiopurines for dysplasia/CRC, but results are inconsistent, and effect sizes diminish when adjusted for degree of inflammation. The guideline explicitly rejects the notion that medication can substitute for colonoscopic surveillance.

## Assessment

This is an important negative statement. Some clinicians have argued that patients in deep remission on biologics might need less frequent surveillance. The guideline rejects this: inflammation control may reduce risk but does not eliminate it, and surveillance remains mandatory.
