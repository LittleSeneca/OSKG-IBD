# yamada-uc63-5: Tofacitinib is the first oral JAK inhibitor approved for UC, with efficacy independent of prior anti-TNF exposure

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch63 UC Clinical Manifestations and Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

Rates of clinical remission and endoscopic improvement were significantly higher with 10 mg twice-daily dose of tofacitinib for induction, and significantly higher with both the 5 mg and 10 mg twice-daily dose of tofacitinib during maintenance.

## Evidence

OCTAVE Induction 1 and 2, OCTAVE Sustain (phase III program). Remission at induction: ~18.5% tofacitinib vs 8.2% placebo. Maintenance: 34.3% (5 mg) and 40.6% (10 mg) vs 11.1% placebo. Safety: herpes zoster rate is dose-dependent (6.6 per 100 PY at 10 mg bid vs 2.1 at 5 mg bid). No immunogenicity concerns (small molecule). A black box warning exists for thrombosis and mortality at 10 mg bid in rheumatoid arthritis patients with cardiovascular risk factors.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for UC efficacy. HIGH for shingles risk. MEDIUM for long-term safety (cardiovascular/thrombotic signals from RA population require ongoing monitoring).

## Stakes

JAK inhibitors are the first oral advanced therapy for UC. For patients averse to injections/infusions, they represent a paradigm shift. The comparable efficacy in anti-TNF-naive and experienced patients is a significant advantage over vedolizumab (which works better in naive patients). The shingles risk mandates vaccination (non-live recombinant zoster vaccine) before starting therapy.

## Assessment

The tofacitinib section appropriately emphasizes both the convenience advantage (oral) and the safety concerns (shingles, potential cardiovascular risk). The authors note that tofacitinib "should not be used alongside other immunomodulators" -- a pragmatic safety recommendation. The OCTAVE data is presented clearly with the dose-response relationship for both efficacy and herpes zoster risk.
