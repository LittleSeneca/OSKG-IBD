# yamada-cd64-7: Postoperative endoscopic recurrence is rapid and predictable; risk-stratified prophylaxis and 6-12 month surveillance colonoscopy are standard

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch64 Crohn's Disease Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

Endoscopic recurrence rates of 70-90% have been reported as early as 1 year after surgery.

## Evidence

Rutgeerts' prospective study (n=89). Anti-TNF for postoperative prevention: infliximab reduced endoscopic recurrence from 85% to 9% (p<0.001) in one RCT; the PREVENT trial showed infliximab associated with significantly less endoscopic recurrence at 76 weeks; the POCER study found adalimumab superior to AZA in high-risk patients. Mesalamine: NNT of 10 for clinical recurrence prevention (modest). Antibiotics: metronidazole reduced endoscopic recurrence from 75% to 52% (p=0.09) but intolerance limits use. Thiopurines: NNT of 13 and 7 for clinical and endoscopic recurrence respectively.

## Stakes

Without postoperative prophylaxis, the majority of Crohn's patients will have endoscopic recurrence within a year and 50% will require re-operation within 10 years. Risk-stratified prophylaxis with colonoscopic surveillance represents a systematic approach to breaking this cycle.

## Assessment

This is an excellent synthesis of postoperative management. The Rutgeerts score is explained with clinical context. The evidence hierarchy for each drug class is presented (anti-TNF > thiopurines > antibiotics > 5-ASA > budesonide/placebo). The risk-stratified algorithm (low risk: monitor; moderate: thiopurine +/- antibiotics; high: anti-TNF) is evidence-based and clinically actionable.
