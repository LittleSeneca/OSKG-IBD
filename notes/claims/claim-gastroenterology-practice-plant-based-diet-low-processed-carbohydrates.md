# chutkan-dysbiosis-4: IBD (Crohn's and UC) is linked to dysbiosis, and a plant-based diet with high-dose probiotics can achieve remission and reduce medication dependence

**Source:** [[The Microbiome Solution - Chutkan - Ch5 Dysbiosis]] — Chutkan — The Microbiome Solution (2015), Ch 5

## The Claim

In my gastroenterology practice, a plant-based diet low in processed carbohydrates and refined sugar, in combination with high-dose prescription-strength probiotics, has been extraordinarily successful for getting IBD into remission and reducing or eliminating the need for toxic medications.

## Evidence

Cites studies showing IBD patients have distinct stool bacterial profiles from healthy controls and that different Crohn's subtypes have different microbial changes. Notes that antibiotics, Western diet, and insufficient childhood microbial exposure are risk factors for IBD. The treatment claim is based on her clinical experience, not a controlled trial.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Not documented.

**Alternative reading:** The standard of care for moderate-to-severe IBD involves immunosuppressants and biologics because the inflammatory cascade, once established, may be self-perpetuating independent of the microbial trigger. Dietary and probiotic interventions may support remission maintenance but are less established for inducing remission in active disease. Chutkan's claim should be read as a hypothesis from clinical observation, not a proven treatment protocol.

## Assessment

This is the most consequential and least-supported claim in the chapter. Chutkan's clinical experience is valuable as a signal that non-pharmacological approaches deserve more research, but the specific claim of achieving remission and eliminating "toxic medications" through diet and probiotics alone should not be taken as established fact. The risk of undertreated IBD (strictures, fistulae, colorectal cancer) is severe enough that this claim requires cautious interpretation.
