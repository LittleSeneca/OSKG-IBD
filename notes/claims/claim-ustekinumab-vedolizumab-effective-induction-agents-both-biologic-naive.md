# ecco-cd2020-6: Ustekinumab and vedolizumab are effective induction agents for both biologic-naive and anti-TNF-experienced patients; no head-to-head data differentiate them (R1.8-R1.10)

**Source:** [[ECCO Crohn's Medical 2020 - Torres]] — Torres et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Crohn's Disease: Medical Management (2020)

## The Claim

Ustekinumab and vedolizumab are effective induction agents for both biologic-naive and anti-TNF-experienced patients; no head-to-head data differentiate them (R1.8-R1.10)

## Evidence

**Ustekinumab:** Meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (n=1,947): RR for clinical response 1.56 (95% CI 1.38-1.77); RR for clinical remission 1.76 (95% CI 1.40-2.22). Endoscopic improvement at 8 weeks: 47.7% vs. 29.9% (RR 1.60; 95% CI 1.13-2.26). No increase in AEs (RR 0.96) or serious AEs (RR 0.79). Anti-drug antibody formation <5%.

**Vedolizumab:** Meta-analysis of 3 RCTs (n=969): RR for clinical remission 2.01 (95% CI 1.50-2.71); RR for clinical response 1.55 (95% CI 1.14-2.11). Serious AE rate not different from placebo (RR 0.94).

**Indirect comparison (ustekinumab vs. vedolizumab):** One systematic review (n=1,249) found no significant difference for clinical response (RR 1.14; 95% CI 0.65-1.99) or clinical remission (RR 1.16; 95% CI 0.54-2.48) in anti-TNF-failure patients. Quality of evidence was **very low** for both outcomes.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G1 (ACG 2018, R27, R30) gives strong, high-quality evidence for both vedolizumab and ustekinumab induction. G8's "high" rating only for ustekinumab (not vedolizumab) reflects the larger evidence base for ustekinumab. G1 did not attempt an indirect comparison between the two agents. G8's R1.10 ("equally suggest either") is an honest acknowledgment of the evidence gap — clinicians must choose without comparative data.

## Assessment

R1.10 is both the most honest and most frustrating recommendation in the guideline. "Equally suggest either" is methodologically correct (very low-quality evidence for the comparison) but clinically unsatisfying. The choice between ustekinumab and vedolizumab drives real-world treatment decisions daily, and the guideline cannot help. The higher quality evidence for ustekinumab (high vs. moderate for vedolizumab) is an implicit signal, but the guideline stops short of recommending it preferentially. Surgery is explicitly mentioned as an alternative option in refractory patients — a characteristically European perspective.
