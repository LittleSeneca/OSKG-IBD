# yamada-ucdx63-5: Histological architectural distortion distinguishes chronic UC from acute self-limited colitis, and histological remission predicts lower relapse risk

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch63 Ulcerative Colitis Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

While there are no pathognomonic histological criteria for the diagnosis of ulcerative colitis, a primary distinction between ulcerative colitis and acute self-limited (infectious) colitis is architectural distortion.

## Evidence

In UC, the normal vertical ("test tube") alignment of glands is distorted, branched, and irregularly shaped. The lamina propria is expanded by lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils, and neutrophils. Neutrophilic infiltration produces crypt abscesses -- characteristic but not pathognomonic (seen in other inflammatory and infectious conditions). In quiescent UC, architectural distortion persists but acute inflammation (neutrophils, crypt abscesses) is absent. Regenerating epithelium produces cellular atypia that must be distinguished from dysplasia (stratification of nuclei, loss of polarity = neoplasia). A meta-analysis of 10 studies (757 patients in endoscopic remission): those with histological remission had 63% lower risk of clinical relapse vs persistent histological activity (annual relapse risk: 5.0% vs ~14% for endoscopic remission without histological remission).

## Stakes

The distinction between acute colitis (infection, self-limited) and chronic colitis (IBD) hinges on histology. If biopsies show acute inflammation without architectural distortion, the diagnosis is infectious colitis regardless of the endoscopic appearance. Conversely, architectural distortion in an asymptomatic patient establishes chronicity. The histological remission data raises the question of whether "histological remission" should become a formal treatment target -- currently it is not (STRIDE-II recommends endoscopic remission as the target), but the 63% risk reduction is compelling.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some pathologists argue that architectural distortion can occur in severe or prolonged infectious colitis, making the acute/chronic distinction less absolute than traditionally taught. The clinical context (duration of symptoms, stool studies, treatment response) should always be integrated.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

The histological remission data (5% annual relapse with histological remission vs 14% with endoscopic remission alone) is one of the most clinically important findings in this chapter. It suggests that the current treatment target (endoscopic improvement, MES 0 or 1) may be insufficient -- patients with MES 0 who still have histological activity are at meaningfully higher relapse risk. This supports the trend toward "targeting histological remission" in clinical trials.
