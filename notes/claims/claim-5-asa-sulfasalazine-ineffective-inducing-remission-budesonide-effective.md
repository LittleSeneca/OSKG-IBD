# ecco-cd2020-2: 5-ASA and sulfasalazine are ineffective for inducing remission in CD; budesonide is effective for mild-to-moderate ileocecal disease (R1.1-R1.2)

**Source:** [[ECCO Crohn's Medical 2020 - Torres]] — Torres et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Crohn's Disease: Medical Management (2020)

## The Claim

5-ASA and sulfasalazine are ineffective for inducing remission in CD; budesonide is effective for mild-to-moderate ileocecal disease (R1.1-R1.2)

## Evidence

A meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (5-ASA or sulfasalazine vs. placebo) found no significant effect for induction of clinical remission (RR 1.28; 95% CI 0.97-1.69). A Cochrane review confirmed this. Sulfasalazine showed a marginal effect of borderline significance (RR 1.38; 95% CI 1.00-1.89), limited to colonic disease. Budesonide 9 mg/day was superior to placebo for clinical response (RR 1.46; 95% CI 1.03-2.07) and clinical remission (RR 1.93; 95% CI 1.37-2.73). Budesonide was NOT superior to mesalazine for clinical remission (RR 1.30; 95% CI 0.98-1.72) but was superior for clinical response (RR 1.22; 95% CI 1.03-1.45).

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G1 (ACG 2018, R11) gives a **strong** recommendation against mesalamine with moderate-quality evidence — stronger wording than ECCO's "weak." G1 (R12) is also strong for budesonide but with low-quality evidence vs. ECCO's moderate. G8 is more cautious due to the borderline significance of sulfasalazine's effect. Both guidelines agree on the fundamental message: 5-ASA doesn't work for CD.

## Assessment

ECCO's "weak" recommendation against 5-ASA (vs. ACG's "strong") is notable. The difference may reflect the European panel's desire to leave room for sulfasalazine in colonic CD — a legacy European practice. The budesonide recommendation is one of the few "strong, moderate" recommendations — appropriately so, given consistent RCT data. The anatomical limitation (ileum/ascending colon only) is practically crucial: budesonide is useless for jejunal, proximal ileal, or left-sided colonic disease. The guideline acknowledges this treatment gap explicitly.
