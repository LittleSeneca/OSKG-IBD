# pimentel-ch9-4: Small Intestinal Fungal Overgrowth (SIFO) is an emerging diagnosis -- stool/blood/saliva tests are not valid for diagnosis

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch9 Refractory SIBO]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 9

## The Claim

Similar to SIBO, fungal elements can overgrow in the small intestine and cause symptoms. The only way to diagnose small intestinal fungal overgrowth (SIFO) is to perform an endoscopic aspiration of the small intestine and culture it for fungal elements. Current stool, saliva, or blood tests are not accurate in diagnosing SIFO.

## Evidence

Pimentel's group has "launched the largest trial to date to define mycobiome in various diseases." Candida albicans is the most common gut fungus. Treatment: antifungal medications such as nystatin.

## Stakes

SIFO may explain some SIBO-treatment-refractory cases. If a patient has both SIBO and SIFO, treating only the bacterial overgrowth with rifaximin may produce partial response because the fungal component remains untreated. The diagnosis requires invasive testing (endoscopic aspiration), which is a practical barrier.

## Assessment

SIFO is an important emerging concept that explains some treatment-resistant cases. Pimentel's caution about stool-based fungal testing mirrors his critique of stool-based bacterial testing (Chapter 3). The practical difficulty of diagnosing SIFO (requires endoscopy) means it will remain underdiagnosed until non-invasive biomarkers become available.
