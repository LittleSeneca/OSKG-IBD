# gottschall-ch910-3: Recovery timeline: initial improvement within 3 weeks, minimum 1-2 years on diet, 1 year post-symptom before reintroduction

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch9-10 Implementing the SCD]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 9-10

## The Claim

Most cases begin to improve within three weeks after the dietary regimen has been started and improvement usually continues... Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis take much longer with the minimum time of two years on the diet. A rule of thumb is to stay on the diet for at least one year after the last symptom has disappeared.

## Evidence

Personal experience with her daughter (7 years on diet, 2 years to symptom resolution) and clinical observation. No controlled data on optimal duration.

## Confidence

**Rating:** low
**Rationale:** LOW. The timeline claims have no controlled evidence. Two years minimum for Crohn's/UC is a specific recommendation based on Gottschall's daughter's case (n=1). The one-year-post-symptom rule is a con

## Stakes

Committing to 2+ years of strict dietary restriction is a major life decision for IBD patients. If shorter durations are equally effective, the unnecessarily long recommendation causes unnecessary burden. If the recommendation is correct, premature discontinuation risks relapse.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Pediatric EEN trials show remission induction in 6-8 weeks, followed by gradual food reintroduction over weeks, not years. The Crohn's Disease Exclusion Diet (CDED) uses a 6-week induction + 6-week maintenance followed by liberalization. These shorter timelines are supported by trial data that SCD lacks.

**Alternative reading:** The long timeline may be necessary for patients who are not using concurrent medication. Modern IBD management typically combines diet with pharmacotherapy, potentially allowing shorter dietary induction periods.

## Assessment

The timeline recommendation is the least evidence-based aspect of the SCD protocol. Gottschall's conservatism is understandable given the lack of trials, but the 2-year minimum for Crohn's/UC is arbitrary. Modern dietary protocols (CDED, EEN) achieve remission induction in weeks, not years. The SCD timeline likely reflects the gradual pace of mucosal healing without pharmaceutical support, but this is speculation.
