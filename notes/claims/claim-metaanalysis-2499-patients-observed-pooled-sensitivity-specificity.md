# yamada-ucdx63-3: Fecal calprotectin is the most useful non-invasive biomarker for UC, with threshold-dependent sensitivity and specificity

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch63 Ulcerative Colitis Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

A metaanalysis of 2499 patients observed a pooled sensitivity and specificity of 49% and 92% for C-reactive protein, and 88% and 73% for fecal calprotectin in IBD. Fecal calprotectin is a more sensitive biomarker of mucosal inflammation than C-reactive protein, and is more sensitive in ulcerative colitis compared to Crohn's disease.

## Evidence

The 2,499-patient meta-analysis (Mosli et al. 2015) provides pooled test characteristics. Threshold optimization: sensitivity is maximized at <50 microg/g (rules out inflammation), specificity is maximized at >250 microg/g (rules in moderate-severe inflammation). In patients with normal stool frequency and no rectal bleeding, FC <50 rules out moderate-severe inflammation with >95% accuracy. In patients with increased stool frequency and rectal bleeding >50% of stools, FC >250 rules in moderate-severe inflammation with >95% accuracy. Practical limitations: variability with time of collection, temperature-dependent degradation, and patient access to lab facilities.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The meta-analytic data is robust. The threshold-dependent clinical interpretation (rule-out at <50, rule-in at >250) is supported by pooled analyses from clinical trial data.

## Stakes

Fecal calprotectin is the closest thing to a non-invasive "endoscopy surrogate" in UC. Its proper use can reduce unnecessary colonoscopies (when FC is low in low-risk patients) and trigger timely treatment escalation (when FC is high despite symptomatic improvement). However, the chapter cautions that even when clinical + biochemical targets are met, "secondary assessments of mucosal inflammation are still required" -- calprotectin is adjunctive, not a replacement for endoscopy.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some clinicians argue for tighter calprotectin targets (e.g., <100 rather than <250) for treatment escalation decisions, particularly in post-operative or high-risk patients. The chapter's data supports 50/250 as the evidence-based thresholds.

**Alternative reading:** Fecal lactoferrin is an alternative neutrophil-derived biomarker with similar performance characteristics, though less widely studied than calprotectin.

## Assessment

The 88% sensitivity vs 73% specificity trade-off is important. Calprotectin is better at ruling OUT disease than ruling it IN -- a negative calprotectin in a low-risk patient effectively excludes IBD. But a positive calprotectin (>250) still has a ~27% false positive rate for moderate-severe inflammation. The clinical algorithm in Figure 63.12 (combining symptoms + calprotectin + endoscopy) is well-designed and represents current best practice.
