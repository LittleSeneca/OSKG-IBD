# aga-uc2020-2: All approved biologics and tofacitinib are superior to placebo for induction and maintenance — this is the guideline's only strong recommendation

**Source:** [[AGA UC 2020 - Feuerstein]] — Feuerstein et al. — AGA Clinical Practice Guidelines: Ulcerative Colitis (2020)

## The Claim

All approved biologics and tofacitinib are superior to placebo for induction and maintenance — this is the guideline's only strong recommendation

## Evidence

16 RCTs pooled. Induction (6-8 weeks) RR vs. placebo: infliximab 2.85 (2.11-3.86), adalimumab 1.62 (1.15-2.29), golimumab 2.49 (1.58-3.93), vedolizumab 2.22 (1.36-3.64), tofacitinib 3.22 (2.03-5.08), ustekinumab 2.91 (1.72-4.94). Maintenance (30-54 weeks) RR vs. placebo: infliximab 2.25 (1.67-3.05), adalimumab 2.28 (1.52-3.42), golimumab 1.88 (1.32-2.68), vedolizumab 2.31 (1.63-3.28), tofacitinib 5mg BID 3.09 (1.99-4.79), ustekinumab 1.83 (1.33-2.49). All well-tolerated; SAE rates not significantly different from placebo. Evidence rated down for imprecision (<200 events). Tofacitinib dose nuance: induction 10mg BID × 8 weeks (extendable to 16 weeks in partial responders); maintenance 5mg BID with option to escalate to 10mg BID if loss of response — but the higher dose carries an FDA warning for increased pulmonary embolism and all-cause mortality.

## Assessment

This is the least controversial recommendation in the guideline — every agent beats placebo. The single strong recommendation anchors the document. The nuance is in the tofacitinib dosing footnote: the FDA safety signal for 10mg BID (pulmonary embolism, all-cause mortality) means the higher maintenance dose should be used with caution. Compare this to the ACG guideline which lists tofacitinib 10mg BID for induction without the FDA caveat (published before the July 2019 update).
