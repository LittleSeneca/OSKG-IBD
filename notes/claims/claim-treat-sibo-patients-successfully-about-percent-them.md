# pimentel-lfe-2: SIBO can cause apparent food intolerances through bacterial fermentation -- treating SIBO can resolve these intolerances

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Low-Fermentation Eating]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), LFE

## The Claim

When we treat SIBO patients successfully, about 30 to 40 percent of them who initially had a positive lactose intolerance test now have a negative lactose intolerance test, thus providing more proof of the involvement of SIBO in lactose intolerance.

## Evidence

Lactose breath test before/after SIBO treatment data from Cedars-Sinai. Clinical observation.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The lactose intolerance reversal data is published, compelling, and suggests SIBO-induced enzyme dysfunction is reversible. The wheat/gluten sensitivity reversal claim is less well-docume

## Stakes

This is the most important clinical counterargument to permanent restrictive diets. If SIBO causes apparent food intolerances that resolve with treatment, then dietary therapy should be temporary (during treatment) rather than permanent. This directly contradicts SCD's "years on the diet" model but is consistent with low-FODMAP's phased reintroduction and the SSFG's "no food permanently forbidden" approach.

## Assessment

This is a transformative clinical insight. It explains why some patients can eat "forbidden" foods after successful SIBO treatment and why permanent dietary restriction may be unnecessary for many patients. The limitation: 30-40% reversal means 60-70% still have lactose intolerance after treatment, suggesting some enzyme damage is permanent or other factors are involved.
