# yamada-ucdx63-6: Disease activity is assessed by composite indices combining symptoms, endoscopy, and biomarkers; disease severity is a longitudinal prognostic construct

**Source:** [[Yamada 2022 - Ch63 Ulcerative Colitis Diagnosis]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

For all patients with ulcerative colitis, two aspects are vital for decision making: disease activity assessment, which is the cross-sectional assessment of symptomatic and endoscopic activity at that time point, and disease severity assessment, which involves evaluating the longitudinal disease course and prognosticating patients as being at low or high risk of complications.

## Evidence

The Mayo Clinic score is the most widely used activity index, incorporating stool frequency (0-3), rectal bleeding (0-3), physician global assessment (0-3), and endoscopic subscore (0-3). The PRO2 (patient-reported outcome) uses only stool frequency and rectal bleeding -- resolution of rectal bleeding (RBS 0) plus normalization of stool frequency (SFS 0-1) is the current recommended clinical treatment target. The UCEIS (Ulcerative Colitis Endoscopic Index of Severity) scores vascular pattern, bleeding, and erosions/ulcers (0-8). The ACG activity classification: remission (MES 0-1), mild (<4 stools/day, intermittent bleeding), moderate (4-6 stools/day, frequent bleeding, urgency), severe (>6 stools/day, systemic signs), fulminant (>10 stools/day, severe pain, systemic toxicity). For disease severity, a Delphi panel identified the most important prognostic factors (in order): active ulcers/erosions on endoscopy, impact on daily activities, elevated CRP, lack of response to prior biologics/immunosuppressives, hospitalization in last 12 months, corticosteroid use in last 12 months, anemia, increased stool frequency, low albumin, extensive colitis, anorectal symptoms, and rectal bleeding.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH for the activity indices (validated in multiple clinical trials). MEDIUM-HIGH for the severity Delphi -- it is expert consensus, not derived from a prediction model.

## Stakes

The activity/severity distinction is clinically essential but often conflated. A patient can have severe disease activity (hospitalized with acute severe UC) but low disease severity (first flare, rapid response to steroids, no prognostic risk factors). Conversely, a patient can have mild current activity but high severity (extensive colitis, prior biologic failure, low albumin). Treatment decisions should be driven by both: activity determines the urgency of intervention; severity determines the choice of maintenance therapy.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Different clinical trials use different indices (Mayo, UCEIS, modified Mayo, partial Mayo), making cross-trial comparison difficult. The FDA and EMA have different standards for what constitutes clinically meaningful improvement.

**Alternative reading:** Not documented.

## Assessment

The PRO2 (stool frequency + rectal bleeding) is a practical clinical tool because it doesn't require endoscopy. The finding that RBS 2-3 has >90% positive predictive value for moderate-severe endoscopic inflammation is clinically useful -- persistent rectal bleeding despite therapy is a reliable signal that endoscopic disease is active. The converse finding (RBS 0 only has ~50% chance of MES 0) is equally important -- absence of bleeding does not guarantee endoscopic remission, which is why confirmatory endoscopy is still needed before de-escalating therapy.
