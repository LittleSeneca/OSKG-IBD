# acg-cd2018-25: Smoking cessation is the only modifiable risk factor; risk stratification guides prophylaxis intensity (R54)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Smoking cessation is the only modifiable risk factor; risk stratification guides prophylaxis intensity (R54)

## Evidence

Three greatest risk factors for postoperative recurrence: (1) active tobacco smoking (especially women and heavy smokers), (2) penetrating disease (fistulas, abscesses, perforation), (3) two or more prior surgeries. Other risk factors: shorter diagnosis-to-surgery interval (<10 years), ileocolonic location, perianal fistula, more severe disease leading to surgery, longer resected segment, preoperative corticosteroid need. **Risk stratification:**
- **Low risk:** Non-smoker, no penetrating disease, first surgery → no prophylactic treatment, colonoscopy at 6 months post-op
- **Moderate risk:** Non-smoker, penetrating disease, first surgery, no prior medications → thiopurines ± metronidazole, colonoscopy at 6 months; add anti-TNF if endoscopic recurrence
- **High risk:** Prior resection within 10 years, or progression to surgery despite immunomodulator/biologic → postoperative anti-TNF ± immunomodulator, colonoscopy at 6 months

## Assessment

The risk-stratified approach is clinically practical but based on expert consensus rather than randomized evidence comparing strategies. The "very low" evidence for R54 reflects that smoking cessation RCTs in postoperative CD don't exist — but the observational data is so consistent that the recommendation is still appropriate.
