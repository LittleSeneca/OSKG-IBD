# pimentel-ch5-2: The small intestinal "disrupter" bacteria in SIBO are predominantly E. coli and Klebsiella (both hydrogen producers)

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch5 SIBO Definition and Diagnosis]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 5

## The Claim

In a recent publication, we showed that the two main 'weeds' in SIBO are E. coli and Klebsiella. These bacteria are referred to as 'disrupters.' When they increase in quantity, they bully the other bacteria to die off and they dominate the small intestine with their presence and their effects. And, by the way, they are hydrogen producers.

## Evidence

REIMAGINE study data. The ecological model: "Think of the small intestine as a lawn. If you don't mow the lawn (if there are no cleaning waves), the weeds (proteobacteria) that are always there outperform the grass (firmicutes, a type of gram-positive bacteria)."

## Stakes

If E. coli and Klebsiella are the primary SIBO pathogens, then: (a) rifaximin should be particularly effective against these gram-negative bacteria, (b) probiotic strains that compete with E. coli/Klebsiella might be preventive, and (c) breath test patterns (hydrogen production) should correlate with E. coli/Klebsiella abundance.

## Assessment

The identification of specific disrupter species is a major advance beyond the generic "SIBO is colonic bacteria in the wrong place" model. It opens the door to targeted antimicrobial therapy and biomarker development. The E. coli/Klebsiella finding also explains why rifaximin (broadly active against gram-negatives) is effective for SIBO.
