# yamada-uc63-2: Methotrexate is ineffective for ulcerative colitis

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch63 UC Clinical Manifestations and Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 63

## The Claim

Methotrexate is not effective in patients with ulcerative colitis as monotherapy.

## Evidence

Two well-designed RCTs (METEOR and MERIT-UC). The authors explicitly state that "clinical guidelines recommend against the use of methotrexate monotherapy for induction of maintenance in patients with ulcerative colitis."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. This is an important evidence-based negative finding. Methotrexate works for Crohn's (parenteral 25 mg weekly) but not for UC. The reason for this disease-specific difference is not well underst

## Stakes

Methotrexate should not be used as a thiopurine alternative in UC. This is a common clinical error -- extrapolating from Crohn's data. Patients who cannot tolerate thiopurines in UC need to move to biologics or JAK inhibitors, not methotrexate.
