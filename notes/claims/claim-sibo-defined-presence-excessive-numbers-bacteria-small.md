# acg-sibo2020-1: SIBO is a clinical syndrome defined by excessive small bowel bacteria causing GI symptoms -- not merely a laboratory finding

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

SIBO is defined as "the presence of excessive numbers of bacteria in the small bowel causing gastrointestinal symptoms." The guideline emphasizes that the definition implies BOTH measurable bacterial overgrowth AND resultant symptoms. Pathologic fermentation of nutrients that would ordinarily be completely absorbed in the small intestine leads to excess gas and bloating.

## Evidence

**GRADE rating:** Not graded (definitional). However, the guideline cites the North American Consensus (Rezaie et al., 2017) establishing >=10^3 CFU/mL on duodenal/jejunal aspirate as the culture threshold, and a rise in hydrogen >=20 ppm above baseline within 90 minutes of 75g glucose or 10g lactulose as the breath test threshold.

The 10^3 threshold is based on collated normal-subject data showing healthy controls have <10^3 CFU/mL. The older 10^5 threshold was based on postsurgical blind loop patients and is too stringent for general SIBO. The 20 ppm / 90 min breath test threshold replaced the older "double peak" criterion on lactulose -- the first peak within 90 min is now considered sufficient, eliminating the requirement for a second colonic peak.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The AGA Practice Update (Quigley 2020) agrees on the 10^3 CFU/mL culture threshold but is more skeptical about breath testing. The Rome Foundation Report (2017) discusses breath testing limitations at length and emphasizes that no single test is definitive. The BSG guidelines (2019) do not address SIBO diagnostic criteria directly.

## Assessment

The definitional shift from "laboratory finding" to "clinical syndrome" is important and overdue. It means a positive breath test alone is not sufficient for diagnosis -- symptoms must be present. But this creates a circular problem: the symptoms of SIBO (bloating, gas, distension, abdominal pain) are nonspecific and overlap completely with IBS, functional dyspepsia, and other functional GI disorders. The guideline acknowledges this: "no single symptom can be specifically attributed to SIBO."
