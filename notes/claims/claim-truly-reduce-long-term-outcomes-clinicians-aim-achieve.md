# yamada-cd64-1: Treat-to-target with objective markers (not just symptoms) improves long-term outcomes in CD

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch64 Crohn's Disease Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

To truly reduce long-term outcomes of CD, clinicians should aim to achieve not only symptomatic improvement but rather deep remission as a goal of therapy.

## Evidence

The REACT and CALM trials are cited as the evidence basis. REACT demonstrated improved long-term outcomes (hospitalization, surgery) with algorithmic 12-week reassessment and dose adjustment based on symptom-based scores. CALM showed that targeting objective markers (fecal calprotectin, CRP) produced superior outcomes to symptom-based management alone. Mucosal healing is associated with reduced hospitalization, surgery, and greater likelihood of maintaining remission off biologic therapy.

## Stakes

If treat-to-target is correct, then monitoring with biomarkers and endoscopy is not optional -- it drives therapeutic decision-making. This represents a shift from the historical pattern of reacting to symptoms. It also means patients who are "feeling well" may need therapy escalation based on objective inflammation, which requires patient buy-in.

## Assessment

This is the most important conceptual claim in the chapter. The authors present it as settled, which reflects the 2022 publication date -- the evidence from CALM (2017) had matured. The discussion of the "therapeutic pyramid" being "challenged on several fronts" and replaced by treat-to-target is historically illuminating: it captures the paradigm shift that occurred between 2010-2020.
