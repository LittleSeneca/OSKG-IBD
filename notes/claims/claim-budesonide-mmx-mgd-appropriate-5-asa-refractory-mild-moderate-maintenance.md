# ecco-uc2022-5: Budesonide MMX 9 mg/d is appropriate for 5-ASA-refractory mild-moderate UC but has no maintenance role; is not recommended for extensive or severe disease

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Budesonide MMX 9 mg/d is appropriate for 5-ASA-refractory mild-moderate UC but has no maintenance role; is not recommended for extensive or severe disease

## Evidence

Three RCTs (n=542), 8-week follow-up: budesonide MMX superior to placebo for clinical remission (RR 2.86; 1.62-5.04), clinical response (RR 1.46; 1.11-1.93), and endoscopic response (RR 1.43; 1.10-1.84). Pooled phase 3 analysis: combined clinical + endoscopic remission 17.7% vs 6.2% placebo (OR 3.3; 1.7-6.4). Subgroup analysis: benefit seen in left-sided colitis but not statistically significant in extensive disease. In 5-ASA-refractory patients: combined clinical + endoscopic remission 13% vs 7.5% (p=0.049), histologic healing 27% vs 17.5% (p=0.016). No maintenance data exist for budesonide MMX. The guideline restricts budesonide MMX recommendations to mild-moderate disease and reserves systemic steroids (prednisolone) for moderate-severe disease, reflecting the trial populations.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019, R9, R14) makes STRONG recommendations for budesonide MMX with moderate quality evidence — notably stronger than ECCO's WEAK recommendation with low quality. This reflects different GRADE judgments about the same evidence base. Both guidelines position budesonide MMX after 5-ASA failure and before systemic steroids. Neither addresses beclomethasone dipropionate, which is available in Europe but not the US.

## Assessment

The downgrade from ACG's "strong" to ECCO's "weak" is interesting and probably reflects the ECCO methodologists' stricter handling of the extensive disease subgroup (where budesonide MMX was not statistically superior to placebo). The absolute effect size (17.7% combined remission) is modest, and the weak recommendation feels more honest about the limitations. The guideline's silence on beclomethasone dipropionate — a topical steroid available in several European countries — is a notable omission given the European authorship.
