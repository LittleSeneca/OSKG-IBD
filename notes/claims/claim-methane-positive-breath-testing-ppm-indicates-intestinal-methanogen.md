# acg-sibo2020-5: Methane-positive breath testing (>=10 ppm) indicates intestinal methanogen overgrowth (IMO), not bacterial SIBO -- a nomenclature distinction with clinical implications

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

Methane-positive breath testing (>=10 ppm) indicates intestinal methanogen overgrowth (IMO), not bacterial SIBO -- a nomenclature distinction with clinical implications

## Evidence

Methanogens (primarily Methanobrevibacter smithii) are archaea, not bacteria. They use hydrogen as substrate to produce methane. They can overgrow in the colon as well as the small intestine -- the location is not determined by breath testing. The North American Consensus established >=10 ppm methane at any point during the test as diagnostic of methanogen colonization. The guideline cites the neomycin trials (references 104, 105) and the TARGET trials. The combination of rifaximin + neomycin showed 87% methane eradication vs 33% for neomycin alone and 28% for rifaximin alone.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The AGA Practice Update uses "intestinal methanogen overgrowth" sporadically but does not formally adopt the IMO nomenclature. The Rome Foundation Report discusses methane in the context of constipation-predominant IBS but does not propose a separate nomenclature.

## Assessment

The IMO nomenclature proposal is Pimentel's signature contribution to this guideline. It is logically coherent (archaea are not bacteria) and clinically useful (methane-positive patients are predominantly constipated and may need different treatment -- neomycin added to rifaximin). The resistance to adoption by other societies reflects the entrenchment of "SIBO" as a catch-all term and the practical reality that most clinicians test for both hydrogen and methane simultaneously and treat based on which gas is elevated.
