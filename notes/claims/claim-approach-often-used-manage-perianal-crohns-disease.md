# steinhart-surg-5: Perianal Crohn's disease requires combined medical-surgical management; setons prevent recurrent abscesses

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch8 Surgical Treatment]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 8

## The Claim

An approach that is often used to manage perianal Crohn's disease is a combined surgical and medical approach, whereby the surgeon drains any abscesses and puts in setons as necessary and the gastroenterologist treats the Crohn's disease with medication, most often infliximab or adalimumab.

## Evidence

Clinical practice patterns. Steinhart acknowledges "there haven't been large trials to study this combined approach, but it appears that it may provide long-term healing and symptom control better than what can be provided by either surgery or drug therapy alone." Surgical flap procedures have "a fairly high failure rate of approximately 40% to 50%."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** MEDIUM-HIGH for combined approach (well-established in practice, supported by observational data, no large RCTs). HIGH for seton function. HIGH for flap failure rate.

## Stakes

Perianal disease is one of the most disabling manifestations of Crohn's. The combined med-surg approach has transformed outcomes from a cycle of recurrent abscesses and sphincter damage to controlled drainage and biologic-mediated healing. Access to coordinated gastroenterology-colorectal surgery care is inequitably distributed, as Steinhart notes.

## Assessment

The combined approach is the standard of care for complex perianal Crohn's. Steinhart's description of seton mechanics (counterintuitive -- keeping a fistula open to prevent abscesses) is well-explained for a patient audience. His acknowledgment that coordinated care is "not readily available everywhere" is an honest structural critique.
