# sarna-sibo-diet-2: The SIBO Specific Food Guide (SSFG) is a hybrid of SCD and low-FODMAP, using a color-coded tier system instead of binary "legal/illegal" classification

**Source:** [[Sarna 2021 - Healing SIBO Dietary Protocol]] — Lapine (Sarna) — SIBO Made Simple (2021), Dietary Protocol

## The Claim

This food guide was developed by Dr. Siebecker and is based on her clinical experience treating SIBO. It combines the recommendations of the Specific Carbohydrate Diet and the low FODMAP diet and is low fermentable.

## Evidence

Siebecker's clinical experience (no trials). Sarna's personal experience as a vegetarian navigating SIBO diets.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** LOW for the SSFG as a validated dietary protocol (no trials). MEDIUM-HIGH for the color-coded tier approach being more flexible and sustainable than binary classification. The SSFG is a clinical tool,

## Stakes

The tiered approach represents an important evolution beyond SCD's rigidity. By acknowledging that fermentability is dose-dependent and individually variable, the SSFG empowers patients to experiment rather than obey. This is psychologically healthier and more sustainable.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** SCD purists would argue that the color-coding obscures the biochemical distinction between allowed and forbidden carbohydrates. If the SCD's monosaccharide theory is correct, some red-category foods are genuinely harmful regardless of quantity.

**Alternative reading:** The SSFG's flexibility may lead to inconsistent implementation and reduced efficacy. A patient who interprets "orange foods in small amounts are okay" liberally may never achieve the degree of fermentable carbohydrate restriction needed for symptom relief.

## Assessment

The color-coded approach is a significant practical improvement over binary "legal/illegal" classification. It acknowledges dose-dependency (a fundamental principle of fermentation that Gottschall's model ignores) and individual variation. The weakness is the complete absence of formal validation -- we don't know if SSFG adherence produces outcomes comparable to SCD or low-FODMAP.

## SIBO Dietary Approaches Compared (from Ch4 text)

Sarna provides capsule summaries of five SIBO dietary approaches:

### Low FODMAP Diet (Monash University)
- Created for IBS/IBD, adopted for SIBO
- Restriction phase followed by structured reintroduction
- Risk: long-term strict low-FODMAP "can actually disrupt the number and diversity of good bacteria in your microbiome"
- Key insight: "it's important to not get stuck in the initial restrictive phase"

### Cedars-Sinai Low Fermentation Diet / LFE (Pimentel)
- "Low fiber and emphasizes the importance of fasting between meals so that the MMC can function properly"
- White rice, white bread, cookies generally allowed
- Vegetables less restricted than low-FODMAP
- Lactose-free dairy allowed
- "If you're feeling overwhelmed... this diet is a great way to start because it is easy to follow (even when eating out)"

### Specific Carbohydrate Diet / SCD (Gottschall)
- "Grain-free, low-sugar, low-lactose diet originally created for the management of celiac disease"
- "Because it focuses on foods that are easy to digest but doesn't consider FODMAPs"
- "Generally recommended for people with more moderate cases of SIBO who can tolerate a wider variety of vegetables"

### GAPS Diet (Campbell-McBride)
- Variation of SCD designed for autistic patients
- "Works very well for people with mood or brain disorders who also have GI disorders"
- "Not a SIBO diet" -- may need modification to eliminate high FODMAP foods
- "Emphasizes animal products, so it is hard to follow if you are vegetarian"

### SIBO Bi-Phasic Diet (Siebecker/Jacobi)
- SSFG broken into two phases by Dr. Nirala Jacobi
- Phase 1: symptom reduction
- Phase 2: concurrent with SIBO treatment (herbal antibiotics)
