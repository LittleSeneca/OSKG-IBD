# steinhart-surg-4: Strictureplasty conserves bowel length in Crohn's patients at risk of short bowel syndrome

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch8 Surgical Treatment]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 8

## The Claim

Strictureplasty is a method of avoiding removal of additional segments of intestine... by opening up the affected and narrowed segment of the intestine and creating a larger internal passageway for food to pass through without causing symptoms of obstruction.

## Evidence

Surgical technique description. Steinhart notes the limitations: "The affected segment is often too long," "sometimes too diseased and thickened," and when "there may be only a single short segment of narrowing... the risk of the strictureplasty procedure (leak, infection, recurrent obstruction) is not worth the potential benefit of avoiding a resection."

## Stakes

Short bowel syndrome is "a serious consequence of Crohn's disease surgery and can lead to the need for long-term or permanent intravenous feeding at home." Strictureplasty is the primary surgical strategy to prevent this outcome. Every centimeter of bowel preserved matters.

## Assessment

This is appropriately presented as a conservation strategy rather than a first-line approach. Steinhart's honesty about when strictureplasty is and is not appropriate (too long, too diseased, single short stricture) gives patients realistic expectations.
