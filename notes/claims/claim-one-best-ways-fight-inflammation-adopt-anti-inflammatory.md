# foote-cookbook-3: An anti-inflammatory diet is the foundation for Crohn's management, with 10 specific "power foods"

**Source:** [[Foote 2020 - Crohn's Disease Cookbook]] — Foote — Crohn's Disease AIP Cookbook (2020)

## The Claim

One of the best ways to fight inflammation is to adopt an anti-inflammatory diet.

## Evidence

General nutritional science consensus. No specific trials cited for this food list. The anti-inflammatory framing draws on Mediterranean diet research and individual food component studies (curcumin in turmeric, omega-3s in fatty fish, polyphenols in berries).

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** MEDIUM. Individual anti-inflammatory foods have supporting evidence (turmeric/curcumin has RCTs in IBD; omega-3s have mixed results). The aggregate "anti-inflammatory diet" concept is supported by Med

## Stakes

This is the mainstream dietetic alternative to named protocols (SCD, low-FODMAP, CDED). It's more flexible and easier to follow but less specific -- "eat anti-inflammatory foods" is less actionable than "eliminate all grains."

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** Some SCD advocates would note that several anti-inflammatory foods (garlic, tomato, citrus) are potentially problematic for some IBD patients. The low-FODMAP diet restricts garlic, onion, and some fruits. Foote's inclusive approach may overestimate tolerability.

**Alternative reading:** The anti-inflammatory diet may work primarily through caloric adequacy and nutritional repletion rather than specific anti-inflammatory compounds. Malnourished patients improving on ANY adequate diet could explain apparent anti-inflammatory effects.

## Assessment

The anti-inflammatory framing is sensible as a remission maintenance strategy. It's less useful as a flare intervention (when many anti-inflammatory foods become poorly tolerated). Foote's strength is in providing a positive, additive framework ("eat more of these") rather than a restrictive one.
