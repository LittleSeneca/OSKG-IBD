# gottschall-ch12-3: Historical clinical observation consistently linked carbohydrate intake to intestinal disease exacerbation

**Source:** [[Gottschall 1994 - Ch1-2 Origins and Scientific Evidence]] — Gottschall — Breaking the Vicious Cycle (1994), Ch 1-2

## The Claim

Gottschall presents a historical through-line: Aretaeus (300 AD) treated celiac-type diarrhea with fasting and plantain juice; Prince Charles (1745) cured ulcerative colitis with a milk-free diet; Dr. Christian Herter (early 1900s) observed that "proteins were well tolerated, fats were handled moderately well but carbohydrates (sugars and starches) were badly tolerated" and that carbohydrate ingestion "almost invariably caused a relapse or a return of diarrhea"; Dr. Samuel Gee (1888) stated milk was "the least suitable food" and highly starchy food "unfit," adding "We must never forget that what the patient takes beyond his power to digest does harm."

## Evidence

Historical case reports and clinical observations spanning centuries. The Herter and Gee observations are particularly specific about carbohydrate intolerance.

## Stakes

If clinicians for centuries observed carbohydrate intolerance in intestinal disease, the modern neglect of dietary therapy is a departure from, not a continuation of, medical tradition. This is a powerful rhetorical argument even if the evidence is pre-scientific.

## Disagreement

**Who disagrees:** No direct disagreement -- these are historical observations. The debate is whether they justify specific dietary protocols today.

## Assessment

The historical evidence is persuasive as a pattern but cannot substitute for controlled trials. Gee and Herter were observing real phenomena (disaccharidase deficiency in inflamed mucosa) without understanding the biochemistry. Gottschall uses their observations to argue for SCD, but they equally support low-FODMAP, LFE, or any diet that reduces fermentable carbohydrates.
