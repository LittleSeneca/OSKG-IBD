# ecco-uc2022-3: Oral 5-ASA ≥2 g/d induces remission with low-quality evidence but strong recommendation; topical 5-ASA ≥1 g/d is strongly recommended for distal colitis; combined oral + rectal is weakly recommended over oral monotherapy

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Oral 5-ASA ≥2 g/d induces remission with low-quality evidence but strong recommendation; topical 5-ASA ≥1 g/d is strongly recommended for distal colitis; combined oral + rectal is weakly recommended over oral monotherapy

## Evidence

Meta-analysis of 11 RCTs (n=2,156): oral 5-ASA RR 1.56 (95% CI 1.24-1.97) for clinical remission, RR 1.58 (1.35-1.86) for clinical response. Endoscopic response in 4 RCTs (n=416): RR 1.73 (1.0-3.0). SAEs: 6.1% vs 9% placebo. For topical 5-ASA, meta-analysis of 8 studies (≥1 g/d for 2-8 weeks): clinical remission RR 3.56 (2.08-6.09), clinical response RR 2.46 (2.01-3.01), endoscopic response RR 2.75 (2.04-3.7). For combination therapy: 4 trials (n=322), clinical remission RR 1.45 (0.98-2.13) — not statistically significant but trend favored combination. Serious inconsistency (I²=71%) and risk of bias present. The guideline's own Cochrane reference confirms no apparent differences between 5-ASA formulations and no overall evidence for superior efficacy of higher total daily doses across dose-ranging trials.

**Key nuance on dosing:** The ASCEND trials' subgroup analysis suggested benefit of 4.8 g/day over 2.4 g/day in patients with more active disease or prior corticosteroid/5-ASA/rectal therapy use, and greater mucosal healing at 4.8 g/day. But this was not seen in MMX 5-ASA trials. The guideline does NOT make a formal low-vs-high dose recommendation — unlike ACG 2019 which conditionally recommends low-dose (2-2.4 g/d) as sufficient.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019) makes similar recommendations but with an explicit 2-2.4 g/d "low dose is sufficient" conditional recommendation (R13) — a significant divergence. G5 (AGA 2020) uses ≥2 g/d for extensive and ≥1 g/d rectal, aligning more closely with ECCO. The European convention of ≥2 g/d as the standard dose (without a "low dose sufficient" qualifier) reflects higher baseline 5-ASA dosing in European practice. The combination therapy recommendation is consistently weak/conditional across all guidelines — the evidence simply isn't strong enough for a firm recommendation despite clinical logic.

## Assessment

ECCO's refusal to make a "low dose is sufficient" recommendation is clinically significant. In European practice, most clinicians start at ≥3-4 g/d for active disease and the ≥2 g/d floor in R1 is genuinely a floor, not a target. The ACG's R13 (2-2.4 g/d sufficient) would be considered undertreatment by many European experts. The combination therapy recommendation, while weak, carries more weight in the ECCO document because of the stronger European tradition of topical therapy — enemas and suppositories are more culturally accepted and prescribed in Europe than in the US.
