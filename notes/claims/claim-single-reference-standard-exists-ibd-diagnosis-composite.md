# ecco-diag2019-1: No single reference standard exists for IBD diagnosis — composite assessment of clinical, biochemical, endoscopic, imaging, and histological findings is required

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

No single reference standard exists for IBD diagnosis — composite assessment of clinical, biochemical, endoscopic, imaging, and histological findings is required

## Evidence

At minimum: biochemical assessment (FBC, CRP, electrolytes, LFTs), stool microbiology (including *C. difficile*), and ileocolonoscopy with biopsies. CRP broadly correlates with clinical severity in CD but less so in UC; laboratory markers may be normal in both. Loose stools >6 weeks discriminate IBD from most infectious diarrhoea. Anaemia workup uses inflammation-adjusted ferritin cut-offs: <30 µg/L without active disease [EL2]; up to 100 µg/L may still indicate iron deficiency with inflammation [EL4]; ferritin 30-100 µg/L + transferrin saturation <20% suggests mixed iron deficiency + ACD [EL2]. Immunisation status (HBV, HAV, measles, varicella, HCV, EBV, HIV) should be assessed at diagnosis; consider latent TB screening [EL5].

**Statement 1.2:** "Genetic or serological testing is currently not recommended for routine diagnosis of CD or UC." **[EL3]** — pANCA/ASCA accuracy is limited; antiglycan/antimicrobial antibodies add little value; >250 IBD SNPs exist but no genetic panel allows diagnosis.

## Assessment

The composite diagnostic approach is methodologically honest — IBD lacks a pathognomonic test. The inflammation-adjusted ferritin cut-offs are a crucial practical distinction absent from G1. The vaccination screening recommendations reflect ECCO's emphasis on preventive care before immunosuppression.
