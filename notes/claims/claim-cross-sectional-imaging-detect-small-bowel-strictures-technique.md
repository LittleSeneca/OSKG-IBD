# ecco-diag2019-10: Cross-sectional imaging should detect small bowel strictures; no technique currently determines fibrosis vs. inflammation

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

Cross-sectional imaging should detect small bowel strictures; no technique currently determines fibrosis vs. inflammation

## Evidence

**Accuracy:** IUS sensitivity 79%, specificity 92% (SICUS improves to 89%). CTE sensitivity 92%, specificity 100%. MRI sensitivity 89%, specificity 94%. CT and MRI comparable: 85% vs. 92% sensitivity, 100% vs. 90% specificity. **The fibrosis problem:** Strictures contain variable inflammatory and fibrotic proportions. No technique is sufficiently accurate to distinguish them for clinical decisions. Novel techniques (dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, magnetization-transfer MRI, ultrasound elastography, CEUS) have limited unstandardised data. Rimola et al. (2015) gadolinium enhancement technique (70s-7min on MRE) distinguished mild/moderate from severe fibrosis irrespective of inflammation — awaits external validation.

**Colonic strictures (Statement 3.1.2):** Any colonic stricture must be carefully surveyed for carcinoma [EL4]; 3.6% and 4.9% CRC probability at 5 and 10 years (Lovasz 2013). Dysplasia/cancer detected in 3.5% of IBD patients undergoing surgery for colonic strictures (GETAID). Yearly surveillance for strictures detected within 5 years. No method (including histology) can definitively rule out malignancy.

## Assessment

The admission that no technique determines fibrosis degree is the most honest and clinically frustrating statement in the guideline — this is the holy grail of CD imaging. The 3.5% malignancy rate in colonic strictures justifies aggressive surveillance.
