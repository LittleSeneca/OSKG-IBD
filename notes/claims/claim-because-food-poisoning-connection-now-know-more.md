# pimentel-ch4-5: IBS is now better understood mechanistically than Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis, yet receives 1/25th the research funding

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch4 Food Poisoning Autoimmunity]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 4

## The Claim

Because of the food poisoning connection, we now know more about the cause of IBS than we do about Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis. IBS is 40 times more common than Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis. We're much further along in understanding the true mechanism of IBS than that of the other two diseases. Yet in the last decade, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis have received $250 million for research funding from the National Institutes of Health, whereas IBS has received only $10 million.

## Evidence

NIH funding figures ($250M for IBD vs. $10M for IBS over a decade). The 40x prevalence comparison. Pimentel's CdtB-vinculin mechanism as a more complete causal explanation than anything available for IBD.

The $250M vs. $10M funding disparity is the single most damning statistic in the book. If IBS affects 45 million Americans and costs $21 billion/year, the 8-cents-per-patient NIH funding level is indefensible regardless of one's position on the SIBO theory.

## Assessment

Pimentel's frustration is justified. The funding disparity reflects historical biases (IBS as "functional"/"psychological" vs. IBD as "organic"/"inflammatory") that his own research has done much to overturn. Whether IBS is truly better understood than IBD is debatable (IBD has more treatment options), but the mechanistic understanding of post-infectious IBS is arguably more complete than the understanding of what triggers Crohn's or UC in any given patient.

**Strongest section:** The CdtB-vinculin autoimmunity model. This is Pimentel's most original scientific contribution and provides the missing mechanistic link between the epidemiological observation (food poisoning → IBS) and the physiological consequence (MMC impairment → SIBO). If validated independently, this is the most important advance in IBS pathophysiology in decades.

**Weakest section:** The discussion of preventing IBS with prophylactic antibiotics during travel ("Many doctors in Southern California -- including us -- take antibiotics to prevent IBS when traveling"). This recommendation is not FDA-approved, not studied in clinical trials, and raises concerns about antibiotic stewardship and resistance. It is presented as personal practice without adequate evidence.

**Historical note:** This chapter represents the culmination of the argument Pimentel began in 2006 (A New IBS Solution). The 2006 book presented SIBO as a cause of IBS but lacked the autoimmunity mechanism. The 2022 book completes the causal chain: food poisoning → CdtB → anti-vinculin → MMC impairment → SIBO → IBS. The blood test did not exist in 2006. This 16-year evolution from clinical observation to molecular mechanism to diagnostic test is a model of translational research.
