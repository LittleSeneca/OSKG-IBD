# ecco-uc2022-6: Thiopurines should NOT be used as monotherapy for induction — they are too slow; initiate alongside an effective induction agent if maintenance is planned

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Thiopurines should NOT be used as monotherapy for induction — they are too slow; initiate alongside an effective induction agent if maintenance is planned

## Evidence

Two RCTs (n=130) comparing azathioprine to placebo for induction (1-4 months, with concomitant corticosteroids): clinical remission RR 1.22 (0.79-1.88) — not significant. No data on clinical response, endoscopic response, or SAEs. No RCTs identified for mercaptopurine or thioguanine. The slow onset of action (8-12 weeks) makes thiopurines inherently unsuitable as induction monotherapy.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019, R21) makes a STRONG recommendation against thiopurine/MTX monotherapy for induction. G5 (AGA 2020) concurs. The ECCO recommendation is notably weaker ("suggest against" vs "recommend against"), which may reflect a European comfort with thiopurines that US guidelines don't share — but the clinical message is identical.

## Assessment

This is one of few instances where ECCO is less forceful than ACG. The "weak" recommendation probably reflects the small sample size (only 130 patients across two old trials) and the GRADE requirement for evidence quality, not clinical equipoise. Practically, no one should use thiopurine monotherapy for active UC, and the guideline's qualifying statement — that thiopurines are appropriate to initiate alongside an effective induction agent when maintenance is planned — is the clinically important takeaway.
