# chutkan-found-1: The microbiome is our second genome and a vital organ, not a collection of passengers

**Source:** [[The Microbiome Solution - Chutkan - Intro Ch1-3]] — Chutkan — The Microbiome Solution (2015), Intro + Ch 1-3

## The Claim

Microbial cells outnumber human cells ten to one. The genes in our microbiome (the collective DNA of our gut bacteria) outnumber human genes by more than 100 to 1. This "second genome" influences digestion, immunity, metabolism, mood, and behavior. The book's title captures the thesis: "Live Dirty" (increase microbial exposure from soil, animals, fermented foods) and "Eat Clean" (eliminate processed foods, artificial sweeteners, antibiotics, and antimicrobial chemicals).

## Evidence

Chutkan draws on her two decades of clinical gastroenterology practice and the emerging microbiome literature. She describes the shift in her patient population from well-defined diseases (ulcers, gallstones, colitis) to symptom complexes without clear pathology.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The core claims about microbial cell counts and genomic diversity are well-established. The "Live Dirty, Eat Clean" framework is a clinical heuristic rather than a tested protocol.

## Stakes

If the microbiome-as-organ thesis is correct, then medical practices that damage gut bacteria (antibiotics, acid suppression, C-sections) should be treated as organ-damaging interventions requiring strong justification, not as routine defaults.

**Where Chutkan differs:** Chutkan is more aggressive than many gastroenterologists in attributing broad symptom complexes to dysbiosis. Her claim that dysbiosis is "the most common disorder" in her GI practice is based on clinical impression, not epidemiological survey data.

## Assessment

The foundational claims are solid and consistent with the broader microbiome literature. Chutkan's clinical narrative is persuasive but the book is a trade publication, not a peer-reviewed study — the patient anecdotes illustrate patterns but do not establish prevalence.
