# pimentel-ch4-4: Post-infectious IBS is mechanistically analogous to other post-infectious autoimmune conditions (reactive arthritis, Guillain-Barré, gastroparesis, dyspepsia)

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch4 Food Poisoning Autoimmunity]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 4

## The Claim

Developing a disease after an infection is not a new phenomenon and has been reported for decades in conditions other than IBS.

## Evidence

These are all well-established post-infectious conditions with known molecular mechanisms. Reactive arthritis: Campylobacter, Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia, Chlamydia → joint inflammation persisting after infection clearance. Guillain-Barré: Campylobacter → molecular mimicry between bacterial antigens and peripheral nerve gangliosides. Gastroparesis: post-viral damage to gastric nerves. Dyspepsia: post-Salmonella functional symptoms.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. These analogies are well-established in the medical literature. Pimentel's contribution is placing IBS in this same category of post-infectious autoimmune/neuropathic conditions.

## Stakes

If IBS belongs in the post-infectious autoimmune disease category, it reframes IBS from a functional disorder to an organic autoimmune/neuropathic condition. This has implications for research funding, drug development, and clinical respect.

## Assessment

The analogies are apt and rhetorically powerful. The Guillain-Barré/Campylobacter connection is particularly relevant because it involves the same bacterium (Campylobacter jejuni) and the same mechanism (molecular mimicry), just targeting different proteins (gangliosides in GBS vs. vinculin in IBS). The fact that both conditions follow Campylobacter infection and both involve molecular mimicry strengthens both arguments.
