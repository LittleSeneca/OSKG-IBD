# acg-sibo2020-7: Antibiotics are the cornerstone of SIBO treatment; rifaximin has the best evidence with 61-78% efficacy

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

Antibiotics are the cornerstone of SIBO treatment; rifaximin has the best evidence with 61-78% efficacy

## Evidence

The meta-analysis of 32 clinical trials (reference 91, Gatta & Scarpignato 2017) on rifaximin: 7 RCTs, 24 cohort studies, 1 crossover trial, 1,331 patients total. Overall intention-to-treat success rate: 70.8% (CI 61.4-78.2). Adverse reactions: 4.6%. Doses ranged from 600-1600 mg/day, duration 5-28 days. Only one study compared rifaximin to placebo. The guideline's suggested dose: rifaximin 550 mg TID.

Other antibiotics with supporting data:
- Ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID: 43-100% response (3 small trials)
- Metronidazole 250 mg TID: 43-87%
- Neomycin 500 mg BID: 33-55% (primarily for methane/IMO)
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg BID: ~50%
- Tetracycline 250 mg QID: ~87.5% (one study, 24 patients)
- Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole DS BID: ~95% (one pediatric study)
- Norfloxacin 400 mg daily: 30-100%
- Doxycycline 100 mg daily-BID: no breath test follow-up data

**Recurrence data:** 12.6% at 3 months, 27.5% at 6 months, 43.7% at 9 months (reference 102, Lauritano et al.)

**Combination therapy for IMO:** Rifaximin + neomycin achieved 87% methane eradication vs 33% neomycin alone and 28% rifaximin alone (reference 105, Low et al.)

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The AGA Practice Update also recommends rifaximin as first-line but is more circumspect about systemic antibiotics. The Rome Foundation Report discusses the limitations of antibiotic trials in detail (heterogeneity, small sample sizes, lack of placebo controls). No other guideline provides the antibiotic efficacy table that this ACG guideline does.

## Assessment

This is the most clinically useful section of the guideline. The antibiotic table (Table 5) is the reference that clinicians actually use. The 70.8% pooled rifaximin efficacy is modest but consistent across multiple trial designs. The 43.7% recurrence at 9 months is the number that should shape clinical expectations: SIBO treatment is often cyclical, not curative. The 87% methane eradication with rifaximin + neomycin combination is a strong signal for dual therapy in IMO patients with constipation.
