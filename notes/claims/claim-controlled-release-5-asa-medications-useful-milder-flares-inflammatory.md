# steinhart-drug-3: 5-ASA preparations are effective for UC but disappointing in Crohn's disease

**Source:** [[Steinhart 2018 - Ch7 Drug Therapy]] — Steinhart — Crohn's and Colitis: Understanding and Managing IBD, 3E (2018), Ch 7

## The Claim

The controlled-release 5-ASA medications are useful in milder flares of inflammatory bowel disease, particularly ulcerative colitis... When the 5-ASA drugs were first developed, it was hoped that they would provide a safe and effective alternative to steroid medications in patients with Crohn's disease. Unfortunately, they have not been found to be as effective in Crohn's disease as had been hoped for.

## Evidence

Clinical experience and trial data. Steinhart notes that 5-ASA drugs may benefit Crohn's patients with colonic involvement or mild disease, but "only a small minority of patients will respond." He also notes their potential role in post-surgical recurrence prevention in Crohn's, where "they are particularly appealing... because the 5-ASA drugs have a well-proven, long-term safety record."

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. This is well-established in the literature. 5-ASA (mesalamine) is first-line for mild-moderate UC induction and maintenance. For Crohn's, the evidence is weak -- most guidelines no longer recomm

## Stakes

Using 5-ASA in Crohn's when ineffective delays effective therapy. However, the excellent safety profile means the risk of trying it is low.

## Assessment

Steinhart is measured. He acknowledges the controversy ("there is some controversy about their effectiveness in Crohn's disease") and the practice pattern ("many physicians still elect to use 5-ASA drugs early in the course of the disease because of their excellent safety record"). His clinical honesty about the drug's limitations is valuable for patients making treatment decisions.
