# pimentel-ch5-1: SIBO is defined as >10^3 CFU/mL in the small intestine and is caused by impaired clearance, not infection

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch5 SIBO Definition and Diagnosis]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 5

## The Claim

SIBO occurs when there are greater than 1000 bacteria per milliliter (>10^3/mL) in the small intestine. In SIBO, this excessive quantity of bacteria is not an infection, but rather an over-colonization of specific bacteria in an area where they don't belong. The bacteria aren't invading or attacking you; they are your own resident bacteria. SIBO is, in effect, a phenomenon of bacteria dislocated from your large intestine to your small intestine.

## Evidence

Standard SIBO definition from gastroenterology. The REIMAGINE study's confirmation of the small intestine-colon bacterial gradient. The mechanism of bacterial reflux: "the ileocecal valve... is a simple connector rather than a functioning sphincter. This means that the valve never fully closes and therefore bacteria can pass through it. The small intestine dumps these bacteria back into the colon when the housekeeper waves kick in."

## Stakes

The distinction between SIBO as bacterial overgrowth vs. infection is clinically important: antibiotics for SIBO aim to reduce bacterial load, not eradicate a pathogen. The SIBO-is-not-infection framing explains why SIBO recurs if the underlying clearance defect is not addressed.

## Assessment

The >10^3 definition is the field standard, though some researchers use >10^5 for jejunal aspirate. Pimentel's SIBO-as-dislocation model is his working framework, but the REIMAGINE finding that the small intestine has its own distinct microbiome (proteobacteria + firmicutes) complicates the simple colon-to-small-intestine reflux model. The "lawn and weeds" analogy (proteobacteria as weeds outcompeting firmicutes grass when the MMC "lawnmower" fails) is more consistent with REIMAGINE data.
