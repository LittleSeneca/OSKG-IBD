# yamada-surg65-2: Strictureplasty preserves bowel length in multifocal small bowel CD with acceptable morbidity

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch65 Surgical Treatment of IBD]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 65

## The Claim

Given CD is not cured by surgery, and short bowel syndrome can occur in up to 13% of cases, strictureplasty has been used

## Evidence

Multiple surgical series. Risk factors for complications: low preoperative albumin, weight loss, advanced age, preoperative steroid use, abscess/fistula at time of strictureplasty. Reoperation rates: 29% at 5 years, 34% at 7.5 years, 73% at 10 years.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. Strictureplasty is a well-established bowel-sparing technique with extensive surgical literature. The morbidity and recurrence data come from multiple series with consistent findings.

## Stakes

Strictureplasty is the primary surgical strategy for preventing short bowel syndrome in patients with multifocal small bowel CD. The high long-term reoperation rates (73% at 10 years) place realistic expectations on the procedure's durability.

## Assessment

This section provides the surgical outcome data that Steinhart's patient-facing chapter lacks. The complication risk factors (albumin, weight, steroids, abscess) give concrete guidance for preoperative optimization. The finding that recurrences occur "remote from original strictureplasty sites" supports the concept that CD recurrence represents new disease, not failure of the surgical technique.
