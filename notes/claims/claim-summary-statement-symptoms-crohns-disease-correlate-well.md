# acg-cd2018-2: Symptoms do not correlate with active inflammation; objective assessment is mandatory

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Summary Statement 9. "Symptoms of Crohn's disease do not correlate well with the presence of active inflammation, and therefore should not be the sole guide for therapy."

## Evidence

The guideline explicitly cautions against treating symptoms alone. Objective evaluation by endoscopy or cross-sectional imaging should be undertaken periodically. This principle underlies the modern "treat-to-target" approach: mucosal healing (absence of ulceration on endoscopy) is a goal of therapy. Endoscopic scoring systems include CDEIS and SES-CD. Improvement in radiologic parameters on CTE/MRE correlates with better clinical outcomes (fewer hospitalizations, surgeries, steroid use). The guideline references the STRIDE consensus (Peyrin-Biroulet 2015) on selecting therapeutic targets.

## Assessment

This is the most clinically transformative concept in the guideline. The disconnect between symptoms and mucosal inflammation explains why corticosteroid tapering fails in so many patients (symptoms resolve but mucosa doesn't heal) and why objective monitoring is essential. The guideline's consistent return to this theme throughout the treatment sections reinforces its importance.
