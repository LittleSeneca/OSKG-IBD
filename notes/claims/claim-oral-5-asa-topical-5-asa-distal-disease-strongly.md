# ecco-uc2022-7: Oral 5-ASA ≥2 g/d and topical 5-ASA for distal disease are strongly recommended for maintenance despite very low-quality evidence; thiopurines are strongly recommended for steroid-dependent or 5-ASA-intolerant patients

**Source:** [[ECCO UC Therapeutics 2022 - Raine]] — Raine et al. — ECCO Guidelines on Ulcerative Colitis: Therapeutics (2022)

## The Claim

Oral 5-ASA ≥2 g/d and topical 5-ASA for distal disease are strongly recommended for maintenance despite very low-quality evidence; thiopurines are strongly recommended for steroid-dependent or 5-ASA-intolerant patients

## Evidence

Oral 5-ASA maintenance: 2 RCTs (n=306), 48-52 weeks. Clinical remission RR 1.54 (1.11-2.14). Endoscopic remission RR 1.20 (1.00-1.44) — trend but not significant. SAEs RR 0.41 (0.23-0.71) favoring 5-ASA. Topical 5-ASA maintenance: 4 RCTs, doses 1 g three times weekly to 1 g daily, 12-24 months. Clinical remission RR 2.22 (1.26-3.90). Endoscopic remission in one small study (n=25): RR 4.88 (1.31-18.18). Thiopurine maintenance: 4 RCTs (n=232), 1-year follow-up. Azathioprine superior to placebo for clinical remission (56% vs 35%; RR 1.59, 1.19-2.11). Significant safety concerns: thiopurines should be discouraged in patients >65 years due to lymphoma and infection risk. No evidence supports methotrexate for UC maintenance — the MERIT-UC trial was negative.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G2 (ACG 2019) makes similar strong recommendations for oral and rectal 5-ASA maintenance (R16, R17) with moderate quality evidence — ECCO's "very low" quality rating is notably harsher. The thiopurine maintenance recommendation (R10) is stronger in ECCO (strong, moderate quality) than ACG (R32: conditional, low quality), reflecting the larger European experience with thiopurine monotherapy and the UK IBD BioResource data (Stournaras et al., 2021, n=11,928) showing thiopurine effectiveness in UC.

## Assessment

The "very low quality" ratings for 5-ASA maintenance are striking — they reflect GRADE's strict requirement for modern, well-reported RCTs, which don't exist for these older drugs. The strong recommendations despite very low evidence quality are justified by decades of clinical experience, low cost, and excellent safety. The ECCO's stronger thiopurine endorsement compared to ACG reflects genuine practice differences: European gastroenterologists are more comfortable with thiopurine monotherapy as a steroid-sparing strategy, while US practice has moved more rapidly toward biologics.
