# ecco-diag2019-11: MRI is the most accurate modality for perianal fistula classification; combining any two of MRI/TRUS/EUA achieves 100% accuracy

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

MRI is the most accurate modality for perianal fistula classification; combining any two of MRI/TRUS/EUA achieves 100% accuracy

## Evidence

MRI diagnostic accuracy: 80-100%. TRUS: 50-100% (variable — limited by discomfort, anal stenosis, smaller field of view). Schwartz et al. (2001): EUA 91%, MRI 87%, TRUS 91% accuracy individually; 100% when any two combined. Buchanan et al. (2004): digital rectal exam 33%, TRUS 75%, MRI 85% sensitivity. MRI changed management by detecting unsuspected abscesses; should precede EUA unless immediate sepsis drainage needed. Undiagnosed extensions/abscesses are major causes of recurrent disease after surgical cure.

**Intra-abdominal fistulae/abscesses (Statement 3.2.1):** Fistula: CT sensitivity 70%, specificity 97%; MRE 76%/96%; IUS 74%/95% [EL1]. MRI preferable for deep-seated or pelvic disease [EL4]. Abscess: IUS 84%/93%; CT 86%/88%; MRE 86%/93%. CEUS differentiates phlegmon from abscess.

**Perianal fistula monitoring (Statement 2.1.8):** Reassess by clinical evaluation + rectal endoscopy + MRI [EL1]; TRUS (without stenosis) [EL1] or TPUS [EL2] as MRI alternatives. MRI and endo-anal ultrasound may identify internal fistula tract persistence despite external closure — deep imaging healing is superior to clinical evaluation.

## Assessment

The 100% accuracy with any two combined modalities (Schwartz 2001) justifies the multimodality approach. The "deep healing" concept — imaging-detected internal tract persistence despite external closure — validates MRI as a more stringent endpoint than clinical examination.
