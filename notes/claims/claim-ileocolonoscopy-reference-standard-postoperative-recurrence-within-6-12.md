# ecco-diag2019-9: Ileocolonoscopy is the reference standard for postoperative recurrence within 6-12 months; FC, IUS, and MRE are non-invasive alternatives with excellent accuracy

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

Ileocolonoscopy is the reference standard for postoperative recurrence within 6-12 months; FC, IUS, and MRE are non-invasive alternatives with excellent accuracy

## Evidence

Without treatment, postoperative endoscopic recurrence: 65-90% within 12 months, 80-100% within 3 years. Rutgeerts score is used for description. After total proctocolectomy + ileostomy: 5-year/10-year median cumulative clinical recurrence 23.5%. **FC:** Meta-analysis (Qiu 2015, 10 studies): sensitivity 0.82, specificity 0.61. FC >100 µg/g: 89% sensitivity, 58% specificity for Rutgeerts ≥i2; NPV 91% — could avoid colonoscopy in 47%. **IUS:** Bowel wall >3.5 mm indicates recurrence; SICUS predicted recurrence correctly in 100% confirmed endoscopically. Bowel thickness >6 mm associated with 40% new surgical intervention vs. 13% for 3 mm. **MRE:** MRI-based index achieved high correlation with Rutgeerts score. Meta-analysis (Yung 2017): MRE, IUS, SBCE all had AUC >0.9 for detecting Rutgeerts ≥2. **SBCE:** Can detect proximal small bowel recurrence beyond anastomosis.

**Ileo-anal pouch (Statement 2.4.3):** Endoscopy with biopsies for pouch-related symptoms [EL2]. Up to 40% single pouchitis episode within 12 months; 19% intermittent, 5% chronic. Pouch failure: 7% at 3 years, 9% at 5 years. PDAI is the most common scoring system. FC ≥92.5 µg/g: 90% sensitivity, 76.5% specificity for pouch inflammation.

## Assessment

FC NPV of 91% for Rutgeerts ≥i2 means a negative FC spares nearly half of postoperative patients from unnecessary colonoscopy. IUS bowel wall cut-offs (>3.5 mm recurrence, >6 mm surgical risk) provide a graded assessment endoscopy alone cannot offer.
