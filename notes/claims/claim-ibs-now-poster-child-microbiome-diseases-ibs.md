# pimentel-ch3-3: IBS is the "poster child" of microbiome diseases -- the only GI disease with an FDA-approved microbiome-modulating drug

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Ch3 Gut Microbiome]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Ch 3

## The Claim

IBS is now the poster child of microbiome diseases... IBS remains the only gastrointestinal disease of the intestines for which the FDA has approved a microbiome-modulating drug (rifaximin).

## Evidence

Rifaximin's FDA approval for IBS-D (2015). The diagnostic triad now available: breath tests (identifying SIBO/IMO), blood tests (anti-vinculin and anti-CdtB antibodies), and treatments that modulate the microbiome (rifaximin, dietary changes). Pimentel contrasts IBS with other microbiome-associated diseases (obesity, Parkinson's, depression, C. difficile, IBD, hepatic encephalopathy) where the microbiome connection is still in the research stage.

## Stakes

This framing positions Pimentel's research program as the translational success story of microbiome science -- basic research (REIMAGINE, the 2002 MMC study) leading to an FDA-approved treatment (rifaximin) and diagnostic tests (breath testing, anti-vinculin/anti-CdtB blood tests). It implicitly argues for greater research funding for the IBS-microbiome connection.

## Assessment

Pimentel is correct about rifaximin's unique position as an FDA-approved microbiome modulator for IBS. However, rifaximin is a broad-spectrum antibiotic, not a targeted microbiome therapy. The field of true microbiome therapeutics (defined microbial consortia, engineered bacteriophages, postbiotics) has not yet produced an approved IBS therapy. Pimentel's claim is accurate but should not be confused with the kind of precision microbiome medicine still in development.
