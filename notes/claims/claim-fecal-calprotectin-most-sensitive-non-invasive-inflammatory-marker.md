# ecco-diag2019-3: Fecal calprotectin is the most sensitive non-invasive inflammatory marker; all newly diagnosed CD patients require small bowel assessment via MRE, IUS, and/or capsule endoscopy

**Source:** [[ECCO Diagnostic 2019 - Maaser]] — Maaser et al. — ECCO-ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD (2019)

## The Claim

Fecal calprotectin is the most sensitive non-invasive inflammatory marker; all newly diagnosed CD patients require small bowel assessment via MRE, IUS, and/or capsule endoscopy

## Evidence

**FC:** Most sensitive marker; good diagnostic accuracy at ~150 µg/g cut-off (meta-analysis, Lozoya Angulo 2017) but no exact IBD/IBS cut-off exists; lacks specificity to discriminate IBD from other intestinal inflammation. FC correlates well with endoscopic indices and is useful in initial diagnosis, relapse detection, and treatment monitoring. Likelihood of positive SBCE is very low with FC <50 µg/g.

**SBCE:** Two validated indexes (CECDAI, Lewis Score). Diagnostic yield comparable to MRE/IUS except proximal small bowel where SBCE is superior (OR 2.62; p=0.03). Capsule retention: ~1.5% in suspected CD, 2-13% in established CD. Use patency capsule if stenosis suspected. MRE overestimates obstruction risk (specificity 59% for predicting retention — ~40% of patients unnecessarily denied SBCE based on imaging alone). ≥3 small intestine ulcers on SBCE highly suggest CD (provided no NSAIDs for ≥1 month) [EL4]. In UC/unclassified IBD, SBCE reclassifies ~25% to CD; 37% remain unclassified. **Statement 1.12:** Balloon-assisted enteroscopy may confirm diagnosis histologically if endoscopy negative and MRE/SBCE positive [EL3].

**Ileocolonoscopy limitations:** Samuel et al. — 53.7% of CD patients with normal ileoscopy had active small bowel CD; disease can skip the distal ileum or be confined to the intramural portion and mesentery.

## Assessment

The 53.7% active small bowel CD with normal ileoscopy is the key number justifying mandatory small bowel assessment. The patency capsule specificity limitation (MRE predicts obstruction in only ~60% — meaning 40% unnecessarily denied SBCE) is an important clinical nuance.
