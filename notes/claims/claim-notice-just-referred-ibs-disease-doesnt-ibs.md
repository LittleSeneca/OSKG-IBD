# pimentel-ch1-2: IBS is a disease, not a syndrome -- the terminology itself has caused harm

**Source:** [[Pimentel 2022 - Intro Ch1 IBS and SIBO Overlap]] — Pimentel & Rezaie — The Microbiome Connection (2022), Intro + Ch1

## The Claim

Notice that we just referred to IBS as a 'disease.' Doesn't IBS stand for irritable bowel 'syndrome'? As you read through the chapters of this book you'll see that IBS is a legitimate 'disease' and that the word 'syndrome' may no longer apply.

## Evidence

The term "syndrome" historically leaned on "the now-dispelled belief that IBS is a disease of psychological origin." The authors argue that since SIBO provides an organic etiology, IBS should be reclassified. They cite the history of IBS being listed in the DSM-IV (until 2000) as evidence of the psychological framing's persistence.

## Stakes

The disease/syndrome distinction directly impacts research funding ($5 million/year NIH funding for IBS vs. $21 billion economic burden), physician attitudes (dismissal of IBS patients as "difficult"), and patient self-perception (internalized stigma). If IBS is recategorized as a microbiome disease, it would likely receive substantially more research funding.

## Assessment

Pimentel is right about the practical consequences of the "syndrome" label (stigma, underfunding, diagnostic nihilism), but the disease/syndrome terminology debate is a distraction from his more important scientific claim: that IBS has a definable organic etiology in the majority of cases. The Rome Foundation's own evolution from Rome I to Rome IV shows movement toward biological criteria, though no biomarkers have been incorporated yet.
