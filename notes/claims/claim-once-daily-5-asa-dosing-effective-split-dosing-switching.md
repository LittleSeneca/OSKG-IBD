# acg-uc2019-6: Once-daily 5-ASA dosing is as effective as split dosing; switching between 5-ASA formulations in nonresponders is futile

**Source:** [[ACG UC 2019 - Rubin]] — Rubin et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Ulcerative Colitis (2019)

## The Claim

Once-daily 5-ASA dosing is as effective as split dosing; switching between 5-ASA formulations in nonresponders is futile

## Evidence

Meta-analysis: low dose 2-2.4 g vs 4.8 g/d 5-ASA showed RR 0.91 (CI 0.85-0.98) -- the higher dose had a small but statistically significant advantage. Subgroup analysis suggested moderate-disease patients may benefit from 4.8 g/d. Once-daily vs split dosing: no efficacy difference (RR nonremission 0.95, CI 0.82-1.10). Community nonadherence rates reach 68% in patients on ≥4 prescription medications. No formal switch studies between 5-ASA formulations exist; meta-analyses show therapeutic equivalence.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G5 (AGA UC 2020) aligns on once-daily dosing and the futility of switching formulations. The low-vs-high dose recommendation is more controversial: the subgroup signal that moderate disease benefits from 4.8 g/d complicates the "low dose is fine" message.

## Assessment

R15 (once-daily dosing) is among the most clinically actionable recommendations -- adherence in real-world practice is ~40%, and once-daily dosing demonstrably improves it. R12 (don't switch 5-ASA formulations) is the most important negative recommendation for mild UC: it prevents wasteful cycling through mesalamine brands when patients need escalation. R13 (low dose sufficient) has the weakest evidence quality and the most clinical tension -- many experts would push to 4.8 g/d before declaring 5-ASA failure.
