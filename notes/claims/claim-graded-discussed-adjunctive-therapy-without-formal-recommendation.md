# acg-sibo2020-8: Dietary therapy for SIBO is based on reducing fermentable substrates; evidence is extrapolated from IBS

**Source:** [[ACG SIBO 2020 - Pimentel]] — Pimentel et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (2020)

## The Claim

Not graded. Discussed as adjunctive therapy without formal recommendation.

## Evidence

The dominant theme is reduction of fermentable products: low fiber, avoid alcohol sugars and fermentable sweeteners (sucralose), avoid prebiotics (inulin). The guideline cites a meta-analysis (reference 106) that found "very low quality evidence" for low FODMAP diets in IBS. However, it also cites mechanistic studies: daily hydrogen output is higher on high FODMAP diets (reference 107), and low FODMAP diets produce a small decrease in hydrogen production (reference 108, McIntosh et al.). The guideline does NOT make a formal dietary recommendation, reflecting the absence of SIBO-specific dietary trials.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
The AGA Practice Update is similarly noncommittal on diet. The Rome Foundation Report discusses diet in the context of functional GI disorders broadly. The gap between this guideline's dietary position and the clinical reality (where diet is central to SIBO management) is notable.

## Assessment

This is the guideline's weakest section. The statement that "data on using diet for SIBO are principally extensions of the data from IBS" is accurate but incomplete. Pimentel's own LFE protocol (published in book form in 2022) is SIBO-specific but lacks published trial data. The guideline was published in January 2020, just before the explosion of interest in SIBO-specific diets. A 2024 update would likely have a much stronger dietary section.
