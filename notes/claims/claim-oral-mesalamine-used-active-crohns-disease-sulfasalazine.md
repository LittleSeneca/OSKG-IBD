# acg-cd2018-8: Oral mesalamine should NOT be used for active Crohn's disease; sulfasalazine is effective only for colonic disease (R10-R11)

**Source:** [[ACG Crohn's 2018 - Lichtenstein]] — Lichtenstein et al. — ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Crohn's Disease (2018)

## The Claim

Oral mesalamine should NOT be used for active Crohn's disease; sulfasalazine is effective only for colonic disease (R10-R11)

## Evidence

The negative recommendation against mesalamine is one of the strongest in the guideline. Despite widespread use, multiple meta-analyses show no consistent benefit of mesalamine over placebo for induction or mucosal healing. Sulfasalazine is effective only in colonic/ileocolonic disease due to its colonic delivery mechanism. Topical 5-ASA (suppositories, enemas) has established efficacy in UC but limited benefit in CD.

**Cross-guideline comparison:**
G8 (ECCO 2020) similarly concludes mesalamine is ineffective for CD. G6 (AGA 2021) should agree. This recommendation directly contradicts common community practice patterns where mesalamine remains widely prescribed for CD.

## Assessment

This is one of the most important "negative" recommendations in the guideline. The strong language ("should not be used") is notable — the panel is attempting to change entrenched practice. The "moderate" quality of evidence gives this real weight. The persistence of mesalamine prescribing for CD despite this recommendation reflects the gap between guideline evidence and real-world practice.
