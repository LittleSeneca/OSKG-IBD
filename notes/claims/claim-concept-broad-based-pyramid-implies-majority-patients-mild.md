# yamada-cd64-8: The therapeutic pyramid (step-up) model is obsolete; early effective therapy prevents bowel damage

**Source:** [[Yamada 7E - Ch64 Crohn's Disease Management]] — Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7E (2022), Ch 64

## The Claim

The concept of a broad-based pyramid implies that the majority of patients have mild, uncomplicated, and nonprogressive disease. Although many patients will have mild disease 5 years after diagnosis, the long-term clinical course suggests gradual progression toward fibrostenotic and penetrating complications for the majority of patients over time.

## Evidence

Natural history studies showing progressive complications. The TREAT safety registry: concomitant corticosteroid or narcotic use were each associated with increased mortality -- challenging the assumption that "lower" therapies on the pyramid are safer. The treatment algorithm (Figure 64.15) stratifies by disease severity AND prognosis, with anti-TNF + immunomodulator positioned as first-line for moderate-severe disease.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** HIGH. The pyramid model has been replaced by risk-stratified early intervention. The evidence that biologics are more effective earlier in the disease course (before irreversible bowel damage) is cons

## Stakes

If the pyramid is wrong, then "step-up" therapy (starting with safest/weakest drugs and escalating on failure) allows preventable bowel damage. The paradigm shift toward early effective therapy is the most significant change in CD management philosophy in the biologic era.

## Assessment

The authors dismantle the pyramid model systematically: (1) it assumes most patients have mild disease (false -- natural history shows progression), (2) it assumes lower-tier therapies are safer (false -- steroid/narcotic mortality in TREAT), (3) it ignores the window of opportunity for biologics to prevent damage. This is the strongest section of the chapter from a conceptual standpoint.

**Strongest section:** The treatment algorithm (Figure 64.15) and Table 64.4 (special considerations) together provide a clinically actionable framework. The evidence synthesis for each drug class is methodical, with systematic review data, number-needed-to-treat/harm where available, and explicit acknowledgment of evidence gaps.

**Weakest section:** The nutrition/diet section is notably thin for a chapter of this scope. EEN is mentioned for pediatrics but adult dietary strategies receive minimal attention. Given the patient interest in diet as therapy and the emerging evidence base (CDED, Mediterranean diet), this gap is significant.

**Overall quality:** This is the definitive reference chapter for CD management. The authors at Cedars-Sinai bring both research depth and clinical pragmatism. The treatment algorithm is evidence-based, the drug class discussions include both trial data and practical monitoring guidance, and the special populations section addresses the most common clinical scenarios (abscess, TB, pregnancy, elderly). The chapter's conceptual framework -- treat-to-target, early effective therapy, risk-stratified management -- reflects the 2022 state of the art.
