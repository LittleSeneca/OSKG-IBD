# AGA Clinical Practice Guideline: Medical Management of Moderate to Severe Luminal and Fistulizing Crohn's Disease (Feuerstein et al., 2021)

The first AGA formal clinical practice guideline on moderate to severe Crohn's disease, developed using the GRADE framework with 11 PICO-based recommendations. Unlike the ACG 2018 guideline (which covers all severity levels), this guideline is tightly scoped to moderate-severe luminal and perianal fistulizing CD. Its signature contribution is a **network meta-analysis** providing the first evidence-based biologic ranking: infliximab and adalimumab emerge as most effective in biologic-naive patients, ustekinumab is recommended for primary anti-TNF nonresponse, and natalizumab is recommended **against** due to PML risk — a reversal from the ACG 2018 position. The guideline also introduces an explicit recommendation for **early biologic introduction** (R7), breaking from the traditional step-up paradigm. Accompanied by a 46-page technical review (Singh et al., 2021) with detailed evidence profiles. TDM is deferred to the separate 2017 AGA guideline; postoperative prophylaxis is not addressed.

---

## Scope and Methodology

### Claim 1: This is a GRADE guideline with PICO-structured questions, supported by the first formal network meta-analysis in a CD guideline

**Scope:** Adult outpatients with moderate to severe luminal CD (CDAI ≥220 or high-risk features per IOIBD criteria) and perianal fistulizing CD. Surgical management, TDM, and postoperative prophylaxis are excluded — the latter is a notable gap compared with ACG 2018.

**Methodology:** The technical review panel performed systematic searches for each PICO question. A network meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (1,458 biologic-naive patients) and 6 RCTs (1,606 anti-TNF-exposed patients) provided indirect comparative effectiveness data — the first time a CD guideline has formally ranked biologics. The MCID was set at 10% absolute benefit over placebo. GRADE evidence-to-decision framework guided recommendation strength.

**GRADE ratings across recommendations:** Of 15 recommendation statements: 9 are strong, 6 are conditional. Evidence certainty: moderate (7), low (5), very low (2), knowledge gap (1). Notably, no recommendation achieved "high" certainty — reflecting the reliance on indirect comparisons and imprecision from small trial populations. This contrasts with ACG 2018, which had 5 "high" quality recommendations (all for individual biologics vs. placebo, not comparative rankings).

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** ACG 2018 used pairwise (drug vs. placebo) comparisons to generate 60 GRADE recommendations. The AGA 2021 network meta-analysis approach is more clinically relevant (answers "which drug?" not just "does this drug work?") but carries the limitations of indirect comparisons. ACG 2018 addressed the full disease spectrum (mild through fulminant, postoperative, surgery); AGA 2021's tight scope enables deeper analysis of the moderate-severe treatment decision but leaves the postoperative and mild-disease clinician without guidance.

**My assessment:** The network meta-analysis is this guideline's most important methodological contribution. For the first time, clinicians have evidence-based (if indirect) guidance on biologic sequencing. However, the authors appropriately flag the limitations: no head-to-head trials exist; the analysis assumes similar trial populations and designs; and real-world patients (elderly, multi-class failures, comorbid) differ from trial populations. The MCID threshold of 10% is reasonable but means that drugs with 5-9% absolute benefit (certolizumab, vedolizumab for induction) fall below the clinical significance bar — a judgment call that some clinicians may dispute.

## Biologics in Biologic-Naive Patients

### Claim 2: Infliximab, adalimumab, and ustekinumab are strongly recommended over no treatment; vedolizumab is conditionally recommended; natalizumab is recommended against (R1A-D)

**Recommendation 1A:** "In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA recommends the use of anti-TNFα over no treatment for induction and maintenance of remission." **Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.** Comment: evidence for infliximab and adalimumab was moderate quality; certolizumab pegol was low quality.

**Recommendation 1B:** "The AGA suggests the use of vedolizumab over no treatment." **Conditional recommendation, low certainty for induction, moderate for maintenance.**

**Recommendation 1C:** "The AGA recommends the use of ustekinumab over no treatment." **Strong recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.**

**Recommendation 1D:** "The AGA suggests against the use of natalizumab over no treatment." **Conditional recommendation, moderate certainty evidence.** Comment: patients who are JC virus antibody-negative, who value potential benefits highly and accept PML risk, and who will adhere to monitoring, may reasonably consider natalizumab.

**Evidence summary:** 13 RCTs for induction (4-12 weeks), 9 RCTs for maintenance (22-54 weeks). All interventions were superior to placebo. Key RRs for failure to induce remission: infliximab 0.54 (95% CI 0.39-0.75), adalimumab 0.82 (0.75-0.89), certolizumab pegol 0.92 (0.86-0.98), vedolizumab 0.92 (0.87-0.97), ustekinumab 0.90 (0.85-0.94). Certolizumab and vedolizumab did not meet the MCID of 10% absolute benefit for induction — hence the downgraded ratings. Natalizumab did show benefit (induction RR 0.88, maintenance RR 0.58) but the PML risk and availability of safer alternatives drove the recommendation against use.

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** The biggest shift is natalizumab. ACG 2018 gave natalizumab a **strong** recommendation with **high** quality evidence (R28-R29), restricted only by JC virus status. AGA 2021 reverses this: natalizumab is now "suggested against" despite the same efficacy data, because the risk-benefit calculus changed with the availability of ustekinumab and vedolizumab. This is a case study in how the therapeutic landscape — not new efficacy data — can flip a guideline recommendation. The vedolizumab downgrade (conditional vs. ACG 2018's strong) reflects the MCID analysis: vedolizumab's induction benefit over placebo is at the margin of clinical significance, and its slower onset in anti-TNF-experienced patients reduces clinician confidence.

**My assessment:** The natalizumab reversal is the most clinically consequential single recommendation. It effectively removes natalizumab from the CD armamentarium for all but the most exceptional cases. The differential treatment of anti-TNF agents (infliximab/adalimumab "recommended" vs. certolizumab "low quality") foreshadows the formal ranking in R2A. The ustekinumab "strong" recommendation at the same tier as anti-TNF agents was forward-looking in 2021 — ustekinumab was the newest biologic and this recommendation positioned it as a legitimate first-line option, not just a second-line after anti-TNF failure.

## Biologic Selection by Treatment History

### Claim 3: In biologic-naive patients, infliximab, adalimumab, or ustekinumab are preferred over certolizumab; in primary nonresponders to anti-TNF, ustekinumab is preferred (R2A-C)

**Recommendation 2A (biologic-naive):** "The AGA recommends the use of infliximab, adalimumab, or ustekinumab over certolizumab pegol for induction of remission (**strong, moderate certainty**) and suggests vedolizumab over certolizumab pegol (**conditional, low certainty**)."

**Recommendation 2B (primary nonresponse):** "The AGA recommends ustekinumab (**strong, moderate certainty**) and suggests vedolizumab (**conditional, low certainty**) over no treatment for induction of remission."

**Recommendation 2C (secondary nonresponse to infliximab):** "The AGA recommends adalimumab or ustekinumab (**strong, moderate certainty**) and suggests vedolizumab (**conditional, low certainty**) over no treatment." Comment: if adalimumab was first-line, indirect evidence supports infliximab as second-line.

**Evidence summary (network meta-analysis):** Among biologic-naive patients (8 RCTs, 1,458 patients): infliximab was superior to certolizumab pegol (OR 4.33, 95% CI 1.83-10.27) with moderate confidence. Infliximab trended toward superiority over vedolizumab (OR 2.20, 0.79-6.07) and ustekinumab (OR 2.14, 0.89-5.15) but confidence intervals crossed unity. Ustekinumab (OR 2.02, 1.09-3.75) and adalimumab (OR 2.97, 1.16-6.70) were superior to certolizumab pegol. No significant differences among adalimumab, ustekinumab, and vedolizumab as first-line agents (very low certainty).

Among anti-TNF-exposed patients (6 RCTs, 1,606 patients): ustekinumab was superior to placebo (OR 2.58, 1.50-4.44) with moderate certainty. Adalimumab in patients with prior infliximab intolerance or secondary nonresponse was effective (OR 3.57, 1.66-7.65). Vedolizumab's effect was uncertain (OR 1.53, 0.77-3.06) with wide confidence intervals crossing unity — hence the conditional recommendation.

**Key limitation:** Studies did not consistently report the number of prior biologics, number of drug classes failed, or reasons for prior biologic failure (primary vs. secondary nonresponse vs. intolerance). The guideline acknowledges that TDM results should inform the choice between same-class switch vs. out-of-class switch.

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** ACG 2018 provided no ranking — all biologics were individually recommended with similar GRADE ratings. The question of "which biologic first?" was explicitly unanswered. AGA 2021 fills this gap via network meta-analysis. The certolizumab downgrade is particularly notable: ACG 2018 treated all anti-TNF agents as a class; AGA 2021 identifies certolizumab as inferior based on indirect comparisons. This has real-world implications for formulary positioning and prior authorization.

**My assessment:** The certolizumab downgrade is the sharpest edge in this guideline. It's based on indirect evidence (no head-to-head trials), and certolizumab's unique attributes (subcutaneous only, no placental transfer in third trimester — making it preferred in pregnancy) are not captured in the network meta-analysis. The guideline's treatment of secondary nonresponse is pragmatic but imprecise: "secondary nonresponse to infliximab" encompasses both immune-mediated failure (anti-drug antibodies) and non-immune pharmacokinetic failure (rapid clearance from high inflammatory burden). TDM-guided decision-making (the 2017 AGA guideline) would direct the former to same-class switch and the latter to dose escalation — but this guideline's R2C recommends adalimumab or ustekinumab regardless of mechanism. The tension between these two AGA guidelines (2021 biologic selection vs. 2017 TDM) is unresolved.

## Immunomodulators: Limited and Route-Specific Roles

### Claim 4: Thiopurines are not recommended for induction but suggested for maintenance; SC/IM methotrexate is suggested; oral methotrexate is recommended against (R3A-D, R4)

**Recommendation 3A:** "The AGA suggests against the use of thiopurines monotherapy over no treatment for achieving remission." **Conditional, very low certainty.** 5 trials, 380 patients — thiopurines did not increase corticosteroid-free remission vs. placebo. Evidence rated down for serious bias, indirectness, and serious imprecision.

**Recommendation 3B:** "In patients with quiescent CD or corticosteroid-induced remission, the AGA suggests thiopurines monotherapy over no treatment for maintenance." **Conditional, low certainty.** 5 RCTs: thiopurines superior to placebo for maintaining corticosteroid-free clinical remission (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.47-0.81). Rated down for lack of blinding and imprecision.

**Recommendation 3C:** "The AGA suggests SC or IM methotrexate monotherapy over no treatment for induction and maintenance." **Conditional, moderate certainty.** SC MTX 25 mg/week: effective for induction (RR 0.75, 0.61-0.93) in 1 trial of 141 patients. SC MTX 15 mg/week: effective for maintenance (RR 0.57, 0.34-0.94) in 1 trial of 76 patients. Moderate certainty — the strongest evidence for any immunomodulator in this guideline.

**Recommendation 3D:** "The AGA suggests against oral methotrexate monotherapy." **Conditional, very low certainty.** Oral MTX 12.5 mg/week: not effective for induction (RR 1.14, 0.72-1.82) or maintenance (RR 0.30, 0.04-2.27). The panel noted this dose may be suboptimal, but no data exist on higher-dose oral MTX.

**Recommendation 4:** "The AGA recommends biologic drug monotherapy over thiopurine monotherapy for induction of remission." **Strong, moderate certainty.** Based on SONIC trial: infliximab superior to azathioprine for clinical remission (RR 0.79, 0.67-0.94) and endoscopic remission (P < 0.01). Indirect evidence extends this finding to other biologics given thiopurines' consistent failure to demonstrate induction efficacy.

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** ACG 2018 gave thiopurines a **strong** recommendation for maintenance (R21, moderate evidence) vs. AGA 2021's **conditional** recommendation (low evidence). The downgrade reflects more stringent GRADE methodology (penalizing unblinded trials more heavily) rather than new data. ACG 2018's methotrexate recommendation (R23) was also conditional but did not distinguish between routes — AGA 2021's separation of SC/IM from oral methotrexate is new and clinically useful. ACG 2018 gave SC MTX a single "conditional, low" recommendation; AGA 2021 upgrades this to "conditional, moderate" for SC/IM while adding the negative oral MTX recommendation.

**My assessment:** The oral methotrexate recommendation (R3D) is the most practically useful immunomodulator guidance. Many clinicians prescribe oral methotrexate for convenience, unaware that the only RCT used a dose (12.5 mg/week) that may be subtherapeutic. The guideline is honest about this limitation — it's possible that oral MTX 25 mg/week would work, but there are zero data. The separation of induction from maintenance for thiopurines (R3A vs. R3B) highlights the 8-12 week lag to clinical effect — a point ACG 2018 also made but AGA 2021 formalizes with separate GRADE ratings. The "biologic over thiopurine" recommendation (R4) effectively closes the door on thiopurine monotherapy as a first-line strategy for moderate-severe CD.

## Combination Therapy: Variable Evidence by Biologic

### Claim 5: Infliximab + thiopurine combination is suggested; adalimumab + thiopurine is suggested with very low certainty; no recommendation for vedolizumab or ustekinumab combination (R5A-C, R6)

**Recommendation 5A:** "In biologic and immunomodulator-naive patients, the AGA suggests infliximab + thiopurines over infliximab monotherapy." **Conditional, moderate certainty.** Two trials: combination therapy more effective for induction (RR 0.77, 0.64-0.92) and maintenance (RR 0.74, 0.60-0.90). The infliximab + methotrexate trial (126 patients) showed no difference vs. infliximab monotherapy (RR 1.07 for induction, very serious imprecision).

**Recommendation 5B:** "The AGA suggests adalimumab + thiopurines over adalimumab monotherapy." **Conditional, very low certainty.** DIAMOND trial (open-label): no difference for clinical remission, but higher endoscopic remission at week 26 with combination (84.2% vs. 63.2%, P = 0.02). Rated down for risk of bias (unblinded, high discontinuation), indirectness, and imprecision.

**Recommendation 5C:** "No recommendation regarding ustekinumab or vedolizumab in combination with thiopurines or methotrexate." **No recommendation, knowledge gap.** Zero RCTs exist.

**Recommendation 6:** "No recommendation for withdrawal of either immunomodulator or biologic over ongoing combination therapy in quiescent CD." **No recommendation, knowledge gap.** 3 RCTs, 161 patients: no significant difference in relapse risk at 12-24 months (RR 1.02, 0.71-1.46). Evidence rated very low — unable to exclude significant benefit or harm of either strategy. However, one prospective cohort study (Louis 2012) found 44% relapse at 1 year and 52% at 2 years after infliximab discontinuation while continuing immunomodulator.

**Important mechanistic insight provided by the guideline:** The benefit of combination therapy is likely mediated through improved drug levels and reduced anti-drug antibody formation. This has two clinical implications: (1) TDM-guided proactive dose optimization might achieve the same benefit as combination therapy without the added immunosuppressive risk; (2) newer biologics (vedolizumab, ustekinumab) are less immunogenic, so combination therapy may not provide the same incremental benefit. The guideline also cites Roblin et al. (2020): in patients with secondary nonresponse, adding a thiopurine at the time of anti-TNF switch improved outcomes (77-78% success with combination vs. 22% with monotherapy at 24 months, P < 0.001).

**Harms:** Combination therapy carries a 2-3 fold higher lymphoma risk compared with anti-TNF monotherapy when adding a thiopurine (Khan 2013).

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** This is the area of greatest divergence. ACG 2018 (R26) gave combination therapy a **strong** recommendation with **high** quality evidence — one of only 5 such ratings in the entire guideline, based primarily on SONIC. AGA 2021 gives only a **conditional** recommendation with **moderate** certainty. The difference reflects: (1) AGA 2021's consideration of the DIAMOND trial (negative for adalimumab combination), which was not available when ACG 2018 was drafted; (2) AGA 2021's explicit acknowledgment that TDM-guided monotherapy might achieve equivalent outcomes; (3) different GRADE methodology — AGA 2021 penalizes the unblinded DIAMOND trial and the indirectness of maintenance data derived from active-disease cohorts. ACG 2018 endorsed combination therapy for all anti-TNF agents; AGA 2021 stratifies by biologic and acknowledges that the evidence base for adalimumab combination is substantially weaker.

**My assessment:** R5A-R5C represent a more nuanced, less dogmatic approach to combination therapy than ACG 2018. The stratification by biologic is clinically rational: infliximab is the most immunogenic anti-TNF and benefits most from combination; adalimumab is less immunogenic and the benefit is uncertain; vedolizumab and ustekinumab have minimal immunogenicity and combination therapy is untested. However, Recommendation 6's "no recommendation" on de-escalation is frustrating for clinicians managing patients on long-term combination therapy who want to reduce immunosuppression. The Roblin 2020 data (adding thiopurine at time of switch) is a practice-changing pearl buried in the discussion — it suggests that even patients with pharmacokinetic failure may benefit from combination if switching within class.

## Early Biologic Introduction: A Paradigm Shift

### Claim 6: Early biologic therapy with or without an immunomodulator is suggested over step-up therapy (R7)

**Recommendation 7:** "In adult outpatients with moderate to severe CD, the AGA suggests early introduction of a biologic with or without an immunomodulator rather than delaying their use until after failure of 5-aminosalicylates and/or corticosteroids." **Conditional recommendation, low certainty evidence.**

**Evidence summary:** Three key trials:
- **D'Haens et al. (2008):** Early combination therapy (infliximab + azathioprine) vs. conventional step-up (corticosteroids → azathioprine → infliximab). At 52 weeks, 61.5% in early combination arm achieved corticosteroid- and surgery-free remission vs. 42.2% in step-up (RR for failure 0.67, 0.46-0.97). 8-year extension: lower clinical relapse and corticosteroid use in early combination group.
- **REACT (Khanna 2015):** Cluster-randomized trial of 1,982 patients comparing algorithmic early combination therapy vs. conventional management. No difference in corticosteroid-free remission at 12 months (66% vs. 62%), but at 24 months, early combination therapy reduced major adverse disease-related complications (HR 0.73, 0.62-0.86).
- **Cosnes et al. (2013):** Early azathioprine alone (within 6 months of diagnosis) vs. conventional management in 122 patients. No significant differences at 3 years in corticosteroid-requiring flares (89% vs. 91%), hospitalization (34% vs. 39%), or surgery (8% vs. 6%). This trial effectively demonstrated that early thiopurine monotherapy — without a biologic — does not alter disease course.

**The panel's reasoning:** The step-up paradigm risks harm from disease progression due to inadequate therapy. The early biologic paradigm risks overtreating some patients and exposing them to treatment-related harms and costs. The panel judged that the risk of undertreatment (progressive bowel damage from uncontrolled inflammation) outweighed the risk of overtreatment.

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** ACG 2018's R16 allowed "symptomatic treatment with careful observation" for low-risk patients — effectively endorsing a watch-and-wait approach. The ACG guideline did not have a formal recommendation for early biologic therapy. AGA 2021's R7 is the most explicit early-treatment recommendation in any US CD guideline. However, it is conditional with low certainty — the panel is saying "we think this is right, but the evidence isn't strong enough to be dogmatic." The ECCO 2020 guideline (G8) goes further, providing risk-stratified algorithms that direct high-risk patients to early biologic therapy.

**My assessment:** R7 is philosophically the most important recommendation in the guideline, even though it's conditional with low-quality evidence. It represents a fundamental shift from "don't use a bazooka to kill a fly" (the traditional step-up philosophy) to "the fly might be a hornet — and by the time you know, the damage is done." The REACT trial's finding — that clinical remission rates were similar at 12 months but complications diverged at 24 months — captures the rationale: early biologic therapy may not make patients feel better faster, but it may prevent the structural damage that accumulates during months of inadequate treatment. The panel's acknowledgment that this strategy "may result in overtreating some patients" is honest — risk stratification tools to identify which patients need early biologics remain imprecise.

## Corticosteroids and 5-ASA: What Doesn't Work

### Claim 7: Corticosteroids are suggested for induction, strongly recommended against for maintenance; 5-ASA and sulfasalazine are recommended against (R8A-B, R9)

**Recommendation 8A:** "The AGA suggests corticosteroids over no treatment for induction of remission." **Conditional, moderate certainty.** Prednisone up to 60 mg/day: superior to placebo (RR 0.57, 0.45-0.73). Budesonide: superior to placebo (RR 0.74, 0.60-0.91), though 2 of 3 studies enrolled mild-moderate CD — indirectness concern. Budesonide was inferior to systemic corticosteroids for induction (RR for failure 1.20, 1.01-1.44), but patients with distal ileal/ascending colon disease who prioritize safety over efficacy may reasonably choose budesonide.

**Recommendation 8B:** "The AGA recommends against corticosteroids for maintenance of remission." **Strong, moderate certainty.** 3 trials, 269 patients: no difference vs. placebo (RR 1.02, 0.81-1.29).

**Recommendation 9:** "The AGA recommends against 5-aminosalicylates or sulfasalazine for induction or maintenance of remission in moderate to severe CD." **Strong, moderate certainty.** 2 RCTs for 5-ASA induction: did not meet MCID (RR 0.90, 0.81-1.00). For sulfasalazine induction: 3 RCTs showed benefit (RR 0.78, 0.65-0.93), but disease severity was unclear — may not apply to moderate-severe CD. For maintenance: 4 sulfasalazine studies (415 patients) and 11 5-ASA studies (2,014 patients) showed no benefit (RR 0.98 and 1.02, respectively).

**Key clinical reasoning from the panel:** "Most drugs started for induction should be continued for maintenance, and starting a drug that is ineffective can lead to delays in appropriate therapy and worsening disease." This logic extends the negative recommendation beyond the specific evidence: even if 5-ASA had marginal induction benefit, using it as a bridge delays effective therapy. The panel explicitly notes sulfasalazine's adverse effect burden as additional reason to avoid it.

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** ACG 2018 also recommended against 5-ASA for induction (R11, strong) and maintenance (R46, strong), and against corticosteroids for maintenance (R47, strong). However, ACG 2018 **conditionally endorsed** sulfasalazine 3-6 g/day for mild-moderate colonic CD (R10). AGA 2021's R9 goes further — it recommends against sulfasalazine for moderate-severe CD, collapsing the distinction ACG 2018 made between mesalamine and sulfasalazine. The divergence is partly scope-driven: ACG 2018 addressed mild-moderate disease where sulfasalazine's marginal benefit might apply; AGA 2021 addresses only moderate-severe disease where even sulfasalazine's NNT is too high to justify its use.

**My assessment:** These negative recommendations are the least controversial section of the guideline — there is broad consensus across all CD guidelines that steroids don't maintain remission and 5-ASA doesn't work for CD. The budesonide caveat (patients who prioritize safety may reasonably choose it despite lower efficacy) is a practical acknowledgment that shared decision-making sometimes favors less effective but safer options. The frank statement about budesonide's inferiority to systemic steroids is useful — budesonide is often presented as "just as good but safer," which is not what the data show.

## Fistulizing Disease: Infliximab Remains the Standard

### Claim 8: Infliximab has the best evidence for perianal fistula closure; adalimumab, ustekinumab, and vedolizumab have weaker supporting data; antibiotics should be combined with biologics, not used alone (R10A-C, R11)

**Recommendation 10A:** "The AGA recommends infliximab over no treatment for induction and maintenance of fistula remission." **Strong, moderate certainty.** The only biologic with a dedicated fistula RCT: 94 patients, infliximab superior to placebo for fistula closure at 18 weeks (RR 0.52, 0.34-0.78). Moderate certainty — rated down only for imprecision (low OIS).

**Recommendation 10B:** "The AGA suggests adalimumab, ustekinumab, or vedolizumab over no treatment for induction or maintenance of fistula remission." **Conditional, low certainty.** All data are from subgroup/post hoc analyses:
- Adalimumab: subgroup of 77 patients from 2 RCTs — not effective for fistula closure at 4 weeks (RR 1.08, 0.93-1.27), but network meta-analysis suggests benefit for luminal disease, and indirect evidence supports fistula efficacy.
- Certolizumab pegol: subgroup of 165 patients — no benefit (RR 1.01, 0.80-1.27). The guideline explicitly notes "evidence suggests certolizumab pegol may not be effective."
- Vedolizumab: subgroup of 165 patients with draining fistulas who had luminal response — may improve fistula closure (RR 0.81, 0.63-1.04), very low certainty.
- Ustekinumab: pooled analysis of 4 trials, 238 patients — effective for fistula remission (RR 0.85, 0.73-1.99 [sic — CI appears to be 0.73-0.99 in corrected version]). Low certainty.

**Recommendation 10C:** "The AGA suggests against antibiotics alone for induction of fistula remission." **Conditional, low certainty.** Single 3-arm RCT of 25 patients (ciprofloxacin, metronidazole, placebo): no benefit (RR 0.94, 0.67-1.33).

**Recommendation 11:** "The AGA recommends biologic agents in combination with an antibiotic over a biologic alone for induction of fistula remission." **Strong, moderate certainty.** Two RCTs: anti-TNF + ciprofloxacin for 12 weeks significantly improved fistula closure vs. anti-TNF alone (RR 0.42, 0.26-0.68).

**Evidence hierarchy for fistula efficacy:**
1. Infliximab — dedicated prospective RCT (strong, moderate)
2. Adalimumab — subgroup analysis + indirect evidence (conditional, low)
3. Ustekinumab — pooled post hoc analysis (conditional, low)
4. Vedolizumab — single subgroup analysis (conditional, low)
5. Certolizumab pegol — evidence suggests ineffective
6. Antibiotics alone — evidence suggests ineffective
7. Antibiotics + biologic — strong recommendation for combination

**Comparison against ACG Crohn's 2018 (G1):** ACG 2018 had an extensive fistulizing disease section (R35-R43, 9 recommendations) covering infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab, thiopurines, tacrolimus, antibiotics, abscess drainage, and seton placement. AGA 2021's fistulizing section is narrower — focused exclusively on medical therapy for perianal fistulas without abscess. The agreement on infliximab as best-evidenced agent is consistent. Key differences: (1) ACG 2018 recommended thiopurines for fistulizing CD (R38) — AGA 2021 found insufficient evidence for any recommendation; (2) ACG 2018 included tacrolimus for short-term fistula treatment (R39) — AGA 2021 does not address it; (3) ACG 2018 recommended abscess drainage and seton placement (R42-R43) — AGA 2021 explicitly excludes surgical management from scope; (4) certolizumab's downgrade from "may be effective" (ACG 2018 R37) to "may not be effective" (AGA 2021) is a significant shift.

**My assessment:** R10A is the most solid — infliximab is the only drug with a dedicated fistula RCT, and 18 years after that trial (Present 1999), it remains the standard. The certolizumab downgrade is notable: two subgroup analyses totaling 165 patients showed no benefit, and this, combined with certolizumab's weak luminal efficacy, drove the negative signal. The antibiotic combination recommendation (R11) is a practice-changing pearl — adding ciprofloxacin to anti-TNF therapy nearly doubles the fistula closure rate (RR 0.42). This is a low-cost, low-risk intervention with moderate-quality evidence. The guideline does not address the practical questions clinicians face: when to image (MRI pelvis vs. EUA), when to place setons, and when to refer for surgery — all of which ACG 2018 addressed.

### Strongest Section
**Biologic selection by treatment history (R2A-C).** The network meta-analysis provides the first evidence-based answer to "which biologic?" — the most common clinical question in CD management. The stratification by biologic-naive, primary nonresponse, and secondary nonresponse gives clinicians a decision framework that ACG 2018 could not provide. The certolizumab downgrade, while based on indirect comparisons, is clinically actionable.

### Weakest Section
**De-escalation and treatment discontinuation.** Recommendation 6 ("no recommendation" on withdrawal from combination therapy) and the absence of any guidance on biologic discontinuation in deep remission are honest but leave clinicians without direction on the second most common question after "which drug to start": "when can we stop?" The guideline acknowledges that infliximab discontinuation while continuing immunomodulator carries ~50% relapse risk at 2 years (Louis 2012), but makes no recommendation. The absence of postoperative prophylaxis — a strength of ACG 2018 — is a significant gap for surgeons and gastroenterologists managing post-resection patients.

### Most Controversial Claims
1. **Natalizumab recommended against (R1D).** Reverses ACG 2018. Some clinicians argue that JC virus-negative patients with multi-class biologic failure deserve access to all options. The guideline's comment acknowledges this carve-out but the headline recommendation is negative.
2. **Certolizumab ranked inferior (R2A).** Based on network meta-analysis of indirect comparisons — no head-to-head data. Certolizumab's unique profile (pregnancy safety, subcutaneous-only administration) is not captured in efficacy rankings.
3. **Early biologic introduction (R7).** The "conditional, low certainty" rating reflects the genuine uncertainty. Some clinicians will argue this leads to overtreatment; others will argue it doesn't go far enough (should be a "strong" recommendation).

### Key Take-Home Messages
1. **Infliximab and adalimumab remain first-line biologics** — the network meta-analysis supports their position at the top of the efficacy ranking for biologic-naive patients.
2. **Ustekinumab is the preferred agent after anti-TNF failure** — strong recommendation for primary nonresponse; strong recommendation (alongside adalimumab) for secondary nonresponse.
3. **Natalizumab should not be used routinely** — the risk-benefit calculus has shifted with safer alternatives available. This is the single biggest change from ACG 2018.
4. **Combination therapy is nuanced** — infliximab + thiopurine is suggested; adalimumab + thiopurine has very weak evidence; no data for vedolizumab/ustekinumab combinations.
5. **Start biologics early** — the step-up paradigm risks disease progression. Conditional recommendation but philosophically the most important shift.
6. **Thiopurines have no role in induction** — and biologic monotherapy is superior. For maintenance, thiopurines and SC/IM methotrexate are options, but oral methotrexate should not be used.
7. **Infliximab is the best-evidenced agent for perianal fistulas** — and adding ciprofloxacin substantially improves fistula closure rates.
8. **5-ASA and corticosteroids have no maintenance role** — and in moderate-severe CD, 5-ASA should not be used at all.
9. **Two critical knowledge gaps acknowledged:** (a) no evidence to guide de-escalation of combination therapy, and (b) no evidence on combination therapy with non-anti-TNF biologics.
