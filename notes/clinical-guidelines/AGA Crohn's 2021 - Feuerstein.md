# AGA Clinical Practice Guideline: Medical Management of Moderate to Severe Luminal and Fistulizing Crohn's Disease (Feuerstein et al., 2021)

The first AGA formal clinical practice guideline on moderate to severe Crohn's disease, developed using the GRADE framework with 11 PICO-based recommendations. Unlike the ACG 2018 guideline (which covers all severity levels), this guideline is tightly scoped to moderate-severe luminal and perianal fistulizing CD. Its signature contribution is a **network meta-analysis** providing the first evidence-based biologic ranking: infliximab and adalimumab emerge as most effective in biologic-naive patients, ustekinumab is recommended for primary anti-TNF nonresponse, and natalizumab is recommended **against** due to PML risk — a reversal from the ACG 2018 position. The guideline also introduces an explicit recommendation for **early biologic introduction** (R7), breaking from the traditional step-up paradigm. Accompanied by a 46-page technical review (Singh et al., 2021) with detailed evidence profiles. TDM is deferred to the separate 2017 AGA guideline; postoperative prophylaxis is not addressed.

**GRADE distribution:** 15 recommendation statements (across 11 PICO questions). Strong: 9. Conditional: 6. Evidence: moderate (7), low (5), very low (2), knowledge gap (1). Notably, no recommendation achieved "high" certainty — in contrast to ACG 2018's 5 "high" ratings.

### Strongest Section
**Biologic selection by treatment history (R2A-C).** The network meta-analysis provides the first evidence-based answer to "which biologic?" — the most common clinical question in CD management. The stratification by biologic-naive, primary nonresponse, and secondary nonresponse gives clinicians a decision framework that ACG 2018 could not provide. The certolizumab downgrade, while based on indirect comparisons, is clinically actionable.

### Weakest Section
**De-escalation and treatment discontinuation.** Recommendation 6 ("no recommendation" on withdrawal from combination therapy) and the absence of any guidance on biologic discontinuation in deep remission are honest but leave clinicians without direction on the second most common question after "which drug to start": "when can we stop?" The guideline acknowledges that infliximab discontinuation while continuing immunomodulator carries ~50% relapse risk at 2 years (Louis 2012), but makes no recommendation. The absence of postoperative prophylaxis — a strength of ACG 2018 — is a significant gap for surgeons and gastroenterologists managing post-resection patients.

### Most Controversial Claims
1. **Natalizumab recommended against (R1D).** Reverses ACG 2018. Some clinicians argue that JC virus-negative patients with multi-class biologic failure deserve access to all options. The guideline's comment acknowledges this carve-out but the headline recommendation is negative.
2. **Certolizumab ranked inferior (R2A).** Based on network meta-analysis of indirect comparisons — no head-to-head data. Certolizumab's unique profile (pregnancy safety, subcutaneous-only administration) is not captured in efficacy rankings.
3. **Early biologic introduction (R7).** The "conditional, low certainty" rating reflects the genuine uncertainty. Some clinicians will argue this leads to overtreatment; others will argue it doesn't go far enough (should be a "strong" recommendation).

### Key Take-Home Messages
1. **Infliximab and adalimumab remain first-line biologics** — the network meta-analysis supports their position at the top of the efficacy ranking for biologic-naive patients.
2. **Ustekinumab is the preferred agent after anti-TNF failure** — strong recommendation for primary nonresponse; strong recommendation (alongside adalimumab) for secondary nonresponse.
3. **Natalizumab should not be used routinely** — the risk-benefit calculus has shifted with safer alternatives available. This is the single biggest change from ACG 2018.
4. **Combination therapy is nuanced** — infliximab + thiopurine is suggested; adalimumab + thiopurine has very weak evidence; no data for vedolizumab/ustekinumab combinations.
5. **Start biologics early** — the step-up paradigm risks disease progression. Conditional recommendation but philosophically the most important shift.
6. **Thiopurines have no role in induction** — and biologic monotherapy is superior. For maintenance, thiopurines and SC/IM methotrexate are options, but oral methotrexate should not be used.
7. **Infliximab is the best-evidenced agent for perianal fistulas** — and adding ciprofloxacin substantially improves fistula closure rates.
8. **5-ASA and corticosteroids have no maintenance role** — and in moderate-severe CD, 5-ASA should not be used at all.
9. **Two critical knowledge gaps acknowledged:** (a) no evidence to guide de-escalation of combination therapy, and (b) no evidence on combination therapy with non-anti-TNF biologics.
