# ECCO Guidelines on Therapeutics in Ulcerative Colitis: Medical Treatment (Raine et al., 2022)

The most current international guideline on medical treatment of UC, authored by a 33-expert panel spanning 20+ countries with six patient representatives as full voting members. Published in the Journal of Crohn's and Colitis in 2022 (online October 2021), this guideline replaces the prior ECCO UC guidelines and is the first major UC guideline to incorporate ustekinumab, the first to issue a head-to-head recommendation (vedolizumab over adalimumab from the VARSITY trial), and the first to address the emerging JAK inhibitor safety signals from the ORAL Surveillance trial in rheumatoid arthritis. The guideline uses GRADE methodology across 21 recommendations derived from PICO questions, with meta-analyses conducted de novo by the panel's methodologists. A companion guideline covers surgical treatment and hospitalized severe UC — this document focuses exclusively on outpatient medical management. Key European distinctives: vedolizumab positioned before anti-TNF based on VARSITY, stronger emphasis on topical 5-ASA, detailed tofacitinib safety discussion reflecting EMA regulatory actions, and explicit acknowledgment that therapeutic drug monitoring lacks prospective evidence for a formal recommendation.

## What the Guideline Does NOT Cover

### Acute Severe UC and Surgery

The ECCO medical treatment guideline explicitly defers ASUC management and surgical treatment to a companion guideline: "ECCO Guidelines on Therapeutics in Ulcerative Colitis: Surgical Treatment." This is a fundamental structural difference from ACG 2019, which devotes an extensive section to ASUC (R37-R46, 10 graded recommendations). The medical guideline makes no recommendations on:

- IV corticosteroid dosing for hospitalized patients
- The 3-5 day steroid window and Oxford criteria
- Rescue therapy with infliximab vs cyclosporine
- VTE prophylaxis in hospitalized UC
- CMV evaluation in steroid-refractory disease
- Surgical timing and staged IPAA
- Pouchitis management

**Comparison:** The ACG 2019 guideline's ASUC section is among its strongest — the detailed protocol for the hospitalized patient (IV methylprednisolone 60 mg/d, Oxford index at day 3, surgical consultation for nonresponders, CYSIF/CONSTRUCT-level evidence for infliximab vs cyclosporine) is a model of actionable guidance. ECCO's separation into two documents means clinicians treating hospitalized patients must consult a separate guideline. For a comprehensive UC reference, this fragmentation is a disadvantage. However, the surgical companion guideline may provide more detailed operative guidance than ACG's brief surgical considerations section.

**Cyclosporine note:** ACG 2019 devotes substantial evidence review to cyclosporine (CYSIF trial, CONSTRUCT trial, ENEIDA registry), reflecting its continued use in expert centers. The ECCO medical guideline's silence on cyclosporine reflects the PICO-driven structure — if the panel didn't formulate a cyclosporine PICO question for outpatient management, it doesn't appear. This is a limitation for centers that use cyclosporine or tacrolimus as bridge therapy.

**Overall GRADE assessment:** Of 21 recommendations, 15 are strong (71%) and 6 are weak (29%) — notably, ECCO uses "weak" where ACG uses "conditional." Only ONE recommendation has "high" quality evidence (R16: anti-TNF maintenance). Five have "moderate" quality, five "low," seven "very low," and one "low" for R19. The preponderance of strong recommendations with low/very-low evidence reflects GRADE's allowance for upgrading recommendation strength based on clinical experience, safety, cost, and patient values — a pattern also seen in ACG 2019 but more pronounced here due to stricter evidence quality ratings.

**Strongest sections:**
1. **Advanced therapy evidence syntheses (R12-R15, R18-R21):** The de novo meta-analyses for each agent are the guideline's core contribution. The systematic approach to anti-TNF (9 RCTs pooled), vedolizumab (2 RCTs + VARSITY), tofacitinib (2 RCTs + detailed safety), and ustekinumab (single RCT, well-characterized) provides clinicians with a comprehensive, up-to-date evidence map.
2. **Tofacitinib safety discussion:** The most thorough treatment of JAK inhibitor safety in any UC guideline, incorporating ORAL Surveillance, EMA regulatory actions, RIVETING de-escalation data, and practical risk stratification.
3. **VARSITY-based vedolizumab-over-adalimumab recommendation (R19):** Methodologically honest (weak, low evidence), clinically provocative, and uniquely European.

**Weakest sections:**
1. **Absence of ASUC management:** The decision to publish a separate surgical/hospitalized guideline fragments UC care. A clinician managing a hospitalized patient needs both documents.
2. **No therapeutic drug monitoring recommendation (R17):** GRADE purity at the expense of clinical utility. Most specialists will continue using reactive TDM regardless.
3. **No sequencing algorithm:** Like ACG 2019, the guideline makes strong recommendations for individual agents but offers no framework for choosing among them. The VARSITY-based R19 is the closest to sequencing guidance, but it only addresses one pairwise comparison.
4. **Limited discussion of treatment withdrawal:** Acknowledged as "beyond the scope of this current guideline" and deferred to a separate ECCO Topical Review (Doherty 2018). For a document on therapeutics, the absence of de-escalation guidance is a significant gap.
5. **No chemotherapy or CRC surveillance guidance:** Unlike ACG 2019 which covers chemoprevention and surveillance colonoscopy, ECCO defers these to separate guidelines.

**Cross-reference priorities:**
- **G2 (ACG UC 2019 - Rubin):** The most important comparator. Key differences: (a) ECCO includes ustekinumab (ACG predates it); (b) ECCO makes no TDM recommendation (ACG conditionally recommends reactive TDM); (c) ECCO gives a weak recommendation for vedolizumab over adalimumab (ACG makes no head-to-head recommendation); (d) ECCO covers ASUC and surgery in a separate guideline (ACG integrates them); (e) ECCO has stricter evidence quality ratings — ACG rates 5-ASA induction and anti-TNF induction as "high" while ECCO rates both lower; (f) ECCO uses higher 5-ASA dosing (≥2 g/d as minimum vs ACG's 2-2.4 g/d "sufficient"); (g) ECCO's tofacitinib safety discussion is far more detailed, reflecting the later publication date.
- **G5 (AGA UC 2020 - Feuerstein):** AGA's conditional recommendations and explicit sequencing (infliximab/vedolizumab before tofacitinib) contrast with ECCO's strong recommendations and more nuanced safety-based approach. AGA covers 5-ASA dosing, rectal therapy, and monitoring in more granular detail for mild-moderate UC. ECCO's VARSITY-based recommendation and ustekinumab inclusion make it the most current guideline.
- **ECCO Surgical Treatment companion guideline:** Essential reading for the ASUC and surgical content that this medical guideline omits. Together they form the complete ECCO UC treatment framework.

**Most controversial claims:**
1. **Vedolizumab over adalimumab (R19):** Based on a single trial without dose escalation. The corticosteroid-free remission paradox weakens the case. Does NOT mean vedolizumab over infliximab.
2. **No TDM recommendation (R17):** Will be ignored by most specialists but could be weaponized by payers. The intellectually honest position may be clinically counterproductive.
3. **Strong recommendation for tofacitinib (R14) despite detailed safety concerns:** The panel's balancing act — strong efficacy + real safety risks = strong recommendation with caveats — is logically coherent but creates cognitive dissonance for clinicians weighing the risks.

**Historical significance:** This guideline captures a pivotal moment in UC therapeutics: the post-VARSITY, post-UNIFI, post-ORAL Surveillance landscape. It is simultaneously the most current UC guideline (incorporating ustekinumab and the 2021 safety data), the most methodologically rigorous (strict GRADE application, including the refusal to recommend TDM without prospective data), and the most European in its clinical philosophy (vedolizumab-first, higher 5-ASA dosing, comfort with thiopurine monotherapy). The companion surgical guideline structure acknowledges the multidisciplinary nature of UC care but fragments the reference. The next ECCO update will likely need to address the expanding small molecule pipeline (ozanimod, upadacitinib) and the growing evidence for histologic healing as a formal treatment target.
