# Steinhart -- Ch7: Drug Therapy for Managing IBD

Steinhart, Medical Director of the Mount Sinai Hospital (Toronto) IBD Centre, presents a comprehensive patient-facing overview of every drug class used in IBD. This is the bridge between the clinical canon and the lived experience: pharmacology is explained accessibly, side effects are given honest treatment, and the treatment philosophy (step-up vs top-down, treat-to-target, mucosal healing) is laid out clearly. The chapter covers 5-ASA preparations, glucocorticoids, antibiotics, immunosuppressants (thiopurines, methotrexate, cyclosporine), JAK inhibitors, anti-TNF biologics, anti-integrin (vedolizumab), anti-IL-12/23 (ustekinumab), biosimilars, and emerging microbiome-directed therapies.

## Ch7 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1: Mucosal healing as treatment goal | HIGH | Already validated; risk is overtreatment of mild disease |
| 2: Top-down therapy for Crohn's | MEDIUM-HIGH | Long-term biologic safety data still maturing; risk stratification imprecise |
| 3: 5-ASA ineffective in Crohn's | HIGH | Some patients with colonic Crohn's may benefit; post-surgical recurrence role is weak |
| 4: Steroids effective but toxic | HIGH | Budesonide positioning may shift as more biologics become first-line |
| 5: Thiopurines for maintenance | HIGH | May be displaced by biosimilars; HSTCL risk in young males |
| 6: Anti-TNF as most effective class | HIGH | Newer biologics (ustekinumab, risankizumab) challenging first-line positioning |
| 7: Vedolizumab gut-selective safety | HIGH | Efficacy in Crohn's is modest compared to anti-TNF |
| 8: JAK inhibitors oral advantage | HIGH | Safety concerns (shingles, cardiovascular) may limit use |
| 9: Biosimilars clinically equivalent | HIGH | No-claim switching anxiety is psychological, not pharmacological |
| 10: Probiotics/FMT unproven | HIGH | FMT for UC remains investigational; donor selection may be key |

**Strongest section:** The steroid section is the most detailed and clinically useful for patients, distinguishing common/reversible from uncommon/irreversible side effects with unusual honesty. The biologic sections are practical, addressing administration route, antibody formation, and safety in accessible terms.

**Weakest section:** The CAM section is relatively superficial for a patient-facing book. Given that "up to half of IBD sufferers have tried CAM," a more detailed evidence table for specific CAM therapies (aloe, curcumin, Boswellia, cannabis) would have been valuable. The cannabis section correctly notes that marijuana helps symptoms but not inflammation, but doesn't address CBD or the cannabinoid-derivative trials in sufficient detail.

**Overall quality:** This is an excellent patient-facing drug therapy overview. Steinhart writes with clinical authority and genuine respect for the patient's decision-making role. The case studies (Jonathan, Naveen, Rebecca) ground the pharmacology in real clinical decisions. The treatment philosophy sections (mucosal healing, step-up vs top-down, personalized medicine) give patients the conceptual framework to understand why specific drugs are recommended, not just what they do.
