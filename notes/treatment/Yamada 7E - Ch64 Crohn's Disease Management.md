# Yamada 7E -- Ch64: Crohn's Disease: Clinical Manifestations and Management

The authoritative textbook chapter on Crohn's disease management from Yamada's Textbook of Gastroenterology, 7th Edition (2022). Authored by Ha, McGovern, and Melmed at Cedars-Sinai's F. Widjaja Foundation Inflammatory Bowel and Immunobiology Research Institute. This chapter represents the clinical canon: comprehensive, heavily referenced, and organized around evidence-based treatment algorithms. The treatment section covers 5-ASA, antibiotics, corticosteroids, thiopurines, methotrexate, anti-TNF biologics, vedolizumab, ustekinumab, therapeutic drug monitoring, postoperative prevention, and special populations.

## Ch64 Overall Assessment

| Claim | Confidence | Most Vulnerable To |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1: Treat-to-target with objective markers | HIGH | Implementation barriers (cost, patient adherence, access to monitoring) |
| 2: 5-ASA ineffective for CD | HIGH | Per-protocol analyses show modest benefit; some colonic CD may respond |
| 3: Steroids induce remission not healing | HIGH | Budesonide positioning may shift with newer therapies |
| 4: Combination anti-TNF + thiopurine superior | HIGH (SONIC) | HSTCL risk in young males limits universal application |
| 5: Vedolizumab/ustekinumab safety advantages | HIGH | Efficacy in anti-TNF-experienced is lower for vedolizumab |
| 6: TDM cost-effective | HIGH | Thresholds are assay-dependent; vedolizumab/ustekinumab TDM less established |
| 7: Post-op risk-stratified prophylaxis | HIGH | Optimal strategy for moderate-risk patients remains debated |
| 8: Pyramid model obsolete | HIGH | Early biologic use may overtreat indolent disease; risk stratification is imprecise |

**Strongest section:** The treatment algorithm (Figure 64.15) and Table 64.4 (special considerations) together provide a clinically actionable framework. The evidence synthesis for each drug class is methodical, with systematic review data, number-needed-to-treat/harm where available, and explicit acknowledgment of evidence gaps.

**Weakest section:** The nutrition/diet section is notably thin for a chapter of this scope. EEN is mentioned for pediatrics but adult dietary strategies receive minimal attention. Given the patient interest in diet as therapy and the emerging evidence base (CDED, Mediterranean diet), this gap is significant.

**Overall quality:** This is the definitive reference chapter for CD management. The authors at Cedars-Sinai bring both research depth and clinical pragmatism. The treatment algorithm is evidence-based, the drug class discussions include both trial data and practical monitoring guidance, and the special populations section addresses the most common clinical scenarios (abscess, TB, pregnancy, elderly). The chapter's conceptual framework -- treat-to-target, early effective therapy, risk-stratified management -- reflects the 2022 state of the art.
