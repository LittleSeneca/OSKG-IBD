# SIBO-Diagnostic-Standard: What Is the Validated Diagnostic Standard for SIBO?

**Question:** Given that small bowel aspirate culture (the reference standard) has only ~65% agreement with breath testing, and that normal small intestinal microbiota remain poorly characterized, what is the validated diagnostic standard for SIBO -- and should it guide treatment decisions?

**Source:** Phase 4 contradiction analysis: [[SIBO-Diagnostic-Debate]], documenting ACG vs. AGA vs. Rome disagreement on breath test validity.

**Research Gap Description:**
The ACG SIBO 2020 guideline recommends breath testing for SIBO diagnosis (conditional, very low evidence). The AGA SIBO 2020 update emphasizes definitional imprecision. The Rome 2017 report identified breath tests as unvalidated. The North American Consensus (Rezaie 2017) provided standardization, and 16S rRNA sequencing from Leite et al. (2019) independently validated the 10^3 CFU/mL aspirate threshold. But no prospective study has compared breath-test-guided treatment against empiric treatment for SIBO outcomes. The key number in the literature is the ~65% agreement between aspirate and breath test -- if the gold standard and the clinical test agree only 2/3 of the time, we have a measurement problem, not just a clinical one.

**What Would Resolve It:**
1. A prospective study comparing breath-test-guided antibiotic therapy vs. empiric antibiotic therapy for suspected SIBO, with symptom scores and breath test normalization as dual endpoints
2. Large-scale 16S rRNA sequencing validation of the 10^3 CFU/mL aspirate threshold (extending Leite 2019 to multi-center cohorts)
3. Development and validation of a noninvasive SIBO diagnostic algorithm combining breath test patterns, symptom profiles, and risk factors (prior abdominal surgery, PPI use, motility disorders)

**Why It Matters:**
Millions of patients receive SIBO diagnoses and antibiotic treatment based on a test that leading gastroenterology societies acknowledge is unvalidated. Either the test must be validated, or the field must develop an alternative diagnostic framework. Continuing to recommend an unvalidated test with a conditional, very-low-evidence recommendation is clinically unsustainable.
