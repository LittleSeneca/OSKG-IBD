# Pathogenesis-Treatment-Gap: Can Pathophysiological Mechanisms Guide Treatment Selection?

**Question:** Can the identified pathophysiological mechanisms in IBD (NOD2/ATG16L1 genetics, autophagy defects, innate immune dysregulation, epithelial barrier dysfunction) guide treatment selection, or does the current treatment paradigm remain empiric?

**Source:** Phase 4 structural gap analysis: `topic/pathogenesis` (10 claims, 0.0 avg edges) and `topic/genetics` (10 claims, 0.3 avg edges) are almost entirely disconnected from the treatment guideline spine.

**Research Gap Description:**
The graph contains substantial claims on IBD pathogenesis (NOD2 mutations, ATG16L1 autophagy variants, IL-23/Th17 pathway, epithelial barrier dysfunction) and treatment recommendations (anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23, JAK inhibitors). But no edge connects a specific genetic variant to a specific biologic choice. Treatment selection remains driven by disease severity, location, and behavior -- not by molecular mechanism. The graph accurately reflects clinical reality: precision medicine has not arrived in IBD.

**What Would Resolve It:**
1. A prospective trial of genotype-guided biologic selection (e.g., NOD2 variants predicting anti-TNF response)
2. A study correlating autophagy pathway defects (ATG16L1, IRGM) with response to specific biologic classes (anti-TNF vs. anti-integrin vs. anti-IL-23)
3. Mechanistic studies examining whether barrier dysfunction (claudin-2, occludin) predicts differential response to therapies that primarily target epithelial vs. immune pathways

**Why It Matters:**
Current IBD treatment is trial-and-error: start with anti-TNF, if it fails try anti-integrin, if that fails try anti-IL-12/23 or JAK inhibitor. If pathophysiological mechanisms could guide first-line biologic selection, it would spare patients months of ineffective therapy and cumulative bowel damage. The gap between mechanism and treatment is the central unsolved problem in IBD therapeutics.
