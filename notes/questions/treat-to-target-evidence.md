# Treat-to-Target-Evidence: What Level of Evidence Supports Treat-to-Target?

**Question:** What is the evidence justifying treat-to-target (objective, endoscopic/imaging-based targets) over traditional symptom-based management in IBD, and at what level of certainty?

**Source:** Phase 4 evidence brief [[EB-Treat-to-Target]]

**Status:** Open. The paradigm is consensus-driven, not RCT-backed.

**Research Gap Description:**
All major guidelines endorse treat-to-target, and the symptom-inflammation disconnect is settled science. But no guideline claims that treat-to-target is supported by high-quality evidence from randomized trials comparing treat-to-target against symptom-based care. The paradigm is built on observational data and biological rationale -- a consensus judgment that the precautionary principle (prevent silent bowel damage) outweighs the absence of definitive trials.

**What Would Resolve It:**
1. A randomized trial of treat-to-target (endoscopic monitoring, escalation on objective findings) vs. symptom-based management (escalation on patient-reported outcomes) in biologic-naive IBD patients, with primary endpoints of bowel resection, hospitalization, and quality-adjusted life years
2. A comparative study of histologic vs. mucosal healing as a treatment target, with clinical relapse as the primary outcome
3. A study establishing whether transmural healing (MRE/IUS criteria) in CD provides incremental prognostic value over mucosal healing alone

**Why It Matters:**
Treat-to-target drives treatment escalation decisions for millions of patients. If the paradigm is correct, it prevents cumulative bowel damage. If it is overly aggressive, it exposes patients to unnecessary biologic therapy (with infection and malignancy risks) for endoscopic findings that would never have progressed to clinical disease. The stakes justify a definitive trial despite the ethical concerns about randomizing patients to "symptom-only" care.
