# Diet-Guideline-Gap: Can Dietary Therapy Be Positioned Within Guideline Treatment Algorithms?

**Question:** Can dietary therapy (SCD, low-FODMAP, LFE) be positioned within IBD treatment guideline algorithms alongside pharmacotherapy, or do the different evidence standards for dietary vs. pharmaceutical interventions preclude formal integration?

**Source:** Phase 4 structural gap analysis: the dietary domain (77 claims) is almost entirely disconnected from the treatment guideline domain. Zero diet-to-guideline edges exist. This is the largest structural hole in the graph.

**Research Gap Description:**
The ACG SIBO 2020 guideline [[claim-graded-discussed-adjunctive-therapy-without-formal-recommendation|acg-sibo2020-8]] discusses dietary therapy for SIBO as adjunctive, without formal GRADE recommendation. No major IBD guideline integrates dietary therapy into treatment algorithms at a comparable level to pharmacotherapy. The evidence standards are different: drugs require RCTs for approval; dietary protocols rely on mechanistic rationale, case series, and clinical tradition. The problem is not that dietary evidence is absent (Gottschall cites 600+ publications; low-FODMAP has multiple IBS RCTs) but that it is not structured in the GRADE framework that guidelines require.

**What Would Resolve It:**
1. An RCT of SCD vs. standard care (no dietary intervention) in mild-moderate IBD, with endoscopic endpoints, sufficient to earn a GRADE recommendation in the next ECCO or AGA guideline update
2. A consensus methodology statement from ACG/AGA/ECCO on how dietary interventions should be evaluated within guideline frameworks -- specifically, whether mechanistic plausibility and clinical tradition can supplement or substitute for RCT data in conditions where dietary RCTs are logistically difficult
3. Integration of dietary therapy into the STRIDE treat-to-target framework: can dietary therapy achieve endoscopic healing as an intermediate endpoint, and if so, should it be positioned alongside 5-ASA as first-line therapy?

**Why It Matters:**
Dietary therapy is the only treatment modality for SIBO/IBD that patients can self-administer indefinitely, without prescription cost, and without immunosuppression or antibiotic resistance risk. If it is effective, it belongs in the guideline algorithms. If it is not, patients and clinicians should know that. The current state -- guidelines ignore diet, patients pursue it anyway, clinicians are uninformed -- is the worst of all worlds.
