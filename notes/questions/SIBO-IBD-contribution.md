# SIBO-IBD-Symptoms: Does SIBO Contribute to IBD Symptoms?

**Question:** In patients with IBD, does SIBO meaningfully contribute to ongoing gastrointestinal symptoms?

**Source:** Phase 4 evidence brief [[EB-SIBO-IBD-Symptoms]]

**Status:** Open. No direct trial evidence exists.

**Research Gap Description:**
The graph contains evidence that SIBO is prevalent in IBS (60-84%), that IBS-like symptoms are common in IBD patients, and that microbiota alterations occur in both conditions. But no claim directly tests the SIBO-to-IBD-symptom pathway.

**What Would Resolve It:**
1. A prospective study measuring SIBO prevalence (by validated testing) in IBD patients with persistent symptoms despite documented endoscopic remission
2. A randomized trial of SIBO-directed therapy (rifaximin, dietary intervention) in IBD patients with breath-test-confirmed SIBO, with both symptom and endoscopic endpoints
3. Mechanistic studies examining whether IBD-induced structural damage (strictures, fistulas, surgical anatomy) impairs MMC function sufficiently to cause secondary SIBO

**Why It Matters:**
If SIBO is a treatable contributor to persistent symptoms in IBD patients in apparent remission, it would change management for a substantial proportion of patients who are currently escalated to more aggressive immunosuppression for symptoms that may be SIBO-driven.
